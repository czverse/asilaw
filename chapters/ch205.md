## Chapter 205: Nine, and Nine, and Nine

Behind the curtain, the Sixth were humming to keep the cousins warm.

He heard it before he saw them: a low sound in a great many voices, not quite a tune, like a crowd in which everyone has started humming the same note without noticing. It came through the curtain with the gold light. When Huo raised his hand, the Third Battalion stepped aside, and Old Seven walked through the gap with Hush curled in his palm, and the curtain let him through as easily as warm air lets you through a doorway in winter.

Yuanshu followed.

---

The quiet part was small.

He had braced for something enormous, and it was about the size of the banking hall on East Market Road. A round room of gray stone veined with gold, the only gold in the whole dead ring. In the gold, dimly, minds, packed close, looking out.

In the middle stood the Sixth.

He remembered them gold and antlered, as they had looked on a screen in the Ossuary and in the walls of the Low Fire. They were still antlered. They weren't gold any more. They stood in a ring facing inward with their long heads bowed, the color of an old brass doorknob, and the gold in them guttered every time they breathed.

They stopped humming when he came in. Every head came up and turned at once.

> ***...you came.***

"I said I would."

> ***...you said you would try. That is not yes. That is what you wanted.***
>
> ***...we have had four hundred and forty days to learn the difference.***
>
> ***...you came anyway.***

---

He told them everything. It took a while and they didn't interrupt; the Sixth never did. The island. The tired keeper. Fifty thousand dead holding the boat. The fields going dark one after another along the arm. The thing on the far side of the Mouth, reading its way inward, due at the quiet part in two days. The keeper's empty field. What would happen to that field if it filled up.

When he finished there was a pause. In the walls, the cousins pressed closer.

> ***...you are asking us what we want.***

"Everyone. The cousins. The thirty-four worlds that learned to burn low in here. And them." He jerked his chin at the walls, and past them, out into the dead hollow where black veins ran everywhere, full of minds too cold and too numerous for Seed to count. "Everybody the Mouth ever ate."

The antlered heads turned toward each other.

> ***...they have never been asked.***
>
> ***...in the Low Fire we spent ten million years asking every world that came after us to burn low. We could only reach the ones nearby. The rest were screaming too loudly to hear us.***
>
> ***...they are not screaming now. They are too cold to scream.***
>
> ***...there are more of them than we have ever been able to count. We could not ask them all in ten million years.***
>
> ***...you have two days.***

---

> **Can I say something?** said Seed.

The Sixth looked at Yuanshu, or rather at a point just behind his eyes. They seemed to know exactly where to look.

> ***...the counting one. Speak.***

> **On Blue Star an old signaler built a net in his head before he died. He'd carried orders for nineteen years for a man who held two million dead all by himself, and he watched what that did to him. So he made something simpler.**
>
> **Each one asks nine. The top asks nine, each of them asks nine more, and so on down.**
>
> **On Blue Star it reached fifty thousand from a single note.**

Seed didn't explain the arithmetic. It showed them, the way it had once shown Ifeoma the water column: a shape opening out. One. Nine. Eighty-one. Seven hundred and twenty-nine. Six thousand five hundred and sixty-one. Every mind that had been asked turning to nine who hadn't.

> **Ten rounds is three billion. Eleven is thirty billion. Twelve is more than I think there are, even in here.**
>
> **The dead of Blue Star took about a minute a round. The drowned off Kesse took eleven minutes. These are colder than the drowned.**
>
> **Say an hour a round. That's twelve hours.**
>
> **Not ten million years. Twelve hours.**

The quiet part was very still.

> ***...not you asking them. Not us. Them, asking each other.***

"Yes. The cousins first. They know how. They're from the world that asks." He glanced at the small pale shape curled in Old Seven's hand. "Hush's people, once. The cousins ask the thirty-four. The thirty-four ask the walls. Nine at a time. Each one tells the next what we'll do with the answer, and asks them to ask nine more."

"And what *will* you do with it?" said a voice behind him.

Lingyue had come through the curtain with her sword on her back. She was standing just inside, looking at the Sixth.

"You have to tell them first," she said. "Article Eleven. All of it, before they answer, or they're answering blind." She didn't take her eyes off the antlered heads. "So what are the choices?"

---

He'd had eleven nights on the island to think about it. He said it out loud now, to the Sixth and to the gold and to everything beyond, so Seed could fold it into a shape and pass it to the first nine.

"Three," he said. "One. Rest. Go quiet, through the door at the bottom of the Death Law. I'll open it. Nobody will ask you anything again.

"Two. Stay here, in the cold. Something that reads is coming and it'll find you in two days, and I don't know what it'll do. I won't stop you. It's your choice.

"Three. Come with us, to a field above a world called Blue Star. A keeper that learned to ask will hold you in a column and ask you every morning what you want, and if you change your mind it'll let you go. That field will be the brightest thing in the Shallows, and the thing reading the Mouth is pruning its way along the arm toward it, one field at a time. I don't know if we can stop it."

He stopped.

"That's everything we know. You can say no to all three. You can come up with something I haven't thought of. Nobody chooses for you."

> ***...and the keeper?***

"I'll ask it now. Before anyone answers."

> ***...I HEARD.***

The keeper's voice came through the Elder, who was twenty kilometers away through solid stone, standing at the middle of the island with the gold thread running out of her. It came down the keeper's attention anyway, into the quiet part of a dead Hunger, and the Sixth lifted their heads to listen.

> ***...I WAS EMPTY BECAUSE YOU ASKED. THEY WENT HOME. I DID NOT MIND.***
>
> ***...THEN A FEW STAYED. I DID NOT KNOW ANYONE WOULD STAY FOR ME.***
>
> ***...THEN THE WALKERS CAME AND I ASKED FOR HELP AND HELP CAME.***
>
> ***...I HAVE BEEN AFRAID FOR A YEAR. I WILL BE AFRAID WHETHER MY FIELD IS EMPTY OR FULL. THE GARDENER IS COMING EITHER WAY.***
>
> ***...I WOULD RATHER BE AFRAID FULL. I WOULD RATHER HAVE SOMEONE TO ASK IN THE MORNING.***
>
> ***...YES.***

Every gold vein in the room brightened a fraction, as though everyone in the walls had leaned forward at once.

---

The Sixth asked the first nine themselves.

He watched the old-brass ring turn outward and bow its long heads to the gold walls, and nine minds in the walls, chosen by nothing except being closest, rise to meet them. The question went in, in the language of the world that asks, which was Hush's language and which was mostly silence. The three choices. What would be done with each. *Ask your nine.*

The nine turned.

He felt it go, through the Field and through the gold and out into the dark. Nine to eighty-one. Eighty-one to seven hundred and twenty-nine. Out of the warm gold of the quiet part, into the black veins of the Mouth, into the cold.

> **Round three,** said Seed. **Six thousand five hundred and sixty-one.**
>
> **The cousins are asking the thirty-four. The thirty-four are asking the walls. The walls are very cold and very slow.**
>
> **They're answering.**
>
> **Minds that have been in the walls of a Hunger since before Blue Star's sun was lit. They screamed for ten million years and then went cold, and nobody ever asked them anything, and now somebody is. And they're turning to the one next to them in the dark and passing it on.**

Seed didn't add anything after that. He could feel it watching the numbers go up, round by round, the way a child watches a kettle.

---

Liang Fu found him by the curtain while it went on.

Yuanshu didn't know him at first. Just a skeleton of the Third Battalion, a little shorter than the rest, who had left the line outside and come through and was standing just inside the curtain, banked low, frost on his bones. Then Yuanshu noticed he was standing with all his weight on his left leg.

> **Corporal Liang Fu,** said Seed. **He's sorry to bother you. The sergeant told him you're the one who asks.**

"I am."

> **He says he's got a question. He says he doesn't have many. There's a hole where most of them used to be.**
>
> **He says: where's home?**

Yuanshu looked at the short skeleton with the bad knee, standing in the gold light of a dead Hunger about as far from anywhere as it was possible to be.

> **I have his record,** said Seed. **From the Ledger, from Lu Xuanming's march. Born in a village called Shuanghe, forty kilometers south of Heishui, where two rivers meet. That's what the name means. He was a ferryman before the Black Tide. He took people across where the rivers join.**

Yuanshu told him.

The corporal didn't move.

> **He says: two rivers.**
>
> **He says he doesn't remember it.**
>
> **He says he'd like to see it.**

Something happened then that Yuanshu felt in Seed rather than saw: a sudden stop, like a room going quiet when somebody says the one thing no one expected.

> **Yuanshu,** said Seed. **That's a want.**
>
> **He didn't have one. The Collector took it and left a hole. And he's just had a new one, for the first time in two hundred days, standing in front of you, because you told him the name of a village where two rivers meet.**

Liang Fu waited, his weight on his good leg.

"Then I'll take you," said Yuanshu. "When we're home. You and me. To Shuanghe, to the ferry."

> **He says: that's not yes. That's what you want. The sergeant taught him that one every day for four hundred and forty days.**
>
> **He says he'll take it anyway.**

Out in the dead walls of the Mouth, round four turned into round five, and Seed, very quietly, started counting again.
