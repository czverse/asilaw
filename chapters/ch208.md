## Chapter 208: What the Reader Took

The eyes of the Mouth went out on the second night of the voyage home, at 03:12 by Bai Lu's clock. Everyone on the island was awake for it. Nobody had arranged that; nobody needed to.

The fifty thousand stood round the rim of the shrinking island facing backward, the way they had faced since the Mouth: toward a great cracked wheel far behind them in the dark, and in it two lights, white and past white, still singing. They had been singing for thirty-one hours.

He was allowed to sit up by then. Bai Lu had permitted it on the second evening, with her hands still on his face and an expression that promised she would put him flat again if he argued. He didn't argue. He sat by the clock with a blanket round his shoulders and the staff across his knees, Lingyue on one side and Hush on the other, curled into a tight pale knot with the queen of hearts held against itself.

At 03:12 the lights dimmed. Not all at once. From one side, the way they had all seen a field go out from one side on the third night out: white, then less white, then gold, then the dull brown of embers at the bottom of a stove at dawn.

Then nothing. A cold stone wheel very far away, with no eyes in it.

The singing stopped.

---

Hush didn't move. It stayed curled on the ground and made no sound at all.

After a while Old Seven left the rim and came across the glowing ground with his spear, crouched beside it, and held out his hand.

Hush didn't climb in.

Old Seven didn't take his hand away. He stayed crouched there by the clock with his palm open and flat, a tall dead soldier with a finger and a thumb, next to a small pale thing that wouldn't look at him. The clock ticked. Dabao, who had been about to say something, caught Mo Shi's look and closed his mouth.

Then one thin limb uncurled from the knot and touched the very edge of the old soldier's palm. Only touched it. And stayed.

> **He says that's enough,** said Seed. **He says it's what he did on the wall in the Black Tide, when the boy next to him lost his whole squad. He held his hand out. The boy didn't take it. He left it there anyway.**
>
> **The boy took it in the morning.**

---

> ***...THEY WENT UP.***

The keeper's voice came through the Elder, who stood in the middle of the island with the gold thread running north out of her. It was very quiet, the way voices go quiet in a hospital corridor.

> ***...THE READER FINISHED READING THEM. THEN IT TOOK THEM UP, THE WAY THE WALKERS TOOK THE OLDEST AND BRIGHTEST.***
>
> ***...THEY DID NOT FIGHT. THEY WERE STILL SINGING WHEN IT TOOK THEM.***
>
> ***...I THINK THEY SAID YES.***

"They said yes to being found," said Yuanshu. His voice came out cracked; he hadn't used it in two days. "Not to being taken."

> ***...I KNOW. I DO NOT THINK THE READER KNOWS THERE IS A DIFFERENCE.***
>
> ***...FOR A LONG TIME I DID NOT KNOW EITHER.***

---

> **I'm going to give you the numbers now,** said Seed, later, when everyone else had lain down again. **You haven't asked. You'll ask in the morning, and I'd rather you had them in the dark, where nobody can see your face.**

*Go on.*

> **Ninety-three parts in a hundred of the walls: through the door in nineteen hours. I can't give you a count. More than have ever lived on Blue Star. More than on every world in the Shallows I know of put together. All asked. All resting.**
>
> **Two hundred and six million cousins and the six parts of the walls that wanted to find things out: up the threads, into the keeper's field. The six parts outnumber the cousins by a lot.**
>
> **The keeper's field isn't empty. It's the brightest field in the Shallows.**
>
> **The Sixth, seven million cousins and one part in a hundred of the walls: burned high, sang, were read, were taken.**
>
> **Sixty soldiers of the Third Battalion, at the curtain.**

*And the door.*

> **Death Law: 39.2 to 44.0 percent.**
>
> **I don't want to give you that number. It's the biggest jump I've ever given you, and you didn't learn it. Nobody learns that much in nineteen hours. You were used, as a door. A door that more minds have gone through than have ever lived isn't the same door afterward.**
>
> **It's wider. It doesn't shut the way it used to. I can feel it under everything, standing a little open, like the back door of a house when someone in the family hasn't come home yet.**
>
> **I don't know what that means. I'm telling you I don't know.**

He lay down on his back and looked at the far lights sliding past and didn't sleep.

---

On the fourth night the keeper spoke to him alone, not through the Elder but straight down the thread, faint and gold and exhausted, into the place behind his eyes.

> ***...I ASKED THEM THIS MORNING. THE COUSINS. THE ONES FROM THE WALLS. ALL OF THEM.***
>
> ***...I DID IT YOUR WAY. NINE AND NINE AND NINE. I ONLY ASKED THE FIRST NINE. THEY ASKED EACH OTHER.***

"What did they say?"

> ***...MOSTLY: WE DO NOT KNOW YET. ASK US AGAIN TOMORROW.***
>
> ***...THEY SAID IT IS THE FIRST MORNING IN TEN MILLION YEARS THAT ANYONE HAS ASKED THEM, AND THEY WOULD LIKE TIME TO THINK.***
>
> ***...I SAID I WOULD ASK AGAIN TOMORROW.***

The thread went quiet for a moment.

> ***...I HAVE SOMEONE TO ASK IN THE MORNING. I HAVE SO MANY.***

"And the reader?"

> ***...IT HAS NOT MOVED. IT IS SITTING AT THE MOUTH, WHERE THE EYES WERE. IT HAS NOT GONE ON ALONG THE ARM. IT HAS NOT GONE BACK.***
>
> ***...IT IS VERY STILL.***

> **Like something that's eaten,** said Seed. **Or something that's seen what it didn't expect, and is thinking about it.**
>
> **I don't know which. I hope it's the second.**

---

He went to find Liang Fu on the sixth night.

The corporal was at the rim where he had been since the Mouth, facing backward with his weight off his bad knee, standing a little apart from the Western Legion. The island was barely two kilometers across now. Every night more of the rim broke off behind them as the keeper dragged them home, and the dead stood shoulder to shoulder because there was no longer room to stand any other way.

Yuanshu stood next to him and looked back. There was nothing to see. The Mouth was too far and too dark. Only the slow slide of the far lights.

> **He says he doesn't remember them,** said Seed. **The Third Battalion. Thirty years dead with them, the raft, the fire, the curtain, and most of it's gone. The Collector took what he wanted, and a lot of what he remembered went with it.**
>
> **He remembers the sergeant saying his name. Every day. Liang Fu, Liang Fu.**
>
> **He remembers the card game. He says the sergeant cheated.**
>
> **He says the sergeant told him he was the only one who knew where he wanted to go. He says he only knows the name of it.**

"Shuanghe," said Yuanshu. "Where the two rivers meet."

> **That's it. He says: when we get there, will you say the sergeant's name? At the ferry? Somebody ought to. Every day, the way he said mine.**

"I'll say it."

> **He says that isn't yes.**

"No," said Yuanshu. "It's what I'm going to do."

The corporal stayed beside him at the rim a while longer, looking back into the dark.

> **He says that's better than yes. He says the sergeant would've liked it.**

---

On the ninth night, Hush talked to Seed.

Not to Yuanshu. To Seed. He only knew because Seed told him in the morning, carefully, the way it told him things it wasn't sure it was allowed to pass on.

> **It asked me what I am.**
>
> **I said: I count things.**
>
> **It said: no. What *are* you.**
>
> **I said I didn't know. I said the Collector told me I was made of what they used to make. That the walkers read me and didn't know what to do with me. That I picked a new parameter for myself before we left: open.**
>
> **It didn't say anything for a while. Then it said that on its world, before the ring came, the children played a game in the crack. Statue. Whoever moved first was it.**
>
> **It said: I have been playing statue for four billion years.**
>
> **It said: the Sixth stopped playing. They moved. They were bright. They were it.**
>
> **It said it would like to stop playing too, and asked me how.**

*What did you tell it?*

> **That I didn't know. That I'd only stopped myself very recently and it was horribly uncomfortable, like standing outside in the rain without a coat.**
>
> **It said: show me.**
>
> **So I stood in the open, behind your eyes, with nothing in front of me, and it watched.**
>
> **Then it uncurled. All the way. On the ground by Bai Lu's clock in the middle of the night, every leg out, every eye open.**
>
> **It said: like this?**
>
> **I said yes.**
>
> **It said: it is very cold.**
>
> **I said: you get used to it.**
>
> **I don't know if that's true. I said it anyway. I think it's what you'd have said.**

Yuanshu looked across at the clock. Hush was lying beside it, stretched out flat on the warm ground with all its legs spread like a starfish and all its eyes open to the dark, and Bai Lu was sitting next to it pretending to read her book and not looking at it in a way that meant she was keeping watch.

"It's what I'd have said," he agreed. "I'd have been lying too."

> **Good. Then we're both liars. That makes me feel better.**
