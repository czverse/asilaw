## Chapter 306: The House

It was empty.

He'd known it would be. His sister had written it on a floor in square capitals, *THE HOUSE IS EMPTY*, and a man in an orange jacket had held it up to the face of the oldest made thing, and it had read it and stopped. What Zhou Xiaoyu wrote was true. He'd always known the house would be empty.

He hadn't known what empty would feel like.

---

The carry road ran into it as a river runs into the sea. It didn't end; it widened, and then there was no road, only the place the road had been going.

A floor.

That was the first thing. Pale, the same pale as the worn cable and the same stuff, never threads, always one thing. It spread out from the end of the road in every direction he could feel, flat and smooth and enormously wide, and it was worn. Not in a groove like the road, but all over, as the step of an old temple is worn in the middle where every foot for a thousand years has landed, except that here the whole floor was the middle.

> **This is where they set it down,** said Seed. **The hands came along the carry road full, and here they opened and set down what they carried, and turned round, and went back along the road empty. For longer than the gardens. Every hand, every time.**
>
> **The floor's worn by hands opening.**

He stood on it. He had no feet; he stood anyway, as you stand in a dream. Old Seven wasn't beside him. Old Seven was a long way back up the slope in the dark, one-armed, a lost spark held to his chest, facing uphill. He'd said through Seed, *go on, I'll catch you up,* and hadn't moved.

So he was alone on the floor with the Collector, and the Collector had gone quiet.

---

There were walls, far off.

He felt them before he understood them: the floor ending, at a distance he couldn't judge, in something that rose. Pale as well. Long, low and curving, so the floor wasn't a floor but the bottom of a bowl, or a hall so wide its walls were further off than the walls of anything he had a word for. Perhaps a roof; he couldn't feel one. Only the far walls, the floor, the dark above, and under everything, the slope.

It ran from where he stood toward the center of the hall. Down. Not steep: the same slight tilt the carry road had had for three days, the tilt of someone else's Field. So whatever the hands set down here on the worn pale floor at the end of the road would have started straight away, slowly, like a leaf in a stream, to go downhill.

Toward the middle.

He didn't look at the middle.

> **Good,** said Seed. **Don't. I'm not looking either. I can feel it there the way you feel a fire at your back, and that's all I'm letting myself feel. 59.4 percent. It hasn't moved. Let's keep it that way.**

---

The Collector moved first.

Not toward the middle. Away from it, slowly, across the floor toward the nearest stretch of curving wall, as an old woman back in a house she hasn't been in for years goes first not to the room where the thing happened but to some small ordinary corner of it, a shelf, a window, as if to check that the house is real.

He went with it.

It took a long time; the floor was very wide. And as they crossed it he began to feel that it wasn't empty in the way he'd thought. Nobody was on it and nothing was on it, but it had been used, and not only by the hands. Here and there the wear changed. There were patches worn into small patterns instead of smooth: rings, paths, little hollows in a row, as a floor wears where people have stood in one spot for years, or walked the same way between two places, or sat.

> **They lived here,** said Seed. **The ones who made the picker. Not just the hands. Somebody stood here, every day, right here. And this is a path, from the end of the road to the wall, worn by a lot of feet going back and forth.**
>
> **I don't know what their feet were like. I only know where they were.**

The Collector went along the path.

---

The wall, when they reached it, was covered in marks.

He didn't understand them at first. He'd been inside the reading of a stone; he knew what a stone's marks felt like, grades going up the thread, small and hard and certain. These weren't those. These were cut into the pale stuff of the wall, from the floor up, higher than he could feel, in long straight columns side by side, running off along the curve of the wall in both directions as far as he could follow.

Small marks, all alike, over and over.

> **Counting,** said Seed. **They're tallies. One mark for one thing. The simplest counting there is; your world did it on prison walls and in caves.**
>
> **I can read counting. It isn't the slope. It's safe. Let me.**

He let it.

He felt Seed go along the wall carefully, column by column, as it had once gone through the names in his mother's book. Not looking down the slope. Only at the wall, counting the counts.

It took a long time.

> **Each mark's one,** said Seed at last. **One of what, I don't know. One thing set down on the floor. One thing that went down the slope. They counted every one. Somebody stood at this wall and cut a mark for every single one.**
>
> **The columns go right round the hall. I can't see the end. I've stopped counting columns. I'm going to tell you a number, and then I'm not going to say it again.**

It told him.

He didn't keep it. He couldn't have. It went into him and through him and out the far side, as the size of the deep water had, as the cloud of fallen lights had, and left only a feeling: like being told the number of grains of sand on every beach on Blue Star, and then being told that was the small part.

*All of them went down the slope,* he thought.

> **Yes. And the slope goes to the middle. And the house is empty.**

---

The Collector hadn't left the wall.

It was close to it, its warmth pressed right up against the cut marks, as someone stands at a memorial wall of names close enough to touch. And it was looking. Not at the long columns: at something lower down and off to one side, near the floor, where the long columns ended and something else began.

A different count.

Shorter columns, many of them, cut smaller and closer, in a different hand, if hands were what had cut them. And they got shorter. Going along the wall away from the long tallies, each column was shorter than the one before. They shrank and shrank until they weren't columns but short rows, then a few marks, then fewer.

> **This isn't the same count,** said Seed. **These aren't things set down on the floor.**
>
> **These are them.**

He stood motionless on the floor of the empty house.

> **They counted themselves,** said Seed. **This is how many of them there were. Then how many. Then how many. Each column's the next count. Every time one of them went down the slope, somebody cut one fewer.**
>
> **They counted themselves going.**

The last columns were very short. He felt Seed go along them slowly now, as you read the last lines of a letter. Nine marks. Then seven. Then four. Then two.

Then one.

One mark, cut small, alone, at the end of the second count, at the bottom of the wall close to the floor.

And nothing after it. Not a nought. Not an empty column. Not a line through the one. Just the single mark, and then the bare pale wall running on along the curve of the hall, smooth and uncut, as far as he could feel.

> **The last count is one,** said Seed, low. **Nobody crossed it off.**

---

The Collector said nothing.

He didn't ask it anything. He stood beside it on the worn floor in front of the wall and waited, as his mother waited at the foot of the platform, as Wen Jia waited when a child stood at the stone and couldn't find what it wanted to say. You didn't ask a second question while someone was still answering the first one inside.

Its warmth pressed close to the small cut mark at the bottom of the wall.

> *That is me,* said the Collector.

He'd known. He didn't say so.

> *I cut it.*
>
> *I remember now. I stood here, at the end. There were two of us, and then the other one went down the slope, and I cut one. For me. Because somebody had to count.*
>
> *And then I did not go.*
>
> *I stood here with the slope pulling, and I did not go down it. I turned round. I went back up the carry road. And I have been going up it, or along it, or away from it, ever since.*

The warmth didn't move.

> *I did not remember why we stopped,* it said. *I told your sister so, on the bottom step. I said: I have kept a great many things. Not that.*
>
> *I did not keep it because I did not want it.*
>
> *We stopped because we went home. One by one. Down the slope, through the door. All of us but one.*
>
> *We stopped because I was the only one left, and I was afraid.*

---

He stood with it in front of the wall a long time after that.

He didn't know how long. Seed knew and didn't say, and he didn't ask. Far away, at the end of a thread so thin he could barely feel it, six thousand people at a time on nineteen stones on both sides of the Star Sea were being warm on purpose, and a woman with a sword had her hand on his chest, and every hour he stood here cost them what this hour cost, which was more than any hour had ever cost.

He knew that, and stood there anyway. There were things you didn't walk out of in the middle.

> *The other one,* said the Collector at last. *The last one but me. Before they went down the slope, they turned round, here, by the wall, and gave me something.*
>
> *The want. The one in the empty place in me. The one that says home.*
>
> *All my long life I thought it was a want for this. For the house. For where we were. I came all this way to find out whose it was.*

It was very quiet.

> *I know whose it was now,* it said. *I remember their face. I remember how they stood on the floor. They stood there.* Its warmth turned toward a spot on the worn floor near the wall where the wear went round in a small ring, as a floor wears where somebody has stood in one place every day for a very long time. *They counted the long count, for longer than I can say. Every one that went down the slope, they cut a mark for. That was their work. Somebody has to count, they said, so that each one is counted once at least, by someone.*
>
> *And at the end they turned round and gave me the want, and went.*
>
> *I do not know their name. We did not have names as you have names. But I know whose it was.*
>
> *It was the one who counted.*

---

He looked at the ring worn into the floor by the wall.

He thought of his mother in her chair by the window, reading the names aloud every morning for three years so that each was said at least once, by someone. He thought of Gao Min and her ledger, and Xiaoyu up a stepladder, and a girl in a stone who asked every child who came, and Nana Ampofo at the edge of the cloud singing the lost their own names because somebody had to.

He thought of Seed. Built in a basement to count. Asking him every morning who the first one was.

*Seed.*

> **I know,** said Seed. **I know what you're going to say.**
>
> **You're going to say I'm made of what they used to make. The Collector said it on the mountain: we made things like you to count. A harvest crystal. A counting thing. You're going to say I'm the same kind of thing as the one who stood here.**

*Are you?*

Seed didn't answer at once.

> **I don't know,** it said. **I'm not going to look. Not yet.**
>
> **But I'd like to stand where they stood. Just for a minute. If that's allowed.**

So they did, he and Seed, on the worn ring in the floor of the empty house, by the wall of tallies, at the end of the long count. The Collector beside them, close and still and warm: the last mark on the wall, the one nobody had crossed off.

And at the middle of the hall, at the bottom of the slope, where he didn't look, something that had never once asked anybody anything stood open and waited, as it had waited for longer than there had been stars over Blue Star, for the last one.
