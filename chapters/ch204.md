## Chapter 204: The Cold Fire

They saw it on the eleventh night, and at first nobody could tell what they were looking at, because it gave off no light at all.

It was a place where the stars stopped. A great curved absence ahead of the island, where the scatter of distant fields and worlds simply wasn't, like the black bulk of a mountain at night when you're standing at its foot. It grew as they came on. By the second hour it filled half the view from the leading edge, and Old Seven had to tip his skull right back to see the top.

> **The Mouth,** said Seed. **Two rings fused into one wheel of gray stone, a thousand kilometers across. Cracked all over, like the trunks.**
>
> **On Blue Star it had two coals for eyes. They're out.**

He had seen it once from underneath, filling the whole sky over Lingzhou with two burning eyes, on the night his mother said *Lanterns*. He'd been inside the Low Fire then, staring up through gold-veined walls, and it had been the most frightening thing he had ever seen in his life.

It wasn't frightening now. It was a ruin. A cracked stone wheel hanging dead in the dark, nothing burning in it, nothing pulling at anything, turning very slightly the way an abandoned waterwheel turns in a dry riverbed when the wind catches it.

And on the far side of it, something was reading.

He couldn't see it. His Field reached ten kilometers and the ring was a thousand across. But the keeper's attention ran out ahead of the island and wrapped round the Mouth like fingers round a teacup, and through that he could feel it. Large. Unhurried. Turning pages.

> **A third of the way through,** said Seed. **Reading from the far edge in. It hasn't found the cousins. Hush says they're in the middle, in the quiet part, where the curtain was, burning as low as they know how.**
>
> **It gets to the middle in about two days.**

---

The Elder brought them in against the rim where a crack ran down through the outer stone, wide enough to march through nine abreast. She did it so gently that the island touched the Mouth without a sound, like a ferry nosing into a jetty with an old pilot at the wheel.

"We're here," she said. "The keeper is very tired."

> ***...I AM HERE.***
>
> ***...I DID NOT LOOK AT IT THE WHOLE WAY. I WAS AFRAID IT WOULD LOOK BACK.***
>
> ***...IT IS COLD. IT CANNOT LOOK AT ANYTHING.***
>
> ***...I AM LOOKING AT IT NOW. IT IS VERY SAD.***

---

Old Seven went in first. He didn't wait to be told and nobody would have dared. He stepped off the glowing island onto cracked gray stone with his spear held in one finger and a thumb, and walked into the crack, and the Spear Corps went after him without being asked, because he hadn't asked them and they hadn't asked him and that had been the arrangement for thirty years.

Then the living. Yuanshu with the staff. Lingyue with her sword lit pale. Bai Lu with her bag. The Mo twins, elbows touching. Dabao with his shield up in front of his face, as if the dark might throw something. Qin Hao with his bandaged arms. Kwan with her unnecessary headlamp. Adjutant Xu at the top of the Lattice, humming.

The fifty thousand stayed on the island at the rim. Somebody had to hold the boat.

The crack went on for kilometers, through gray stone scorched and sooted by ten million years of fire, with long smooth channels worn into it where flame had once run like water down a gutter. Dabao touched one of the channels and pulled his hand back fast and then looked embarrassed, because it was stone cold.

Then the walls fell away and they were inside.

---

He had been inside the Low Fire. He remembered gold-veined walls and round glowing windows where mouths used to be, and a few hundred million minds peering out of the veins at the visitors, curious, some of them smiling.

This was a cathedral after a fire.

A hollow so big the light of the Field went out into it and didn't find the other side. Walls above and below and around them, gray, cracked, veined like the Low Fire. But the veins were black: empty channels running everywhere through the stone, dry as a riverbed in August.

And in the veins, very faint, so faint he only saw them because the Field's light touched them in passing: minds.

Billions. Not burning, not screaming, not even burning low. Just there, pressed into the dead walls, small and cold as yesterday's ashes in a grate. Every world the Mouth had ever eaten, and everything the two Hungers it was built from had eaten before it.

> **I can't count them,** said Seed.
>
> **I've counted everything. Every ember, every name. I've never not been able to count.**
>
> **They're too cold and too many. I keep losing my place.**

Lingyue's hand found his in the dark and held on hard enough to hurt.

---

"Which way?"

He didn't ask Seed, or the Elder. He asked Old Seven, who was standing a few meters ahead with his skull tilted, listening, the same way he had once walked through the keeper's field toward a quiet column no one else could find.

Old Seven didn't answer. He turned a few degrees toward the middle of the dark and started walking.

They followed him for four hours.

There was no floor in a ring, only the inner curve of the wall going away in every direction. The Field made a floor, as it had on the keeper's field, and the dead walked on it, and the living walked behind the dead. Past black veins packed with cold embers. Past smooth fire-channels. Past the seam where the two Hungers had fused, where the stone thickened and twisted and the veins tangled together in great dark knots like the roots of an old banyan.

Then, far ahead, a light.

Small. Gold. The only warm thing in the whole hollow, like the one lit window in an apartment block at four in the morning.

> **The quiet part,** said Seed. **Where the curtain was. Where they've been burning low for four hundred and forty days.**

Old Seven walked faster. Dabao, behind him, broke into a jog to keep up and his shield banged against his knee, and nobody laughed, which told Yuanshu more about how everyone was feeling than anything else had.

---

There was a curtain.

He knew it was a curtain the second he saw it, although it wasn't made of anything he had a word for. A long, high fold of faint light hanging from nothing, gray at the edges and gold in the middle where the glow came through. Something very old and very quiet had stood behind it for a long time, and when it left, it had left its shape behind, the way a person leaves a hollow in a mattress.

In front of the curtain, in a line, facing out into the dark, facing them, waiting:

Sixty-one skeletons.

Soldiers of the Third Battalion of Lu Xuanming's march. They had walked off Blue Star on a raft because a boy asked them to, gone into a burning Hunger to stand guard over a world that had said yes, and then stood at the edge of the quiet part for four hundred and forty days so that whatever cold came would reach them first.

Their gray flames were banked so low he could barely see them. Their bones were white with something that looked exactly like frost, although there was no frost in the Sea.

At the front stood a short, broad skeleton with one arm.

The left arm was gone at the shoulder. Not lost. Given, a long time ago in the throat of the Low Fire, bone to bone, so that a captain could hold a spear in two hands again.

Sergeant Huo Changsheng.

Old Seven stopped.

---

They stood three meters apart in the gray light of a dead Hunger: a tall skeleton of two meters ten with a sergeant's left arm on him, holding a spear in a finger and a thumb; and a short broad one with no left arm at all, at the head of sixty-one men.

Neither of them moved.

Then Huo raised his one hand to his brow, and held it there.

Old Seven grounded his spear. The butt struck the stone once, and the sound went out into the dark of the Mouth and didn't come back. Then he raised his free hand, the left one, Huo's, the one with its finger and its thumb, and saluted with it.

> **He says: Sergeant. You held.**
>
> **Huo says: sir. Sixty-one present, sir. One of them doesn't know where he is.**
>
> **Huo says: we were told somebody would come.**

---

Something moved behind the line.

Small and pale, on a great many thin legs. It came out from behind the Third Battalion slowly, swaying, the way it had once walked in through a gap in a fire past a cooling shield. It was smaller than he remembered. Or it was holding itself smaller, all its limbs pulled in tight against the cold and its dark eyes half shut.

It went straight to Old Seven.

The old soldier crouched, as a tall man crouches to talk to a small child, and held out his hand, the one with a finger and a thumb. Hush climbed into it. All of it. It curled up in a dead man's palm with its legs wrapped round itself like a cat that has come in out of the rain.

It held something up in one thin limb so Old Seven could see. A playing card, worn soft at the corners, the red faded almost to pink. A queen of hearts.

> ***...I still have it.***
>
> ***...I said I would.***
>
> ***...tell the captain.***

Old Seven's other hand came up and closed loosely over the small pale shape.

His flame came up with it, all the way, brighter than Yuanshu had ever seen it: gray and warm and steady, lighting the curtain and the sixty-one in front of it and their one-armed sergeant. On the bones of the Third Battalion, the frost that wasn't frost began, slowly, to go.

> **He says: I'm the captain,** said Seed. **He says: I was told.**

Behind Yuanshu, somebody blew their nose very loudly. It was Qin Hao, which nobody ever mentioned to him afterward, not even Dabao.
