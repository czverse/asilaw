## Chapter 295: A Year

> **It's been a year,** said Seed.

It said so on the ninth morning of the voyage home, on the observation deck of the Eighth Candle, while he sat on the floor with his back to the window and Xiaodeng asleep on his chest, because Lingyue had said that if she didn't get four hours' sleep she'd throw the baby, the sword and him into the fold, in that order.

> **More than a year. Four hundred and sixteen days. Since the deep water. Since Nana Ampofo stood at the edge of the cloud and said: we'll stay, we'll keep asking, ask us again in a year.**
>
> **We didn't ask.**
>
> **I'm sorry. I should have said, on Anthos, at the Assembly. I was counting other things. I didn't forget; I want you to know that. I knew the number every morning. I didn't say it.**

He sat very still with his daughter asleep on him.

*Why not?*

> **Because I was afraid of the answer.**
>
> **They're in the deep water. Eleven hundred and forty-six of the drowned of Kesse, asking the ones who fell out of the hands of the one who picks, nine by nine, the ones at the edge, the oldest and slowest. In the cold and the dark, where nothing pushes back.**
>
> **I thought: if we ask them, they might say they want to come home, and I don't know how to bring them home from there. Or they might say they want to stay forever, and I didn't want to hear that.**
>
> **So I didn't ask. That's what the Collector did, for a very long time. It didn't ask the thing it was afraid of the answer to.**
>
> **I'd like to stop doing that.**

---

He asked them that night.

Not from the Candle. The Candle was a rowed listener ship in the middle of a fold between the inner worlds and the edge of the Shallows, with no stone aboard and nothing on it that touched the threads except him. So he asked the way he felt everything now: down into the net and back along it, a very long way, to the Lens.

Ichiro caught it. He said he'd been waiting. He'd been looking out at the deep water through the root ever since the man in the gray coat put the last piece back, and he could see the edge of the cloud from there, small, as you see a fishing fleet's lamps from a headland at night. He could see the Watch. The woman in the coat had looked up once a month ago when he looked at her, as she'd looked up when he rang to her on his way home.

> **He says give it to him and he'll carry it,** said Seed. **He'll ring it in the fog-code, the way he rang it to her the first time. She'll know it's from you.**

So he said it aloud on the observation deck of the Eighth Candle, with his daughter asleep on his chest and his wife asleep in the next room and a one-armed dead captain in the doorway facing in. Through Seed. Through the Lens, a very long way behind them now, in the bow of a ship rowed by two hundred and forty thousand people who'd chosen to be there. Out of the root, along the threads, across the dark between arms, to the edge of a cloud of small dim lights that had fallen out of the hands of the oldest thing anyone had ever made.

*Nana Ampofo. It's me, the boy. It's been a year. More. I'm sorry I'm late.*

*I said I'd ask you again. I'm asking.*

*What do you want?*

---

The answer took four days.

He hadn't expected it to be fast. The deep water was the deep water. Ichiro said that even through the root, even whole, a mark going out to the edge of the cloud and coming back was like ringing a bell across a bay in fog: you rang, and waited, and the fog swallowed the sound, and you didn't know whether anyone had heard until the answer came back out of the white.

On the fourth night it came.

> **Ichiro has it. It's in the fog-code. She learned it from the quiet ones, and from him a little when he went past. She rings it badly. He says it's the first thing he's ever heard rung worse than his boat call, and he's delighted.**
>
> **It says:**

> **WATCH OF THE SHELF.**
>
> **WE HEARD.**
>
> **YOU ARE LATE. WE KNEW YOU WOULD BE. YOU ARE ALWAYS LATE. THAT IS ALL RIGHT.**
>
> **WE HAVE ASKED ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-ONE MILLION.**
>
> **MOST WENT THROUGH THE DOOR. SOME WENT DOWN THEIR THREADS. SOME STAYED. THE ONES WHO STAYED WE SING TO. WE SING THEM THE NAMES. THEIR OWN. WE ASKED THEM WHAT THEY WERE CALLED AND WROTE IT DOWN IN OUR HEADS.**
>
> **THERE ARE STILL MANY AT THE EDGE.**
>
> **YAW WANTS TO COME HOME.**

He sat very still on the floor, with the small weight of his daughter rising and falling on his chest, and read it again.

> **YAW ASKED FIRST. IN THE VOTE ABOUT THE TOP. HE IS TIRED. HE HAS ASKED NINE HUNDRED THOUSAND BY HIMSELF. HE WANTS TO LIE AT THE BOTTOM OF THE SEA OFF KESSE AND HEAR THE BOATS GO OVER.**
>
> **FOUR HUNDRED AND TWO WANT TO COME HOME WITH HIM.**
>
> **SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO WANT TO STAY. AND KEEP ASKING. UNTIL THE EDGE IS EMPTY. THEN THEY WANT TO BE ASKED AGAIN.**
>
> **I AM STAYING.**
>
> **SOMEBODY HAS TO SING THEM THE NAMES.**
>
> **— NANA. ASK ME AGAIN IN A YEAR. ON TIME.**

---

For a while he said nothing at all.

Lingyue had woken. She'd come to the doorway of the next room and was leaning in it, arms folded, hair loose, as she leaned in doorways, and she'd heard. Seed had let her.

"How do they get home?" she said. "Four hundred and three. From the deep water."

> **Ichiro says: the root. Everything comes through the root now. He passed the man in the gray coat down into the stone on Anthos. He can pass the drowned of Kesse.**
>
> **They'll come along the threads to the Lens, and he'll send them down through the net to the stone nearest Kesse. Which is Kesse. The midwife's in it.**

Lingyue was quiet a moment. "And then?"

> **Then they walk into the sea. The midwife says she'll meet them. She held four hundred children at their first breath, and she'd like to hold four hundred and three of the drowned at their last.**

---

They came home on the eleventh night of the voyage.

He didn't see it. He felt it, as he felt everything on the net now, through Seed and the Lens a long way behind and the threads under everything. Four hundred and three cold dim embers the color of deep water, coming in out of the edge of a cloud of fallen lights in the dark between arms, slowly, as the drowned did everything, along the threads into the root. There a fog-ringer caught them one after another and said each of their names, because he'd asked them on the way in, and passed them down.

Down through the net to a stone on a beach at Kesse, on the north coast of the Southern Compact, where a midwife from the bottom of a well had sat in the knot a year and asked every child of Kesse who touched her what they wanted to become.

And she held them.

He felt that too. Seed said it was the first time anything had come down a stone's thread and been held at the bottom instead of read. Four hundred and three of the drowned off Kesse came out of the stone one after another into the night, onto a beach, and walked down it slowly into the sea.

And first out, first into the water: a fisherman who'd gone over the side of a boat in a storm more than forty years ago, who'd answered first the night the boy asked them, who'd asked in a vote about who should be at the top, *why does there have to be a top?*

> **Yaw says tell the boy thank you,** said Seed. **Tell him it was cold out there.**
>
> **Tell him the sea's warmer.**

---

> **Death Law: 58.3 to 59.1 percent,** said Seed.
>
> **I didn't open the door. The door wasn't part of it. They didn't go through; they came home. It went up anyway.**
>
> **I think because the Death Law isn't only the door. It's everything that happens to the dead. And four hundred and three of them came home from the deep water and walked into the sea. Nobody's ever done that.**

He sat on the observation-deck floor with his daughter awake now on his chest, gazing up at him with the cloudy unfocused eyes of a four-month-old.

*Fifty-nine,* he thought.

> **Yes. I need to tell you something. I've known it since Corraine. I didn't want to tell you on Anthos.**
>
> **It isn't going to go to sixty.**

He went still, and the baby on his chest stirred, and settled.

> **Sixty is a Fragment. A piece of a Great Law, complete enough to stand on its own without you holding it up. I don't know who holds one. I've never stood close to one. I only know what the line looks like from this side.**
>
> **You're a Lord. Level seventy-five. Your body's a Lord's body; your soul's a Lord's soul. That's what I'm built on, and I can't comprehend more than the thing I'm built on can hold.**
>
> **I can feel the edge. It's just past fifty-nine. Fifty-nine point nine, perhaps. And then a wall. Not a wall somebody built: the kind a cup has. It only holds so much. Pour in more and it doesn't hold more. It breaks.**

*So it stops.*

> **It stops. Until you're more than a Lord.**
>
> **I'm sorry. I know you didn't do any of it for the number. But I said I'd always tell you the numbers. This is the number. It's the first one I've ever told you that I can't change by running anything.**

---

He sat on the floor and thought about it, and his daughter fell asleep again on his chest.

He didn't mind.

That surprised him. He sat on the floor of the observation deck of a rowed ship crossing the Star Sea and turned it over, as you turn over a stone on a beach to see its underside, and found he didn't mind at all. He'd never wanted the number. He'd wanted the door to be a little open, always, so that anyone who came to it could go through. It was. He'd wanted to be able to ask. He could.

"What do I have to do," he said, "to be more than a Lord?"

> **I don't know. King, probably. A King's a world's worth. Lingyue's grandfather is one, and he's past eighty. I don't know how long he was a Lord. I could ask him.**
>
> **You've been awake three years.**

"Then it'll take a while."

> **Yes. I think that's good.**
>
> **The first time I told you the numbers, in the cemetery at three in the morning when you raised Old Seven, I thought the numbers were the point. I don't think that any more. I think the point's the door, and the asking, and coming home.**
>
> **The number can wait.**

In the doorway, one-armed, facing in, the tall dead captain of the Eastern Line had turned his skull a little toward the floor where the boy sat with his daughter asleep on his chest.

> **He says a soldier with one arm is still a soldier. A Lord at fifty-nine is still the boy who asked him to get up.**
>
> **He says the drowned waited a year. He can wait the rest of his.**

He looked up.

> **He says he told you at the long table, with the box: ask me again in a year. It hasn't been a year. It's been a season. You're not late for him yet.**
>
> **When it's a year, ask him at stone No. 7. Not here. Not on a ship.**
>
> **He says he already knows what he's going to say. And for once he'd like you to have to wait for it.**
