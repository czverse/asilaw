## Chapter 273: Little Lamp

They argued about the name for nine days, and then Xiaoyu settled it by accident.

Not argued, Lingyue said; wrong word. You couldn't argue with someone who agreed with everything you said and then sat on a step for six hours. She said it from the bed in the back room of the Ossuary, where Bai Lu had let her come home on the fourth day on condition that she didn't get up, which she did constantly, and didn't fly, which she hadn't, yet. The baby slept in a drawer. Fat Tan had taken it out of the old ledger cabinet in the vault and lined it with a blanket and set it on two chairs by the window where Hush could see it from the sill. Lingyue had looked at it and said *a drawer*, and Fat Tan had said *it's a very good drawer, it held the Black Tide accounts for thirty years*, and that had been the end of it.

"It's a decision," said Yuanshu. "A name. She can't say yes to it. We'd be deciding for her."

"Everything's a decision," said Lingyue. "Feeding her's a decision. The drawer's a decision. You decided she'd be born."

"We decided."

"We decided. On a slope. At a wedding. I know." She looked at the drawer. "Article Twelve. Decide once. Say out loud nobody said yes. Then owe her the question." She looked back at him. "So we decide, and when she's old enough we ask her if she wants to keep it. And if she says no, she picks another."

"Yes."

"So pick one."

He said nothing. He'd been saying nothing for nine days.

---

His mother had suggestions. She offered one each morning at breakfast, and each morning Lingyue said *no* and his mother said *good, I didn't like that one either*, and wrote it on a list. Su Mingli came on the third day, stood at the foot of the bed with her arms folded, looked into the drawer a long time, and said the Shen family had a tradition of naming daughters after weapons. Lingyue said she was aware; she'd been named after the moon over a sword-pass. Su Mingli said that was what she meant, and left.

Shen Jinghong came on the fifth day, slowly, up the Ossuary steps on his granddaughter's arm because he wouldn't take anyone else's, eighty-five years old, King of Light. He sat in the chair by the drawer for two hours without a word, then said that in his day you waited a month and asked the fortune-teller. Lingyue said they weren't asking a fortune-teller. He said *good, they were always wrong*, and fell asleep in the chair.

Seed suggested nothing.

> **I'd like to say why. I ask her every morning what she wants, and tell her first, and she doesn't answer.**
>
> **I don't want to give her a name I chose. Not because it would be wrong. Because I'd remember it was mine. And I'd like there to be one thing about her I didn't decide.**

---

On the ninth night Xiaoyu came down the stairs at two in the morning after asking the Collector what it wanted. The Collector had said it didn't know yet, and then it had said something new.

She came into the back room without knocking, as she came into every room, and stood by the drawer on its two chairs with the capped marker in her hand, looking down at the baby asleep in Fat Tan's blanket. Lingyue was awake; she was always awake at two now. He was on the floor against the bed with the staff.

"The Collector asked about her," said Xiaoyu.

He sat up.

"Not asked. It doesn't ask any more. It said it wanted to tell first." She didn't look up from the drawer. "It said it felt the hall light up, from the deep water. Everything that looks felt it. Lingyue's lamp going up white when she was born. It said it was the brightest thing in the Shallows for nine seconds." She was quiet. "It said it didn't keep it. It said: tell them I did not keep it. It said: tell them it was the first bright thing I have ever seen that was not a want."

On the bed, Lingyue didn't move.

"And then it said that on the Hollow Bell the Singers have a word for a child who comes out of the song already standing. It learned it from them long ago, when it kept their wants. It means *little lamp*. Because when a child's sung into being it glows a little, the first day, like a lamp somebody's just lit."

She looked down into the drawer.

"And I thought: she did. She glowed. The whole hall. Lingyue held the lamp up nine hours so a thousand people could find the shore, and then she couldn't put it down, and then the baby came and the lamp went out."

She uncapped the marker. Then capped it again, because there was nothing in the room to write on that would ever come off, and she wouldn't write on the drawer.

"Xiaodeng," she said. "Little lamp. Like I'm Xiaoyu. Little rain."

Nobody spoke for a long moment.

Then from the bed, very quietly, in the dark, Lingyue said: "Zhou Xiaodeng."

She said it as you'd try on a coat.

"We decided," said Yuanshu. "Nobody said yes."

"We decided," said his wife, looking at the drawer. "And when she's old enough, we ask her."

> **I didn't choose it,** said Seed. **Xiaoyu chose it. And the Collector. And the Singers, a long time ago. And Lingyue, by holding up a lamp.**
>
> **I'd like to say it to her in the morning, if that's allowed. I'm asking first.**

"It's allowed," said Lingyue.

---

On the tenth day the Cantor of the Hollow Bell asked to see her.

She hadn't gone back up to the shell. She'd come down the keeper's road on the morning of the birth and stood at the foot of the platform with his mother's hand on her foot, and when it was over she hadn't gone back. She'd walked out of the Awakening Hall into the sun on East Ring Road, four meters tall, milk-glass, the light inside her showing faintly like a moon behind cloud, and stood there. Then she'd walked slowly through the streets of the old quarter, the city getting out of her way, to the courtyard behind the Ossuary, and stood by the dry fountain next to Ninth, and stayed.

She didn't sing. She had no note. She stood in the courtyard in the winter sun and listened: to Ninth singing hers, to Hush on the sill not singing anything, to the city.

On the tenth day, she asked.

> **She asks to see the child who came out not standing. She won't touch her. She knows she's very large and very old. She'd only like to see.**

Lingyue looked at the drawer a long time.

"Bring her to the window," she said.

---

So the Cantor of the Hollow Bell bent, very slowly, as a reed bends in a very gentle wind, four meters of milk-glass bending down outside the back-room window until the great ring of throats at the base of her neck was level with the sill, and with Hush on it, and with the drawer on two chairs inside.

And looked.

Xiaodeng was awake. She didn't cry. She lay in Fat Tan's blanket in a drawer that had held the Black Tide accounts for thirty years and looked up with the cloudy unfocused eyes of a ten-day-old at an enormous pale shape filling the window.

The Cantor didn't move.

> **She says that on the Hollow Bell a little lamp glows for one day. Then it stops glowing, and it's a child, and it's taught the song.**
>
> **She taught the song to every little lamp of her age-groups for nine hundred years. Thousands. She chose the brightest. She dressed them in light. She sang them up.**
>
> **She says three of them were her own.**

On the bed Lingyue went very still.

> **She says she didn't want to. She's never told anyone that. There was no way to say it; the song didn't have it.**
>
> **She sang her own children up the thread to a hand in the dark because the song said that was what love was. And they were taken, all three, in three ages. And the hand passed the Hollow Bell by. And the song said she'd done well.**

The baby in the drawer looked up at the enormous milk-glass face in the window, and made a sound.

Not a cry. A note, small, ten days old. The note she'd made in the Awakening Hall the morning she was born, *here I am*, except it wasn't furious now. Just a note, the sound a baby makes at something interesting.

The Cantor of the Hollow Bell made a sound back.

---

It wasn't much.

He heard it from the floor by the bed, through Seed and the staff. One note, from one of the great ring of throats. Very small, for something so large. Very old, for something so new. Wavering, as a voice wavers that's never been used for anything but the one sentence it was given and is trying, at nine hundred, to say something that's only its own.

Not a laugh, as Ninth's had been. Not an answer, as the thousand and seventy had answered in the hall. Lower. Slower. Softer.

> **Ninth says—** said Seed. **She's in the courtyard. She heard it.**
>
> **Ninth says that's what the song was, before. Before the hand came, before the first Cantor learned to give. The Old Chord used to say there was a time before the song, when the Hollow Bell sang other things, and nobody remembers what. She always thought it was a story.**
>
> **It isn't. The song was a lullaby once. To children. Before it was a lullaby to a hand.**
>
> **She says the Cantor's found the note it came from.**

The baby in the drawer had gone to sleep.

The Cantor stayed bent at the window, four meters of milk-glass, throats level with the sill, singing her one note very softly over and over to a child of another world asleep in a drawer, until the winter sun had gone down behind the roofs of the old quarter and the courtyard was dark.

Nobody asked her to stop.

---

> **Zhou Xiaodeng. Good morning.**
>
> **I'm Seed. I live in your father's head. I count things.**
>
> **Your name is Xiaodeng. Little lamp. Your aunt chose it, and the Collector, and the Singers a long time ago, and your mother, by holding up a lamp. Your parents decided it. Nobody said yes. You can change it when you're old enough to say so.**
>
> **I'm telling you first. What do you want?**

The child in the drawer didn't wake.

On the sill above her, in the morning sun, Hush had all its limbs out.

And in the courtyard below the window, by the dry fountain, the Cantor of the Hollow Bell and the one who would have been sung up ninth stood side by side, not singing, looking up at a shell over the city that had been silent fifteen days. And in it, very faint, for the first time, Seed said, from somewhere deep in its chambers where the Old Chord were: a note.

One. Old. Wavering. Not in the song.
