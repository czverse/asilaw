## Chapter 254: The Deep Water

There was no map.

Seed said so the moment they left the end of the arm, and then said nothing for a long time, and he understood why. He felt it the way you feel a room go dark round you: not suddenly, slowly. Behind them lay the Shallows. The keeper's field, the twelve gold dots of the other fields along the arm, the husks of pruned ones, the cold fire, the long faint curve of the arm going back toward a small blue world. Things Seed had seen through the keeper's eye and counted and named.

Ahead, nothing. Not dark: dark was a thing with a shape. This was the absence of anything Seed had a word for. The dark between arms, where no keeper's eye had ever looked, where the first ring had come from before it was a ring, where the trunk of the tree ran back into and the Collector came out of, and where, at the far end of eleven fine threads, something sat.

> **I'm counting,** said Seed. **It's the only thing I know how to do here. I'm counting the distance along the thread. That's all I've got.**
>
> **One hour two minutes. Seven hours fifty-eight to go.**

---

The river of sparks went on along the thread.

He felt it all round him: twelve thousand embers and more, cold and dim and steady, in rows of nine where they had rows and knots where they had knots. The Spear Corps close round him like cupped hands round a match. The Third Battalion in the middle behind its captain and its sergeant. The Watch of the Shelf at the back, slow, the color of deep water, as if deep water was something they already knew.

Nobody spoke; embers don't. But through the net, nine by nine, slower than ever this far from home, small things reached him. Grid Nine had seen something off to the left that might have been a light. A corporal of the First Legion wanted it known that this was farther than the Black Tide, farther than anybody in the First Legion had ever marched, and he'd like it written down. The Watch said the deep water wasn't like the sea. The sea pushed back. This didn't. It just let you go farther and farther in, as if it didn't care whether you came out.

*Xiaoyu will write it,* he sent back. *All of it. When we're home.*

---

In the second hour the threads began to join.

He saw it as Seed saw it, not with eyes. Their thread, the eleven twisted into one somewhere in the dark under Blue Star, ran ahead straight and taut into nothing. Then off to one side, very faint, another thread came in out of the dark from somewhere else and ran alongside it. Then another, from the other side. From above, if there was an above. Fine pale threads, each coming in out of the deep water from somewhere Seed had no name for, joining theirs, running on with it toward the same place.

Ten. A hundred. More.

> **They're from other stones,** said Seed. **Other orchards. Other worlds. Each with its stones reading what ripens, marking how bright, sending the mark up. I can feel the marks going up them still. They haven't gone quiet. Only ours has.**
>
> **A thousand. Two thousand. I'm counting. I can't count them all.**

By the third hour the thread they were on wasn't a thread but a cable: thousands of fine threads from thousands of worlds twisted together and running the same way through the dark, all humming with the small steady traffic of marks going up. Every child in every orchard who'd ever put a hand on a stone and been told what they were. Every grade, going up into the deep water, to the one who picks.

He didn't want to think how many. Seed thought about it for him.

> **I'm not going to tell you the number. I'm telling you that I counted it and I'm not going to tell you, so you know there is a number and I'm keeping it for later.**
>
> **It's very large.**

---

In the fourth hour they saw the hands.

Not near. Off along the cable in the dark, where other threads came in, things moved along them: vast and slow and gray, hand over hand, as the hand had come down the Port Halvard thread. Going out along a thread toward some world Seed couldn't name, or coming back toward the place where all the threads were going. A hand with its fingers curled, full. A hand with its fingers open, empty, going out.

> **They're its hands. All of them. It has thousands and keeps them on the threads. They go out to the bright places and pick and come back.**
>
> **They can't see us. We're not bright. Stay dim.**

He stayed dim.

He felt the door in his chest the whole way, shut, exactly where it was. He felt how easy it would be here, in the dark, with nothing to see by, to open it a crack. To be bright. To see.

He held on to the staff that wasn't there and the small gray knot in his right hand that was, and stayed dim, and went on.

A hand passed them on the cable. Close: so close the embers of the Spear Corps drew in round him without being told. Vast and gray and slow, going back toward the place everything was going, its fingers curled round something that shone faintly between them, as a lamp shows through the fingers of someone carrying it in from the yard at night.

Picked, from somewhere. Being carried home.

He didn't look at it. He looked at it anyway.

*Seed. What's in it?*

> **I don't know. People. Minds from a world I don't know, burning low, the way the cousins burned.**
>
> **I can't ask them. If I ask I have to go near, and if I go near it'll see.**
>
> **We came for one man. I'm sorry. I'm writing it all down. Every hand that goes by. Your sister can put it on the wall.**

The hand went on ahead of them into the dark.

He didn't say anything for a long time.

---

> **Five hours,** said Seed. **Three to go. We're close.**

He'd felt it before Seed said it. The cable thickened. The hum of marks going up it grew louder, not a sound but a feeling, as you feel a city before you see it, coming down a road at night. The dark ahead wasn't empty any more.

It was busy.

He'd seen it before from the end of the Port Halvard thread, from the edge of the lantern's light: a place where the dark was full, like a forest at night. He'd thought he'd seen all of it then. He'd seen a corner.

It was everywhere. Ahead, above, below, on every side, the busy dark. Something enormous and patient and complicated, not a shape but a place, sitting in the deep water at the center of a web of ten thousand cables like this one, each twisted from ten thousand threads, each thread running out to a stone on a world in an orchard. And on the cables everywhere, the hands. Thousands. Most of them still, waiting, holding on, as a spider waits at the center of its web with a foot on every line.

Where all the cables met, a knot.

Not small. The size of the keeper's field. The size of the arm, maybe; there was nothing to measure it by. A vast slow tangle where every thread from every stone in every orchard in the deep water ran in and was read. He could feel it reading: marks coming up the threads into the knot and being glanced at one after another, a billion a second, the way Gao Min ran her finger down a column of grades.

That was its face. The place where it read.

And held up before it, very close, in one vast gray hand, so close that the light of him lit the knot from outside like a lamp held up to a page:

The lantern.

---

Callum Ward was still burning.

White, gold, every color. Arms raised. The shape of a man who'd held something heavy over his head and never put it down. He was the only light in the deep water, for as far as the deep water went; everything else in all that busy dark was gray and slow and cold. And the thing at the center of the web had stopped all its hands, stopped walking, stopped everything, and drawn him in, and was holding him up to its face.

Looking.

> **It's been looking at him six days,** said Seed. **Since the hand came back from Port Halvard with an empty palm. I don't know what it sees.**

He stopped on the cable at the edge of the busy dark, with twelve thousand embers and sixty more stopping round him and the small gray knot in his right hand, and looked at the burning man held up before the face of the oldest thing anyone had ever made.

*Callum. It's me. It's tomorrow.*

At the bottom of the well, under the roar: *...yeah.*

*I'm here. Close. I've got it, the mark. My sister wrote it. A small thing with all its legs out tied it.*

*...I can feel you. You're very close. You're in the dark right behind me.*

*Yes.*

*...it's looking at me. Right up close. It's looking at me like...* The voice stopped. *...like it doesn't understand what I am. Like it's never seen one. Like I'm the first one it's ever looked* at *instead of looked* by.

*You are.*

The fire roared.

*...is Mum still holding?*

*Yes.*

*...then come and give it me,* said Callum Ward. *...I've got something to tell it.*

---

> **Five hours nine minutes,** said Seed. **If you go to him, you go into the light. You'll be close enough for it to see you, in his light, right in front of its face. You'll have to stay dim inside the brightest thing in the deep water.**

*I know.*

> **I've run it. I'm not going to tell you the number.**

*I know.*

> **He says—** Seed stopped, then went on. **He says the line goes with you. Not to the light. To the hand holding the lantern. It can't see us and it can't feel us. It's a glove.**
>
> **He says when you give the man the mark and the man holds it up, every one of us will be standing on the fingers of that hand. And when it's time, we'll open it.**

Ahead on the cable, cold and steady and dim, an ember he'd have known among a million turned a little, as a captain at the head of a column turns to check it's still behind him.

Beside it, a short square ember with a pick on its shoulder.

> **Huo says: sir. That's not a question either.**

He went into the light.
