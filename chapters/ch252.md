## Chapter 252: The Mark

It was Gao Min who saw it, because she'd spent forty-two years writing marks down.

They were at the long table in the banking hall at three in the afternoon, the day after the Collector told them what the one who picks was. All of them: Kwan with the laptop, Qin Hao with his notebook, Su Mingli with her arms folded, Lingyue on the stairs with the sword, Xiaoyu at the end of the table beside Shi Lei, who came every afternoon now with his steel spear and sat down without being asked. Old Seven at the bronze door. And Gao Min in her gray Bureau coat with the old taped graph unfolded in front of her, studying it as she'd studied it for twenty years.

They'd been at it since nine. How do you tell something with nobody in it that the house is empty?

Nobody had an answer.

"It reads marks," said Gao Min.

She didn't say it loudly. She said it to the graph, finger on the pencil line, in the flat careful voice she used for everything that mattered.

"Say that again," said Su Mingli.

"The knot in the stone. READ WHAT RIPENS. MARK HOW BRIGHT. SEND THE MARK UP THE THREAD. WHEN THE ORCHARD IS BRIGHT, THE PICKER COMES." She looked up. "It doesn't see the orchard. It's never seen anything but burning. It knows the orchard's bright because the stones send marks up the thread and it reads them." She tapped the graph. "That's what this is. Forty-two years of what it read. That's how it knew where to come."

The table went quiet.

"It can't be asked. There's nobody in it. Fine. But it reads. It's been reading marks off those threads since before the gardens. It's the one thing it's ever done that isn't picking." She took off her glasses. "You don't need to ask it. You need to send it a mark."

---

> **Yes,** said Seed. **I didn't see that. I've spent two days, since the hall, trying to find a way to ask it, and every model I have is a model of asking.**
>
> **It doesn't need asking. It needs telling, in the only language it has. A mark.**

"Saying what?" said Qin Hao.

For a moment nobody answered. Then everybody looked at the end of the table.

Xiaoyu had gone very still.

"The house is empty," she said. "You can stop."

"And who writes it?" said Lingyue from the stairs. Not a question. She knew. She said it so it was said.

His sister looked at the marker in her hand.

"It's true when I write it," she said slowly. "Seed said. When he reads what I write, it's like being told by someone who was there." She turned the marker over. "If I write it and it reads it, it's not just words. It's true. It *knows* it."

> **I don't know if that works on something with nobody in it. I don't know what your Law is. Nobody does. It isn't on the tree in the stone. Wen Jia opened what you said, and what you said was: so nobody can say afterward it wasn't there.**
>
> **That isn't reading and it isn't knowing. It's something else. I can't run it. There's nothing to run it against.**

"So we try," said Xiaoyu.

---

They asked Wen Jia that evening, through the staff, at the foot of the platform in the Awakening Hall, with the warm stone in front of them and the short line of holders who hadn't wanted to stop.

*Can you send a mark up the thread?*

> ***...I can. I haven't. Not once. Not since the night the captain put his hand on me. We said we wouldn't send anything, and we haven't.***
>
> ***...but the knot knows how. It's what it was made for. MARK HOW BRIGHT, SEND THE MARK UP. It's like breathing for it. I've been holding my breath for three weeks.***

*One mark. Not a brightness. Something Xiaoyu writes.*

The stone was quiet a while.

> ***...it'll go up all eleven. The threads join; whatever goes up one goes up all of them. And it'll read it the way it reads everything. Fast. All at once. Like a glance.***
>
> ***...I don't think a glance is enough. It's been reading marks since before the gardens. Millions. Billions. It doesn't stop and look at a mark. It reads it and moves on. That's what it's for.***
>
> ***...if you want it to really see one, it has to be looking. Not glancing. Looking. And it's only ever looked at one thing.***

Nobody said it. Everybody knew.

On the platform, under her own letters, Xiaoyu said it anyway.

"Callum. It's looking at Callum."

---

> **It's holding him up to its face,** said Seed. **It stopped walking to look at him. The first thing in all that time it's ever looked at. If the mark's on him, if he's holding it, when it looks...**

"You want to write on a man," said Su Mingli flatly. "A man who's on fire. In the hand of the oldest thing in the dark. Forty-eight days away."

> **Not on him. Give it to him to hold.**
>
> **Wen Jia sends it up the thread. It goes past along all eleven, and the one who picks glances at it and moves on. But somebody carries it the rest of the way, in their hand, up the thread to the lantern, and puts it in his. And he holds it up.**

At the end of the table Shi Lei made a sound.

"*Told me what I was,*" he said. Everybody looked at him and he went red. "That's what he said. Callum Ward. You said so, when you came back from the thread the first time. He kept saying it. It told me what I was, F, so I told it what it was." He held on to his spear. "He hit it with a hammer because it told him what he was. And now he'd be the one who tells *it*."

The banking hall was silent.

> **Yes,** said Seed. **The one nobody picked, telling the one who picks.**
>
> **I didn't see that either. I'd like to stop not seeing things.**

---

He went up at midnight and asked Callum. The short way, not near, to the edge of the light where a man burned in a vast hand, held up close to the face of something that had stopped walking to look at him.

*Callum. It's me. It's tomorrow.*

*...yeah.*

He told him all of it. The mark, the stone, the girl who wrote things that were true. The house, which was empty. The thing holding him, which had been carrying fruit to it since before the gardens and didn't know. That someone would come up the thread and put the mark in his hand. That when it looked at him he could hold it up, or not. That if he did, nobody knew what would happen. That afterward, whatever happened, they'd cut the hand open and bring him home.

*You don't have to,* he said. *You can just come home.*

The fire roared. At the bottom of the well, a long silence.

*...it told me what I was,* said Callum Ward.

*Yes.*

*...seven years. F. It told me. And I went and hit it, because I wanted to tell it what it was.*

*Yes.*

*...and I couldn't. I just burned. I hit it and it opened me and I just burned.*

The fire roared.

*...so that's the deal, is it. I get to tell it what it is. Properly. The thing that took me. With something true. Before I come home.*

*If you want.*

*...yeah,* said the voice at the bottom of the well. *...yeah, I want. Tell your sister to write it big. It's got a big face.*

---

They asked the dead on the fourth night after Port Halvard.

Down the net, nine by nine, slowly. Seed wrote the question with Xiaoyu as Article Eleven required: what it was and where. Forty-eight days of deep water, which on the thread would be minutes, and then nine hours there, because Seed had run the body and the holders and said nine hours was what a Lord's body could hold empty with fifteen hundred people anchoring it, and not one minute more. A hand to cut open, in the dark, beside a thing that had seen him bright. A man to bring home. A mark to carry.

*It is not your border,* the question said. *It is not defense. It is a man who asked to come home. You can say no.*

It came back as the net answered everything now: a pile of index cards on the long table, and on top, the one that had gone round six hundred times.

Kwan read it.

"The Spear Corps, stone No. 7. They said: he was an F. Nobody picked him. We'll go and pick him." She turned it over. "Everybody's added. Yes. Yes. Grid Nine says they'd like to see the deep water too. The Watch says it's not the sea but it's close enough." She stopped. "And there's one at the bottom that came in an hour ago. It's not from the net."

"Where's it from?"

"The keeper. It came down the gold thread. It's not a yes. It's a message from the cold fire."

> **Shiomi rang it to the keeper and the keeper wrote it down,** said Seed. **It doesn't usually write things down. It says it's learning from Xiaoyu.**

Kwan read it.

"The sergeant says the Sixty are at the cold fire, facing out. The thread to the deep water goes right past them, right over their heads. He's been watching it for three weeks." She swallowed. "He says: when the boy comes up it, we're coming with him."

"And: tell the captain that arm had better still have some fingers on it."

At the bronze door, Old Seven turned round.

---

Lingyue didn't say anything until everyone else had gone to bed.

She sat on the stairs with the sword across her knees and the lamp-sized flame in her palm, not looking at it, and he sat on the step below her with the staff, and for a long time neither of them spoke.

"I can't come," she said at last.

"No."

"It's a thread. You go up it without your body. I can't do that. I'm not a necromancer. I haven't got a thing in my head." Her voice was very level. "I've come with you everywhere. The rift. The north wall. The island. The cut. The pier." She looked at the flame. "Every time something came down out of the sky, I was there, with the sword. Uphill if I had to. I said so on the slope. At the wedding, in front of everyone."

"I know."

"And this time I have to sit in a hall with my hand on your chest holding your body down while you go and do it without me."

He didn't answer.

She closed her hand on the flame and opened it again. Still there.

"Your fire goes," he said. "The pale fire. It's yours and mine wound together, and it goes up through the staff. Everything I cut up there, I cut with your light."

"I know."

"So you're there."

"It's not the same."

"No," he said. "It isn't."

She sat with her hand open and the light in it, and the house was quiet round them.

"Nine hours," said Shen Lingyue. "Seed said nine hours. Not one minute more."

"Yes."

"If you're not back in nine hours," said his wife, "I'm going to pull. I don't care if you're in the middle of something. I don't care if the house is empty or full or on fire. I'll put my hand on your chest and pull, like I did at the pier, and you'll come down that thread whether you've finished or not." She looked down at him. "That's what I'll do with your answer. I'm telling you first."

"Yes," said Yuanshu. "Please."

She thumped him on the arm. Then she didn't take her hand away. She left it there on his arm, on the stairs, in the light from her other palm, until the lamp over the board went out.

---

At the top of the long white wall, in the strip above HUNDRED-ARROW LIN that the ladder didn't quite reach, Xiaoyu had already written the Collector's want and the one who picks. Now, on her toes on the top step with Lingyue holding her ankles, she added one more line.

Not the mark itself. That she'd write tomorrow on the floor of the Awakening Hall, in front of the stone, for Wen Jia to send. This was only so it was written.

*THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU CAN STOP. — TO BE CARRIED BY CALLUM WARD, F, WHO WAS NOT PICKED, AND WHO ASKED TO BE THE ONE WHO TELLS IT.*
