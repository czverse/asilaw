## Chapter 282: Ichiro

He talked all night, and everyone on Blue Star heard him, and nobody minded.

It began in the bow of the Severin flagship, two hundred kilometers over Lingzhou, in a chamber only the House of Severin entered, inside a disc of black stone the width of a courtyard, ground so thin you could see through it. It began the moment the crossing ended, at twenty-three minutes past eleven on the eighth night. Seed felt it start. So did Wen Jia. The old man at Port Abiri felt it and said *oh no, he's started*, in a voice Seed said was fond.

And it went out.

Not to Blue Star, not yet. Through the Lens. Because the Lens was the root, and every stone cut from it was still a little part of it, and a fog-ringer of twenty-two who'd been quiet nine hundred years and talked to fish had just sat down in the root and found he could be heard by everything it had ever been cut into.

> **He's talking to the cells,** said Seed. **All of them, four million one hundred and eighty thousand, through the layer that stops their ears. Not as sound: as marks. Thousands of marks, one after another, very fast.**
>
> **He isn't tying them properly. He's tying them the way he rings a boat call, too fast in the middle. They're getting through anyway.**

---

He heard some of it; Seed let him. He sat on the platform of the Awakening Hall at midnight with his back to the warm black stone, beside the body of a nun of twelve hundred years who sat cross-legged with her hand on it and a lamp in her lap, still burning. On the shore.

> **Hello. Hello. Can you hear me? Not hear. Read. You're reading this. I know you can't hear; they stopped your ears. That's all right. I'm not going to say anything you need ears for.**
>
> **My name's Ichiro. I'm from Shiomi. It's a fishing village. It doesn't exist any more, except it does, it's in a fog in a bay on a world called Blue Star, and my sister's there, and she's also here, holding a lamp. I'll show you in a minute.**
>
> **I'm in the Lens. The big black stone in the front of the ship, the one that reads everybody. I'm sitting in it. I'm sorry, I know it's yours. It's not yours, it's the House's, I know. I'm sitting in it anyway.**
>
> **I'm going to talk to you for a while. I talk too much. Everyone says so. My sister says so. She's right. She's always right. It's very annoying.**
>
> **I'm not going to open anything or burn anything or take anyone out. Not tonight. I'm just going to talk to you, and tomorrow night I'm going to ask you something, and I'm telling you first so you have a day to think about it.**
>
> **I'm going to ask you what you want. That's all. Nobody's going to make you do anything with the answer. Someone's just going to hear it.**

---

Tam sat on the step below him. He'd been there since the First of the Ladder walked up out of the ring of children into the sky, and hadn't moved. His face was turned up and his eyes shut.

"They're reading it," he said. "All of them."

He didn't open his eyes.

"They've stopped singing. All the ones who were singing have stopped to read." His voice was very steady. "Seri's reading it, in the fourth array. She keeps reading the bit about the fishing village."

He was quiet a while.

"Some of them are crying," said Tam. "You can't cry in a wall. They're crying anyway."

---

The First of the Ladder reached the Lens at a quarter past midnight.

Seed felt it. Ichiro felt it and stopped talking, for the first time in an hour, which Seed said was a record, and then started again. Iselde, on the bridge of the Eighth Candle, felt it through the cores of the Rowed Fleet, who weren't deaf and could hear everything, and called him at once on the keeper's eye.

> ***He is in the chamber. The C's on the flagship are talking, the engineers. The Lens chamber's door was opened for the first time in nine hundred years by someone who was not a Paragon of the House at a reading.***
>
> ***He is standing in front of it. He has not done anything.***

> **Ichiro says he's here,** said Seed. **The man in white. He's standing in front of him in the chamber, looking at the Lens. He's got a little bit of it in his hand, cracked, black.**
>
> **He says he doesn't look angry. He looks like a man who's come home and found someone sitting in his chair.**
>
> **He says: hello.**

---

He didn't hear what passed between them. Nobody did. It happened in the Lens, in the chamber in the bow of the Severin flagship, between a fog-ringer and the First of the Ladder, and Ichiro said afterward it wasn't his to tell. Kiyo said that was the first time in his life he'd ever not told something, and she was proud of him.

But he heard the end of it at three in the morning. Seed carried it, because Ichiro asked it to.

> **Ichiro says the man asked him to get out. Not ordered: asked. He said the word, in the Federation's language, without the grade. He said it very badly, like a man saying a word in a language he learned from a book.**
>
> **Ichiro told him no. He told him he was asked here by a lot of people and said yes, and he's staying until everyone in the walls has been asked what they want. And then the man can ask him again, tomorrow. That's the rule.**
>
> **The man said: if I break the Lens, you go with it.**
>
> **Ichiro said: yes. And so does the Ladder. Every mark on every face of every person in the Federation was cut from this stone. Break it and you break all of them.**
>
> **He didn't know that. Nobody in his House knew. They thought it was a treasure. They didn't know it was the root.**
>
> **He stood there a long time. Then he sat down.**

---

> **Ichiro says he's sitting on the floor of the chamber in front of the Lens with his back against the wall and the cracked bit in his hand. He hasn't said anything for an hour.**
>
> **Kiyo's holding the lamp up in the Lens so he can see it. He's looking at it.**

---

The ninth night came, and the First of the Ladder didn't turn the Lens on Blue Star.

He didn't have to. It was already turned on everything, and had been since twenty-three minutes past eleven on the eighth night, when a fisherman sat down in it. At nine on the ninth night, the hour the Fleet Marshal had named, two hundred kilometers over the city in the bow of a ship forty times the size of the Candle, a disc of black stone the width of a courtyard opened.

And Blue Star was asked.

---

He was on the north wall. Everyone was: half of Lingzhou, the Candle's children, the night watch, the dead along the battlements in their rows of nine, and a woman with a sword on her back and a month-old baby in a sling and a lamp in her hand, held.

It wasn't a voice or a sound. It came as the Singers' song had come on the first night, up out of the stones of the city and the river and the sky, the way you hear something you've always known and forgotten. Except it wasn't beautiful as the song had been. It was a young man's voice, quick, a little too fast in the middle, in every language on Blue Star at once, and it was talking.

> *Hello. Hello, Blue Star. Can you hear me? This time you can. Your ears aren't stopped.*
>
> *I'm Ichiro. From Shiomi. It was a fishing village in Yamato. Some of you know it. Most of you don't. It's in a fog in a bay now. My sister's there. She's also here. She's holding a lamp. Look up. North. Over the end of the world. There.*

And in the north, low over the end of the world, beside the keeper's small white ring and the Elder's vast gray one and the small gold point at the end of a branch nobody could see, among ninety burning cathedrals of the Lamplit: a light.

Not white, not gold, not the colors of a House. Pale and steady and, from the ground, the size of a single star. The color of whale oil in fog.

A lamp.

On the north wall Lingyue made a sound.

> *That's her. That's Kiyo. She's twelve hundred years old and she's holding a lamp up inside a stone the size of a courtyard in the front of a ship so you can all see where the shore is. She's very annoying. I love her very much.*
>
> *I'm in a stone that used to read people. Every person. Everything that breathes. And mark them, and put them on a ladder. I'm not going to do that.*
>
> *I'm not going to read you or mark you or open anything in you. You've got your own stones for that, and there's a girl in one of them who's very good at it, and she'd kill me if I tried.*
>
> *I'm just going to ask. And I'm telling you first: nobody's going to do anything with the answer. Not tonight. Someone's just going to hear it and know you said it.*
>
> *That's all I'm for. I talk too much. But I listen too. Everybody forgets that.*
>
> *So.*
>
> *Eight billion people on Blue Star. Four million one hundred and eighty thousand in the walls of ninety ships over your heads. Seventeen thousand four hundred in the walls of twelve ships that rowed here. And everyone on every ship who wears a face cut from this stone. I can see you too. All of you. Hello.*
>
> *And one man sitting on the floor in front of me with his back against the wall and a cracked bit of me in his hand.*
>
> *What do you want?*

---

Eight billion people stood still.

He felt it on the north wall and in the city below and across the curve of the world, as he'd felt the lanterns spread across Blue Star's night side when his mother said the word. Not quiet. The opposite of quiet. Eight billion people asked the same question at the same moment by a fog-ringer in a stone, and every one of them, at once, finding out what they'd say.

He didn't know what they said. Nobody did. Ichiro had told them first: someone would hear it, that was all. He heard it, all night. Seed said he never once stopped listening.

But on the north wall beside him, Yuanshu heard some.

A soldier of the night watch, helmet in hand, saying quite loudly to nobody: *I want to go home and see my kids.* A woman behind him: *I want to stop being afraid.* One of the Candle's children, a girl, saying something in the Federation's language that Seed translated as *I want to learn to swim properly.* Dabao, enormous, A-rank, Unbroken Wall, tears running down into his collar: *I want my mum's cart to have a roof.* Fat Tan at the top of the Sector Nine stairs, saying nothing, and then very quietly: *I want to stop keeping things in boxes.*

Xiaoyu at the parapet with the marker uncapped: *I want to write it all down.*

And Lingyue.

She stood beside him with a sword on her back and a baby in a sling and a lamp in her hand, looking up at a lamp in the north, and said in a voice he'd never heard her use:

"I want her to be asked."

---

He didn't answer.

He found he couldn't. He stood in his old place on the Sector Nine battlement with the staff in his hand and a one-armed dead captain at the top of the stairs and a lamp in the north, and was asked by a fog-ringer in a stone what he wanted.

And for the first time since a cemetery at three in the morning when he was eighteen, he didn't know what to say.

> **That's allowed,** said Seed. **Ichiro says that's allowed. Lots of people don't know. He hears them not knowing. He says it sounds like the sea.**
>
> **He says: ask me again tomorrow, boy. That's what you'd say.**

---

And from two hundred kilometers over their heads, from the bow of a ship forty times the size of the Candle, through the Lens and the keeper's eye and Seed and the staff, very quietly, so that only those who could hear the net heard it, came a man's voice.

Calm. Courteous. Not absolute any more.

The voice of someone who'd never once in his life needed to raise it and had never once been asked a question, sitting on a floor with his back against a wall, answering one.

Seed carried it. It didn't say whether it had asked first. It didn't need to.

> *...I wanted to have been asked.*
>
> *...when I was twelve. On the morning. Before the stone read me and said SSS and my father stood up in the box.*
>
> *...I wanted someone to ask me first.*

> ***...well,*** said Ichiro from the Lens, very gently, and for once not too fast in the middle at all. ***...now someone has.***
