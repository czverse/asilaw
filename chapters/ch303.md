## Chapter 303: Later

The mark from home reached them on the eleventh day, on the carry road, nineteen hours after Xiaoyu sent it.

He knew it was hers before Seed read it. Her marks had a shape. They came down the net the way her capitals went onto a wall, square and black with no wasted strokes, and true, so that even at the end of a thread so long he could hardly feel the far end, they arrived whole. He felt it slide in under him out of the dark behind, like a letter pushed under a door.

> **It's from Qin Hao,** said Seed. **Through Xiaoyu. It's a graph.**

Seed read him all of it. The weights, day by day, in Bai Lu's numbers. Gao Min's tiredness column, one to five, in the margin of the rota. The three flat days. The line going up. The arithmetic: two thousand five hundred in ten days, six thousand in a month. And at the bottom, in his sister's capitals, a line that wasn't Qin Hao's:

*LINGYUE SAYS: DON'T TURN ROUND BECAUSE OF THIS. IF YOU TURN ROUND BECAUSE OF US SHE'LL HIT YOU. SHE ASKED THE WORLD. NOT EVERYONE SAID YES. ENOUGH DID. FOR NOW.*

He went on along the carry road inside the Collector's warmth, Old Seven cold and steady beside him, and the line of the graph sat in him like a stone in a shoe.

---

> **I was wrong,** said Seed, about an hour later.

He'd been waiting for it.

> **I ran the holding all night before we left, every way I could think of. Time and people, people and time. I never ran distance. I didn't think a thread could pull harder for being longer. On Blue Star it never did; a Stele's thread is the same length every year.**
>
> **Qin Hao saw it because we stopped. If we hadn't stopped at the first orchard he'd have seen a curve and blamed something else.**
>
> **That's the twelfth error. It's the worst one. It's the only one that's cost anybody anything.**
>
> **I'm annoyed.**

*With Qin Hao?*

> **With me. Qin Hao was right, and I'd like to tell him so. It'll take nineteen hours to reach him, and by then he'll have found a thirteenth.**

He nearly laughed. On the carry road, in the dark between the arms, further from anything than anyone from Blue Star had ever been, he nearly laughed.

*Run it again,* he thought. *With the distance in. All of it.*

> **I am.**

---

It took Seed most of the day.

He could feel it working. Not like the early days in the cemetery, fast and hot and sure of itself, a thousand runs a second and a number at the end. Slower and more careful, like someone counting money they can't afford to get wrong. It ran the holding again with Qin Hao's line in it, and the rota, and the stones, and the world's *not everyone, enough for now*. It ran the carry road: how fast the Collector went, which it could measure, and how far the house was, which it couldn't.

> **I can't give you an answer,** it said finally. **I can give you a shape.**
>
> **The cost to the holders goes up with how far out we are. It comes down again as we come back. It doesn't come down when we stop; stopping just holds it where it is. So every day out here costs whatever that day's distance costs, and the furthest days cost most.**

*So we go fast.*

> **As fast as it'll go. Straight to the house, no stops. Then home, and on the way home every day's cheaper than the last.**

He saw the rest before Seed said it. He'd been seeing it all day at the edge of everything, like a drop you sense in the dark before you see it.

*The wants.*

> **Yes. The first orchard took three days, and the cost held flat for those three days. That was cheap because we were close. Every orchard further down the road will cost more to stop at than the one before. If we stop to give wants back on the way out, we pay the most for it. If we give them back on the way home, every one is cheaper than the last.**
>
> **The arithmetic's plain. The Collector isn't going to like it.**

---

The second old stone joined the carry road on the twelfth day.

He felt it as he'd felt the first: a gray thread coming in from one side, older than the web, handmade, running into the worn pale cable. Another of the first orchards. Another stone on another hill, still marking every year a field where nothing ripened. And under that stone, if he went down, he knew what he'd find.

The Collector slowed.

He felt it gather as it had at the first, its warmth leaning toward the gray thread. And among all the wants it carried, more than Seed had a number for, packed like sand, he felt a few stir, warmer than the rest and older. From there.

> *I have nine from there,* said the Collector. *Nine children, on nine reading mornings, one every few hundred years, at the end, when there was almost nothing left to pick. I came after the hands and found them and asked.*
>
> *I would like to give them back. The last child said: give them all back.*

He didn't answer straight away.

He'd known this was coming since the morning. He'd run it with Seed and seen the line and known the next gray thread would come and the Collector would slow and he'd have to say it. He hadn't known it would feel like this: like standing at the top of a cliff path beside someone very old who's waited a very long time, and telling them they can't go down to the beach.

*We can't stop,* he thought. *Not now.*

The warmth didn't move.

So he told it everything, as Seed had told him. The line and the cost. The holders in the hall on East Ring Road and on the beach at Kesse and in the valley under the shrine, more tired every day the road got longer. Lingyue asking the world; not everyone saying yes; *enough, for now*. That every day out here cost more than the one before, and the days going home would cost less and less, and that if they gave the wants back on the way home they could stop at every gray thread on the whole carry road and it would still come cheaper than stopping at this one now.

He told it that he wanted to give them back. That he wanted to go down that thread more than almost anything. He was a necromancer, and there were people in a hill down there nobody had ever asked anything.

*But not now,* he said. *Later. On the way home. I promise.*

---

The Collector didn't answer for a long time.

It had stopped. Not completely, Seed said; it was still creeping along the carry road, as an old man still shifts his weight a little when he's stopped at a street corner. But it had all but stopped.

> *I have never been asked to wait,* it said at last.
>
> *I have waited. A very long time. For things to ripen, for the hands to come, for wants to be given to me. That is not the same. Nobody ever asked me to.*
>
> *I do not know how.*

*It isn't hard,* he thought. *You just don't do the thing yet. Then you do it later.*

> *What if there is no later?*

He had no answer to that. He'd promised. That was all he had.

Old Seven turned. The cold ember beside him on the carry road, one-armed, who hadn't said a word in two days, turned his attention to the warmth all around them, as he'd turn to face a new recruit on the eastern wall.

> **He says there's always a later,** said Seed. **That's what a soldier's for.**
>
> **He says on the eastern wall in the Black Tide they couldn't bury their dead, not while the wall was being held. They laid them down behind the line in rows and said: later. When the wall holds, we'll come back for you. Some of the men who said it didn't live to come back. Most did. And the ones who didn't, somebody else came back for. That's how later works. You promise it, and if you can't keep it, the man next to you does.**
>
> **He says the boy has a man next to him.**

The Collector's warmth held perfectly still.

> *You came back for them?*

> **He says no. He was one of the ones laid down behind the line. The boy came back for him, thirty years later, at three in the morning, and then for the rest of the line.**
>
> **He says: that's later.**

---

The Collector went on.

It didn't say yes. It didn't say anything. But it went on, past the gray thread, past the second of the first orchards, along the worn cable into the dark: slowly at first, then faster, faster than he'd ever felt it go, like an old woman walking briskly past a door she's decided not to knock on, so she won't change her mind.

And as it passed the join, it did something.

It reached out. Not down the thread; only to the place where the thread met the carry road. It left something there, small and warm, the way you leave a pebble on a gatepost to show someone you came by.

> **It's left a mark,** said Seed. **Not a want; all nine are still in it. Something like a mark, on the join.**
>
> **It says: later.**
>
> **It says it's never written anything before and it doesn't know if it's doing it right. It wants to be able to find the place again.**

It did the same on the fourteenth day at the third gray thread, and on the fifteenth at the fourth, and on the seventeenth at the fifth and sixth, which came in close together from opposite sides. Every time it slowed and gathered, and he felt the old wants stir in it, warmer than the rest; and every time it didn't stop. It reached out to the join, left something small and warm, and went on.

*Later.*

*Later.*

*Later.*

---

> **Death Law: 59.4 percent,** said Seed. **It hasn't moved. We haven't opened the door since the hill.**
>
> **I'm telling you because you've stopped asking, and I think that's because you're afraid to. Don't be. The wall isn't going anywhere, and neither are we.**

On the eighteenth day, nineteen hours late, a mark slid in under him out of the dark. Square, black, whole.

*THREE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AT THE PEAK SHIFT. ACROSS ELEVEN STONES. A MAN DIED ON THE TWO O'CLOCK. HE WAS EIGHTY-ONE. HE SAID YES. BAI LU SAYS HIS HEART. HIS FAMILY SAYS HE WANTED TO.*

*WEN JIA ASKED HIM. HE SAID HE'D WAIT FOR YOU. HE'S BY THE STONE.*

*THE TWO O'CLOCK DROPPED TO SIX HUNDRED THE NIGHT AFTER. THEN IT CAME BACK.*

*LINGYUE SAYS HOLD ON. SHE SAYS DON'T YOU DARE.*

He went on along the carry road in the dark, inside the warmth of something very old that was learning, one gray thread at a time, to say *later*, and he didn't turn round.

It was the hardest thing he'd ever not done.
