## Chapter 243: The Bell Changes

On the second night the Collector came back.

They hadn't known whether it would. Xiaoyu went to the Awakening Hall at one in the morning anyway, because she'd said tomorrow and the Collector had said tomorrow, and if somebody was going to break a promise it wasn't going to be her. Her brother went with the staff. Old Seven went because he went. Lingyue went with the flame in her hand, apricot-sized and steadier every hour. Gao Min let them in at the side door with her ring of keys and didn't ask why, and sat down on the bottom tier with her thermos and her notebook, because somebody from the Bureau should be counting this too.

The three lines of light still hung over the platform. They hadn't faded. Gao Min said they hadn't faded by a hair; she'd measured them with a lux meter at noon and again at six, and she showed them the figures, which were identical, as if this settled something.

Xiaoyu climbed the steps and stood before the warm stone with the blue jacket over its shoulder.

"Hello," she said. "It's me. It's tomorrow."

> ***...I know,*** said Wen Jia from the knot. ***...it's here. It's been here since midnight, waiting. It's very polite.***

---

> *Hello, child.*

Warm, patient, a cup set down so carefully it made no sound. It came into the hall as it had the night before, out of the deep water along a thread finer than a hair, and rested on his sister like an old person who has come back to visit as promised and sat down in the same chair.

Xiaoyu didn't wait to be asked anything.

"What do you want?"

The attention was quiet a while.

> *I looked. All day. Your day. I do not count as your machine counts, but I know it was a day, because the girl in the stone said so.*
>
> *I went to the place where a want should be. In me. I have never been there. I did not know there was a there.*
>
> *There is. It is very old. Older than the gardens. Older than the stones.*
>
> *It is empty.*

Xiaoyu held the capped marker and said nothing.

> *I do not know whether it was always empty, or whether something was in it once and I kept it, as I keep everything, and forgot that it was mine.*
>
> *I am going to look further. Will you ask me again tomorrow?*

"Yes," said Xiaoyu.

Yuanshu went rigid, and so did Lingyue. That was an answer, and the Collector kept answers.

But nothing happened. The attention rested on her a moment longer, warm and patient, and took nothing.

> **It asked whether she'd ask it,** said Seed. **She said yes. A yes to asking isn't a want it can keep. It's a want to give. It can't keep a giving. It doesn't know how. Nothing it's ever kept pointed outward.**
>
> **I think she knew that. I don't know how.**

> *...thank you,* said the Collector. *Tomorrow, then.*

It went.

Xiaoyu uncapped the marker, came down, went to the end of the bottom tier where Gao Min sat, and, because there was no wall in the hall and she had to write it somewhere, wrote on the back of the acting director's hand:

*NIGHT 2. LOOKED. FOUND A PLACE. EMPTY.*

Gao Min looked at her hand a long time. Then she put her glasses on and copied it carefully into the notebook, and didn't wash that hand for three days.

---

Ren rang at a quarter to three.

He was walking back along East Ring Road in the warm dark when the phone at his belt buzzed, and he knew before he answered, as he'd known from Kwan's face on the night of the three gray rings. Ren didn't ring at a quarter to three. Ren sent messages through Concordia at civilized hours and signed them with her initial.

"The bell," said Shirasagi Ren, calm as always. Behind her voice he could hear wind, and a great many people breathing in the dark: the terraces below the shrine, three thousand monks and hollows and the Quiet, listening as they listened every night. "It's changed. Sister Kiyo is here. I'm putting her on."

A pause. Then an old woman's voice in the old coast Yamato, very fast, and Seed a little behind her:

> **"It isn't the boat call. It isn't the name. It's the fog warning. In Shiomi, when the fog came in so thick the boats couldn't see each other, the shore rang a warning. Not to the boats coming home: to the ones still out. Long, short, long, again and again. It meant stop rowing. Be quiet. Something is close to you in the fog, and it's bigger than you.**
>
> **They're ringing it now. To us."**

---

Seed played it in the banking hall at four in the morning, the recording Ren's monks had taken off the night air, for everyone who came down the stairs, which was everyone.

The long low tone first, as always. The wait. Then not the small bells saying *Shiomi*, not the five quick notes of Ichiro's boat. Long, short, long. Long, short, long. Over and over, steadily, rung by someone who's been told to keep ringing till somebody comes.

Then after nine of them, in the fog-code of a fishing village a thousand years gone, slowly, with Sister Kiyo translating each word down the phone line:

> **SOMETHING WALKED PAST US.**
>
> **ON A THREAD. IN THE DARK BETWEEN THE ARMS. TOWARD THE COLD FIRE.**
>
> **IT DID NOT SEE US. WE WERE QUIET. WE HAVE BEEN QUIET A THOUSAND YEARS. WE ARE VERY GOOD AT IT.**
>
> **IT IS BIGGER THAN ANYTHING. IT HAS MANY HANDS.**

Fat Tan had his hand over his mouth.

> **THE SIXTY SAW IT. IT WALKED THROUGH THE COLD FIRE. IT DID NOT STOP. IT DID NOT LOOK DOWN. THE SIXTY STOOD FACING OUT AND IT WENT OVER THEM.**
>
> **THE SERGEANT ASKED US TO RING THIS. HE SAYS HE CANNOT RING. HE SAYS WE HAVE A BELL AND HE HAS A PICK.**

At the bronze door, Old Seven turned round.

> **THE SERGEANT SAYS: TELL THE BOY IT IS COMING ALONG THE ARM.**
>
> **THE SERGEANT SAYS: TELL HIM IT IS CARRYING SOMETHING. A MAN. ON THE END OF A THREAD, IN ONE OF ITS HANDS. BURNING. HELD OUT IN FRONT OF IT LIKE A LANTERN.**
>
> **THE SERGEANT SAYS: TELL HIM TO HOLD.**

Then long, short, long, until the recording ran out.

---

Nobody spoke for a long while. Then Kwan said quietly: "Callum Ward."

> **I think so,** said Seed. **The Port Halvard Stele opened him all the way and sent him up the thread. Not the mark. Him. The first time a stone's ever sent a whole mind.**
>
> **The thing at the other end took him. And it's carrying him out in front of it, burning, like a lantern.**
>
> **I think it's using him to see.**

On the stairs Lingyue closed her hand over her flame.

"To see what?"

> **What's wrong. The Collector said it only knows that when the stones are bright it comes, and when they're quiet something's wrong. It doesn't ask. It never learned.**
>
> **So it's coming to look. And it hasn't got eyes for quiet, only for bright. So it's carrying something bright to see by.**

---

Seed had the numbers by morning. It worked them as it worked everything now, out loud in the banking hall: what it knew, then what it guessed, then the gap. Kwan drew lines on the laptop. Gao Min checked the arithmetic in pencil, because she didn't trust anything she hadn't done by hand.

> **What I know. Shiomi's bell takes four hundred and twelve seconds to reach us, and the interval's been getting shorter as they come nearer, so I know roughly where they are: past the end of the arm, in the dark between arms, a long way out.**
>
> **They say it walked past them. Then the Sixty saw it walk through the cold fire. The cold fire is thirty-nine raft-days from here. The Sixty's message went back to Shiomi, and Shiomi rang it to us.**
>
> **What I guess. From how long all that took, it passed the cold fire about nine days ago.**
>
> **And the threads.**

Kwan turned the laptop round: eleven pale lines rising off the diagram, taut, jerking together every few seconds.

> **The pulling's getting stronger. Steadily. Like a rope getting heavier as somebody climbs it, the nearer they get to the top.**
>
> **If it walks at the speed the pulling says, it'll be here in about sixty days. I'm not sure. I've never measured anything walking on a thread before. But it's coming along the arm. It went past the tree. It went past the cold fire. It's coming here.**

"Why here?" said Fat Tan. It was the first thing he'd said all night. "There are other stones. Other orchards. The Collector said *all the orchards*. Why us?"

> **Because ours went quiet. All eleven at once. It's never felt that before, anywhere.**
>
> **A gardener goes to the tree that's stopped fruiting.**

---

At nine in the morning Gao Min's phone rang.

She looked at the screen and didn't answer at once, the way you look at a number you've been expecting and hoping not to see. Then she answered and listened and said "How much?" and listened again and wrote a figure in her notebook.

She hung up.

"Port Halvard. The Bureau office there measures the Stele every morning since the hammer. Height, lean, temperature. It's protocol; I wrote the protocol." She looked at the figure. "It's taller."

Nobody understood.

"Four centimeters. Overnight. It's four centimeters taller than yesterday." She turned the notebook round so they could see. "Stones don't grow. It isn't growing. The floor round the base has cracked, hairline, all the way round." She shut the notebook. "It's being pulled out of the ground."

From Port Abiri, on the radio at Kwan's belt, the old man who liked the sea:

> ***...yes. I can feel it. Ours too. A little.***
>
> ***...it's pulling the threads, and the threads are tied to the stones, and the stones are in the ground.***
>
> ***...we're holding the knots. We're not letting anything up. But we can't hold the stone down. We're in it. You can't hold down a thing you're sitting in.***

> **It's pulling up the stakes,** said Seed. **It can't see through the quiet, so it's pulling the stones up to look at them.**
>
> **With the eleven still inside.**

Three kilometers away on East Ring Road, a bright blue jacket lay over the shoulder of a warm black stone. When Gao Min rang the hall staff at ten, they told her the floor round its base had a crack in it.

Hairline. All the way round.
