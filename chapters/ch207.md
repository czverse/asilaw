## Chapter 207: The Eyes of the Mouth

He held the door open for nineteen hours, and for most of them he had no idea where he was.

It began at the end of the twelfth hour, when the Sixth walked out of the quiet part together, through the curtain, past the line of the Third Battalion and off into the dark toward the seam where the two rings had fused. Toward the eyes.

He didn't watch them go. He was already sitting cross-legged on the gold-veined floor with the staff across his knees and his eyes shut, going down: through the Death Law, all thirty-nine point two percent of it, to the cool dark door at the bottom where finished things went.

He opened it wider than he had ever opened it. Wider than Tannery Square or the gleaners, wider than the four thousand at the Western Pass or Major Zhuo's column in the field. He opened it the way you'd fling open every door in a building that was on fire.

And ninety-three parts in a hundred of the walls of the Mouth started to come home.

---

He wouldn't remember most of it. Afterward it came back in pieces, the way a high fever does.

The Field around him was a slope, steeper than he had ever made it, running downhill from every black vein of a ring a thousand kilometers across to the one small warm room where he sat. Down that slope, minds. Not a stream; a river in spate. Ten million years of the Mouth's meals, asked nine by nine and answering *rest*, coming loose from the veins like frost letting go of a windowpane when the sun hits it. Into him. Through him. Out through the door. Gone.

Each of them said something as it went.

Not to him, he thought. To the room, to whatever was nearest, the way people say something to nobody in particular on their way out. Most of it was in languages that had been dead since before Blue Star had a sun. Seed tried to catch all of it and couldn't, and later it gave him the scraps it had managed to keep.

> **Thank you.**
>
> **Thank you for asking.**
>
> **Is it over?**
>
> **I had a daughter.**
>
> **Where are we going?**
>
> **It's quiet. Oh. It's quiet.**

---

> **Hour four,** Seed told him later. **You were bleeding from the nose and the ears. Bai Lu was with you. Lingyue had your hand. You didn't know.**
>
> **The keeper's threads had come down into the quiet part by then, hundreds of them, gold, through the walls. The cousins were going up them. Two hundred and six million, and the six parts of the walls that wanted to find something out. Up the threads, out of the Mouth, toward the field.**
>
> **The keeper was crying. I didn't know it could. It said nobody had ever come to it because they wanted to.**
>
> **And then the eyes.**

He felt them light even through the flood, even with his own eyes shut and blood running out of them. Behind him in the dark, at the seam of the two rings, two dead furnaces that had been cold for eleven days caught.

Gold. Then white. Then a color past white that his brain had no slot for.

He had seen those eyes once, filling the sky over Lingzhou, and they'd been the most frightening thing he had ever looked at. They weren't frightening now. They were the Sixth.

> **They're burning high,** said Seed. **All of them: the Sixth, the seven million cousins who stayed, the one part in a hundred of the walls.**
>
> **I thought they'd scream. Burning high is what the first fire did to them. They went in screaming.**
>
> **They're singing. The same low note they hummed in the quiet part to keep the cousins warm.**
>
> **It's so loud. Every keeper in the Shallows can hear it. Every field, every world.**
>
> **The reader on the far side has stopped reading inward. It's turned round.**

---

Somewhere around the eighth hour, or the ninth, he knew when the reader got there.

He knew it the way he'd known on the island, watching a gold light across the dark go out from one edge like paper catching. Something very large and very patient arriving beside the eyes. Looking. Starting to read.

> **It's reading the Sixth,** said Seed. **They're letting it. They aren't hiding or burning low. They're burning as high as they can and singing and letting it take them one slice at a time.**
>
> **It's never found anything this bright in a Hunger. It wants all of it.**
>
> **It isn't reading anything else.**

Someone was holding his hand hard enough to grind the bones together and saying his name every few minutes, just once, the way the Quiet said a name. He would find out later it had been Lingyue, for nineteen hours, and that she had refused to let Bai Lu take over even when her own nose started to bleed in sympathy.

---

At the fourteenth hour Huo came through the curtain.

Yuanshu didn't see it. Seed told him. The one-armed sergeant came in from the line where the Third Battalion had stood facing the dark, and stopped in front of Old Seven, who hadn't moved from the curtain in fourteen hours.

> **Huo says: sir. The cousins are nearly all up the threads. The walls are going through the door. The boy can't hold it much longer. Bai Lu says so.**
>
> **Huo says: the island has to go while the thing's looking at the eyes.**
>
> **Huo says: somebody has to stand at the curtain and tell the slowest ones in the walls that the door's still open until the last one's through. Otherwise they get left in the cold with the reader.**
>
> **Huo says the Third Battalion's been standing at the edge of the quiet part for four hundred and forty days so the cold came to them first. They can stand here a bit longer.**

Old Seven didn't answer.

> **Huo says: sir, that wasn't a question. He says he learned that from you.**

---

The door stayed open for nineteen hours.

By the end, the flood was a trickle. Then single minds, very slow and very cold, the last of the walls from the far rim where the cold went deepest, coming down the long slope one at a time like the last stragglers leaving a cinema after the lights come up. Each one saying something at the door.

At the curtain, sixty-one skeletons told each of them, as it passed in the dark, that the door was still open.

The last one through said: *Is there anyone else?*

Huo said: *No. You're the last. Go on.*

It went. He closed the door.

---

He came back to himself on his back on the warm ground of the island, with Bai Lu's hands on his face and Lingyue's hand in his and the brass clock ticking near his ear, and the whole island humming under him, moving fast.

He found out later how he'd got there. Dabao had carried him twenty kilometers back through the crack in the rim with his shield slung on his back, and hadn't stopped once, and when he put him down he'd gone and sat by himself at the edge for an hour and wouldn't talk to anyone, not even Mo Shi.

Yuanshu turned his head.

Behind them, a long way off now and falling further, the cracked stone wheel hung in the dark. In it, two eyes, white and past white, so bright that for a thousand kilometers round the Mouth the dark wasn't dark any more. It glowed like the sky over a city.

Beside the eyes, something he couldn't see, reading.

> **They're still singing,** said Seed. **I can hear them through the keeper. It's read about half.**
>
> **They're still singing.**

At the rim, Old Seven was facing backward. In all the islands and rafts and roads Yuanshu had seen him on, he had never once faced backward. In his palm Hush had uncurled and stood up on its thin legs, every one of its dark eyes open, looking at the two lights.

It held up the queen of hearts toward them, and kept it there.

> **Huo isn't on the island,** said Seed. **The Third Battalion isn't on the island. They were at the curtain telling the last of the walls that the door was open when the island had to leave. They knew it would.**
>
> **Sixty of them. Not sixty-one.**
>
> **Huo sent one back through the crack with Dabao while Dabao was carrying you. He didn't ask him. He told him. He said: you're the only one of us who knows where he wants to go now. Go there.**

A little way along the rim from Old Seven, a short skeleton of the Third Battalion stood with his weight on his good leg, facing backward too, toward the eyes.

> **He says the sergeant told him to pass something on.**
>
> **He says: Shiomi said, wait for us at the cold fire.**
>
> **He says: somebody should.**

Yuanshu lay and watched the two lights get smaller until he couldn't tell if he was still seeing them or only remembering them, and then he cried, and nobody on the island pretended not to notice, and nobody said a word about it either.
