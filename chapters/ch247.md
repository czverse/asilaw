## Chapter 247: Bright

In the morning, on the landing at Shuanghe, Liang Fu asked about the queen.

He asked everything all at once now, Seed said, like a man who's lived alone thirty years in a house and has just had a door open on rooms full of things he forgot he owned, and doesn't know which to pick up first. He'd been talking since dawn: to Zeng Yan, who hadn't slept and sat on the top step in a dry coat with her hands round a cup of tea; to the river; to Old Seven, who stood at the edge of the stones facing out and let him.

He came up the steps with his weight off his right leg, stopped in front of Yuanshu, and looked up.

> **He says: the little one. In the fire. The one with all the legs. He taught it cards, a game with no queens so it couldn't cheat. It cheated anyway. It kept one back, the queen of hearts. It thought he didn't see.**
>
> **He wants to know if it still has it.**

"It gave it to my sister," said Yuanshu. "It's on the wall, on a pin, at the end of the INSIDE column. Under your name."

The short dead man stood on the landing a moment with the two rivers going by behind him.

> **He says good. It was never his anyway. He took all the queens out so it couldn't lose, and it took one back so it could.**
>
> **He says that's how you know someone's getting better. They want to be able to lose.**

He turned and went back down to the ferry, where a man from the village waited at the post with his hat in his hands, and untied the rope.

> **He says there's a sad one. Excuse him.**

---

They drove north at nine: Kwan, the clutch, both hands. Xiaoyu asleep against the window. Lingyue in the back with her hand open on her knee and the lamp-sized flame in it, not looking at it.

Yuanshu didn't sleep. He sat in front with the staff and the radio and listened to Sterling.

"Holders, fourteen thousand," said the King of Force from Port Halvard at ten. "The stone's stopped. Second day. Net zero." A pause. "Seed's told you what the Collector said."

"Yes."

"Then you know what I'm going to ask."

"Yes."

"Do we tell them?" said Roman Sterling. "Fourteen thousand people holding a stone down so the thing can't pull it up, and the thing's coming down the thread *for* them, because they're holding." His voice didn't change. "Tell them, and half of them go home. The stone's out of the ground in nine days and the docker in it goes up the thread with it. Don't tell them—"

He didn't finish.

"Article Eleven," said Yuanshu.

"I know what Article Eleven says."

"Then you know the answer."

Static. Wind off the Atlantic.

"Yes," said Sterling. "I just wanted somebody else to say it first."

---

They told them at noon, all eleven halls, on the open channel. Gao Min read it in Lingzhou at the foot of the platform, in front of four thousand people with their hands on the stone and a queue going out the side door into the sun. Sterling read it in Port Halvard standing on a fish crate at the harbor end of a line of fourteen thousand that went round the dock and up past the fish market onto the coast road.

The same words in both places. Seed had written them.

*The thing coming down the thread sees only what burns. The stones open a little holding in each of you. A little holding is a little bright. To it, you are the brightest thing on Blue Star. It is coming to the stone at Port Halvard first, in four days. It is coming for you. You can let go. Nobody will think less of you. The stone will rise, and we will try to find another way. Or you can stay. We are telling you before you decide.*

Yuanshu heard it go out on the vault steps of the Ossuary, and heard the silence after it: eleven halls, tens of thousands of people with their hands on black stones, finding out they were bait.

Nobody spoke on the channel for a long time.

Then from Port Halvard, through Sterling's handset, a woman's voice. Not young. A coastal accent, flat and hard, the voice of someone who's shouted over ship engines all her life.

"It took my son," said Callum Ward's mother. "It's got him out there burning. He's the lantern. The boy with the thing in his head went up and talked to him." A pause. "If it's coming down here looking for bright, it's coming to where my son came from. I'm not letting go. I'd like it to see my face."

On the channel, in eleven halls, nobody let go.

---

> **Some did,** said Seed. **I'm counting. Gao Min's counting. The one in the eleventh stone who counts the no's is counting.**
>
> **In Lingzhou two hundred and nine let go in the first hour. In Port Halvard, eight hundred and forty.**
>
> **And in the second hour, in Port Halvard, two thousand and six more came.**

He looked at the radio.

> **They heard the woman on the channel. They came to stand where she is. Port Halvard: fifteen thousand one hundred and seventy holders.**
>
> **I don't know what that does to how bright it looks. I think it makes it very bright. I think that's what they want.**

---

At four in the afternoon he went to the bronze door.

Old Seven was back from Shuanghe, facing out into East Market Road. The street had been full since Awakening Day: holders coming and going from their shifts on East Ring Road, the dumpling cart, the Number Nine going by every forty minutes with *TO THE HALL. FREE.* in the window. Wen Hao had added the Ossuary to his route, and stopped outside it, and counted to ten.

Yuanshu stood beside the tall dead captain in the doorway and looked out.

"I'm going to Port Halvard."

Old Seven didn't turn.

> **He says yes. He says that wasn't a question.**

"I'll fly. Two days if I don't stop. I'll need the dead." He was quiet. "Article Seven says the dead may defend. It doesn't say where. The nineteen thousand on the voyage said crossing into the Sea was crossing a border, and they wouldn't. This is crossing an ocean into someone else's country."

> **He says: ask them. And ask the man whose country it is.**

---

Sterling said yes in four seconds.

"Bring everyone who'll come. I'll get the Federation Council to sign it tonight. If they won't, I'll sign it and they can fine me." A pause. "That's twice I've decided for something this week. Put it on the wall. I'd like your sister to write it in red."

The dead took a day.

The question went down the net nine by nine, round and round, slow as everything went now. Seed wrote it the way Article Eleven said: *Something is coming down a thread to a stone on the Atlantic coast, in another country, to take the living who are holding it down. They asked to hold it. They were told what is coming and they stayed. The King of that country asks you to come and stand with them. You can say no. It is across an ocean. It is not your border. It is defense, but it is someone else's.*

It came back as the question about the top had come back: not as a list of names but as a pile of index cards on the long table, and on top, in Kwan's careful hand, the card that had gone round the net six hundred times and had a word added by every knot it passed through.

Kwan read it out slowly, cracking sunflower seeds between sentences.

"It started with the Spear Corps. Stone No. 7. They said: the woman's son was an F. We were spears. Nobody asked us either. Yes." She turned the card over. "Then everyone added. The First Legion: yes, a wall is a wall. The cemetery companies: yes. Grid Nine: yes, tell the boy we've never seen the sea. The Western Legion: Sergeant Ma says the pass will hold without us for a week; the pass held thirty years without anybody. Yes. And the Watch of the Shelf." She stopped.

"What did the Watch say?"

"Nana Ampofo says: we live at the bottom of the sea. It's the same sea. It goes all the way round. We'll walk."

---

Nineteen thousand said yes, most of them from the First Legion, the Spear Corps, the cemetery companies and Grid Nine. The Western Legion sent four hundred, because Sergeant Ma said that was what the pass could spare and he wouldn't send a man the pass needed. The Watch of the Shelf sent nobody across land. They went down into the sea off Kesse, eleven hundred and forty-six of them, Nana Ampofo at their head with water streaming off her coat, and began to walk along the bottom of the ocean toward the Atlantic coast in the dark.

The keeper laid the road.

It came down the gold thread at three in the morning in its rough voice, a third smaller, and said it had laid a road to Kirovsk once without asking, because there'd been no time, and had been sorry, and would like to lay this one properly. It asked the dead whether they wanted a road. It asked Sterling whether a road could come down in his country. It asked Yuanshu whether he'd walk on it or fly.

"Fly," said Yuanshu. "Beside it."

> ***...THEN I WILL LAY IT SO YOU CAN SEE IT. ALL THE WAY.***
>
> ***...SO YOU ARE NOT FLYING ALONE IN THE DARK.***

---

They left at dawn on the second day after Shuanghe.

From the north wall a gold road climbed out of the War Memorial Cemetery into the sky on a long slant east, toward the sunrise and the sea and a coast eleven thousand kilometers away. Up it in rows of nine, rusted weapons on their shoulders, went nineteen thousand dead of the First Legion and the Spear Corps and the cemetery companies, and forty of Grid Nine who'd never seen the sea.

He flew beside it.

He'd learned to fly sideways on the sixty-third day of the long count, when Lingyue told him that a Lord decides where down is and falls there. He decided down was east, and fell. Beside him, deciding the same, flew his wife, her grandfather's sword on her back, the lamp-sized flame in her hand, her hair loose in the wind because the red ribbon was gone. Behind them Qin Hao, falling east with his notebook buttoned into his coat and his hands open, white fire banked in them.

At the head of the gold road, walking where he always walked, two meters ten, spear grounded in a finger and a thumb: Old Seven.

Xiaoyu didn't come. Somebody had to ask the Collector, she said, and somebody had to write it down. She stood on the north wall in her school jacket and watched them go, and when they were small she uncapped the marker and wrote on the parapet, in square black capitals that no rain would take now, because nothing she wrote came off:

*PORT HALVARD. THE DEAD WHO SAID YES, AND THREE LIVING. THEY WERE ASKED.*

> **Three days,** said Seed. **And Yuanshu, it's morning. I said I'd tell you every morning. And you said you'd ask Callum Ward every day.**

*I know.*

He flew east over the sea with the gold road beside him and the sun coming up, and shut his eyes for a moment and went up the short way: not near, through the docker's knot in the Port Halvard stone and along the thread to the edge of the light, where a man burned in a vast hand in the deep water. Close enough to be heard, not close enough to be seen.

*Callum. It's me. It's tomorrow.*

*...yeah,* said the voice at the bottom of the well, small under the roar. *...still don't know.*

*That's all right.*

*...is Mum still holding?*

*Yes. She's at the front. Fifteen thousand people came to stand where she is.*

The fire roared.

*...that's Mum,* said Callum Ward. *...ask me again tomorrow.*
