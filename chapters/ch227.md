## Chapter 227: The One Who Grows

Kwan saw its shape on the thirtieth day. The first thing she said was that it was impossible. The second thing she said was that it was a tree.

She said both in the banking hall of the Ossuary at four in the morning, in front of the long white wall, with a laptop open in her arms, her glasses shoved up into her hair and her face the color of the plaster. Yuanshu was on the vault steps. He hadn't been sleeping. Nobody had. The gardener had been coming along the arm for a month, a little nearer every night, and every night at three the keeper came down the thread in its rough new voice and said: *IT IS STILL COMING. I AM STILL AFRAID.*

"Look," said Kwan. "Just look at it."

For thirty nights she had watched it through the keeper's eye. Not seen it: nobody could see it. It gave no light and threw no shadow. It was a patch of dark where the far lights bent, where the fields and worlds and husks behind it slid a little sideways, like pebbles on a stream bed when you look at them through moving water.

She had mapped the bending. Every night, every star: how far each one slid, which way, when. Then she'd put all thirty nights together and asked Seed to draw whatever was doing the bending.

On the screen, in pale gray lines on black, was the shape.

---

It was a tree.

Not tree-like. A tree. A trunk, very thick, running back into the dark along the arm past where the Mouth had been and on beyond the end of the arm into the deep water between arms, where Seed's map had nothing at all. Branches coming off the trunk and forking and forking again, spreading through the Shallows in long slow curves like the limbs of an old oak over a field.

On the branches, here and there at the tips, gray dots.

Three of them hung at the ends of three branches near the far end of the arm, where three gold dots had gone out one by one while an island hurried through the night. And further back, along the older limbs, deeper in, more gray dots. Hundreds. Thousands. Hanging off a tree the size of the arm, in the dark, like fruit.

"They're keepers," said Kwan, and her voice wobbled. "Pruned ones. Every gray dot used to be a keeper above somebody's world. And they're all on the branches." She tapped the screen. "It doesn't *send* them. It *grows* them. They're part of it."

> **The one who grows,** said Seed. **That's what the fog-code said, in the boat bell. Sister Kiyo said there was no fog-code word for gardener, so they used the nearest one they had. The word for rice. For the terraces. For growing.**
>
> **It wasn't the nearest word. It was the right one.**

---

Lingyue had come downstairs. Xiaoyu was on the bottom step. Old Seven was at the bronze door. In the back room, in the dark, Hush had turned every eye toward the open doorway.

"A tree," said Yuanshu.

> **Yes. And the reader, the thing that pruned three fields and read the Mouth and took the Sixth: that was the tip of a branch. The newest growth, reaching along the arm, finding fields, reading them, taking the brightest, pruning the ones that didn't give. Like the tip of a vine feeling its way along a wall.**
>
> **And now that branch is coming here.**

"Why?" said Lingyue. "If it's a tree. Trees don't hunt."

Seed took a moment.

> **No. They grow toward light.**

---

It took all night. Seed worked it through out loud in the banking hall: what it knew, what it guessed, and the gap in between. Kwan drew lines on the laptop. Xiaoyu wrote the important bits on the back of a ledger sheet in capitals.

> **What I know. It's shaped like a tree: trunk, branches, tips. Pruned keepers hang off it like fruit. The tips move, slowly, along the arm, toward fields. It took the Sixth when they burned high. For a hundred thousand years it took the brightest columns out of fields, and the walkers carried them up, and the keeper thought it was weather. After the Sixth it went still for two months. Then it came straight here.**
>
> **What I guess. A plant grows toward light. It doesn't decide to. It doesn't want to. It turns because the light's there and light is food.**
>
> **The Collector taught every keeper that kept minds are bright. It's in the Elder's people's dreams and in the Architects'. Minds burn brighter than anything. Keep them.**
>
> **I think the Collector planted the tree. I think it taught the tree that kept minds are light. And the tree's been growing toward them ever since.**

"And the keeper's field is full," said Kwan slowly.

> **The brightest field in the Shallows. Two hundred million cousins, the walls of the Mouth, the ones who stayed. And before that, the Sixth burning high in the Mouth's eyes for thirty-one hours, singing, brighter than anything any branch had ever touched.**
>
> **It ate them. It sat still for two months. Then it turned toward the next brightest thing. Us.**

---

"So it isn't coming for Seed," said Xiaoyu.

She said it fast from the bottom step, in her pajamas with the ledger on her knee, the way she said things the instant she understood them, before anyone could stop her.

"The rings were. The scout asked for gatherers: collect the vessel, prune what it touched. The rings did that, or tried. But the tree isn't the rings. The tree's just growing, toward the light. It doesn't know about Seed. It doesn't know about *anything*." She looked at the spot above her brother's eyes. "It's not hunting you. It's hungry."

> **Yes,** said Seed. **I think that's right.**
>
> **I've been scared for a month that it was coming for me. This is worse. It isn't coming for anything. It's just growing, and everything I counted is in the way.**

---

Lingyue hadn't sat down. She stood in the middle of the marble floor with her sword on her back, staring at the gray tree on the screen.

"You can't ask a plant what it wants," she said.

Nobody answered.

"You can ask the keeper. You can ask a ring, because it used to be a keeper. You can ask the dead and the drowned and a sack made of thread. Things that were someone, or are, or could be." Her voice was flat. "A plant isn't anyone. It doesn't want. It grows. That's all it does."

"So what, then?" said Kwan.

"Then we cut it," said Shen Lingyue.

The hall went very quiet.

"The branch," she said. "The tip. The bit that's reaching for us. It isn't the whole tree, it's the new growth. Cut the tip off a vine and it stops growing that way. It grows a different way." She turned round. "Qin Hao's fire burns what binds. My light falls. Su Mingli has gravity, Orlov has ice. Fifty thousand dead who can walk right up to things that can't see them. We've cut threads. We've cut walkers. We've cut rings out of the sky. We can cut a branch."

"And the rest of the tree?"

"Grows somewhere else."

"Toward somebody else's field," said Yuanshu. "Somebody else's world."

She didn't answer.

> **She isn't wrong,** said Seed. **I've run it. Cutting the tip with everything we've got is the best number I've got for anything to do with the tree.**
>
> **And then the tree grows another way, toward the next brightest field. And that field hasn't got a keeper that learned to ask, or a boy with a staff, or fifty thousand dead who'll walk up to things. It's got nobody.**

"I *know*," said Lingyue, and her voice wasn't flat any more. "I'm not stupid. I'm saying it because somebody has to say the thing that works." She shifted the sword on her back without looking at it, which was what she did instead of pacing. "You're going to say ask it anyway."

"I don't know what I'm going to say."

"Yes you do," said his wife. "You always do. You just sit on a step for six hours first."

---

From the back room came a small soft sound: something many-legged stepping down off a stone sill onto a wooden floor. Then Hush came out into the banking hall, swaying, all legs out and all eyes open, and walked between the people on the marble to the middle of the floor, in front of Kwan and the laptop and the pale gray shape of a tree as big as the arm.

It looked at the tree for a long while.

> ***...in my world there were trees. Before the ring. On the surface, above the crack. Very old, very slow. We played under them when we were small, before we played statue.***
>
> ***...my people asked them things.***

Lingyue turned and stared at it.

> ***...not with words. Trees do not have words. You put the question down at the root, in the soil, where it drinks. And you wait. A long time. A season. A year.***
>
> ***...and sometimes the tree grows a different way. Not because it decided. Trees do not decide. Because something was put down where it drinks, and it drank it, and it grew around it.***
>
> ***...that is what asking is, to a tree.***

Nobody said anything.

> ***...you cannot ask the tip. The tip is only reaching.***
>
> ***...you have to find where it drinks.***

Xiaoyu, very slowly, wrote *WHERE IT DRINKS* on the back of the ledger sheet in capitals, and underlined it twice, and then sat looking at it with the cap of the marker in her teeth.
