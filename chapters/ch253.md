## Chapter 253: Up

Hush tied the knot.

Nobody asked it to. On the last afternoon it came down off the windowsill in the back room, slowly, swaying, every limb out, and crossed the marble to the long table, where Xiaoyu sat with a single silver-gray strand of thread on a sheet of white paper, a marker, and no idea what to do next.

The strand came from Fat Tan's box: the walker-thread, the silk the six had left on the north wall when the First Legion struck, which he'd swept up two years ago and wouldn't throw away and kept in the vault beside the queens of hearts. Hush had taken two strands from it once, for two rings. Xiaoyu had taken one more that morning, for the mark.

"I can write on it," she said. "I tried. It stays. But it's just a thread with words on. It isn't a mark. A mark has to go up and be read. Seed says it has to be tied."

Hush looked at the strand a long time.

> ***...it is a question-knot, but backward.***
>
> ***...a question-knot is a thing held out and let go of, so what is asked can answer or not. A mark is a thing held up, so what looks at it sees.***
>
> ***...in my world, before the ring, we tied them for the trees. When a tree was too old to answer and we needed it to know something, we tied what it needed to know and hung it on a branch where the tree would grow round it.***
>
> ***...I can tie it.***

Xiaoyu looked at the small pale thing beside her elbow, gold dust still on its thin legs from the cut.

"Will you write it with me?"

> ***...yes.***

She wrote along the silver-gray strand in tiny square capitals, as you'd write along the edge of a ruler: *THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU CAN STOP.* And Hush, with four thin pale limbs, slowly, while she held the ends, tied it. Not as a person ties a knot; as water ties itself round a stone. When it was done a small gray knot the size of a fingernail lay on the paper with the words gone inside it, and when Seed looked at it through Yuanshu's eyes it said:

> **It's true. I can't read it; it's tied. But I can feel that it's true, the way I can feel the wall.**

Hush sat back on its legs.

> ***...I moved first once. At the cut. That was enough.***
>
> ***...I will not come up the thread. I will sit in the sun on the windowsill and wait. That is also a thing someone has to do.***

---

That evening Xiaoyu wrote the other mark on the floor of the Awakening Hall, in front of the warm stone with the blue jacket on its shoulder, in letters as tall as her arm: *THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU CAN STOP.* And stood back.

> ***...ready?*** said Wen Jia from the knot.

"Ready."

> ***...I've never sent one. Not like this. Not one that's true. Here goes.***

The runes on the black face, which had pointed down into the stone since the night before Awakening Day, turned. All of them. Up. And the letters on the floor went faint, not gone, faint, as if something had read them all at once and carried what it read up the fine thread out of the crown into the dark.

On Kwan's laptop eleven pale lines jumped together, once. A glance.

And went still.

> **It read it,** said Seed. **It glanced and moved on, the way it reads everything. Wen Jia was right. A glance isn't enough.**

"Then we take it the rest of the way," said Yuanshu.

---

They went up at midnight.

The hall was full again, not because the stones were rising but because people had heard. Fifteen hundred of them in a line up the aisle and out the side door and along the pavement of East Ring Road under the trees, hands on one another's shoulders, and at the front, with his hand on the warm black stone, Wen Hao. He'd parked the Number Nine at the curb at eleven and switched off the engine and come in and asked to be at the front, and nobody had argued.

Seed had said nine hours, not one minute more. A Lord's body could lie empty nine hours with fifteen hundred people anchoring it through the stone, the stone through the floor, the floor through the earth. After that the anchor would hold, but there'd be nothing left in the body to hold.

He lay down on the platform with his back against the side of the stone, under the blue jacket. Lingyue knelt beside him with her hand flat on his chest, the sword on the floor beside her and the flame in her other palm. Qin Hao knelt on his other side, white fire banked in his open hands. Su Mingli at the corner of the platform with her arms folded. Gao Min on the bottom tier with her notebook and an old mechanical stopwatch from the Bureau, because she didn't trust the phone. Sister Ning on her stool with her candle lit. Xiaoyu at the foot of the steps with the marker.

And in his right hand, closed, a small gray knot the size of a fingernail.

Old Seven wasn't at the foot of the steps. He was lying down beside the platform on the polished floor, on his back, spear across his chest, a finger and a thumb closed round it, as the dead lie in the ground. In two years Yuanshu had never once seen him lie down.

> **He says a captain goes out ahead of the line,** said Seed. **But he goes out on his feet, and he's never gone up a thread, and I told him the dead go lighter if they're lying down. So.**
>
> **He says if anybody tells the Spear Corps he lay down, he'll deny it.**

"Gao Min," said Yuanshu.

"Ready," said the acting director, and pressed the stopwatch.

He shut his eyes.

---

Up.

As before: through the staff, across the gap into the warm stone, up through the crown of the tree of Laws onto the thread. A raindrop sliding along a line, fast, out through the roof and the sky over Lingzhou, through the blue into the black.

But not alone.

Behind him and beside him and all round him, rising out of the War Memorial Cemetery and the slope by the river and stone No. 7 and a nameless valley cemetery in the Northern Wastes and the bottom of the sea off Kesse, along the net of the dead nine by nine, up into the eleven stones sideways as the eleven had gone, and up the threads: the dead.

Not bodies. Embers. Cold, dim, ash-gray, as the dead are when they're banked, each a small spark climbing a thread in the dark. Thousands. Ten thousand. Twelve. Like the sparks that go up a chimney from a fire just banked for the night.

The Spear Corps, two hundred and seventy-two, in a close knot of sparks round him, as they stood round stone No. 7. Grid Nine, forty, a little way off, looking at everything. The First Legion in their rows of nine, rows of nine even as sparks, because that was who they were. The Watch of the Shelf, eleven hundred and forty-six, cold and slow, the color of deep water.

And ahead on the thread, leading as he always led, one ember, cold and steady and dim, that Yuanshu would have known among a million.

> **He says: keep up, boy.**

---

The docker's rough hand caught his on the way past, as before. The old man who liked the sea said *there you are*. From the bottom, briskly, Wen Jia: *don't let go of the knot. Either knot.* Then the keeper's field went by, gold and vast and warm and a third smaller, and from it as they passed, rough and close as a hearth:

> ***...GO CAREFULLY.***
>
> ***...COME BACK.***
>
> ***...I WILL ASK YOU WHAT YOU WANT WHEN YOU DO.***

Then the branch, a darkness where the far lights bent, with its small gold bud at the end. It turned as they went past. He was sure of it this time: a very small turning, as a bud on a branch turns toward something going by.

> **Twelve minutes,** said Seed. **Eight hours forty-eight to go.**

Along the arm. Past the place where the third field had been pruned and gone dark from one side like a page burning. Past where the Mouth had been.

And below the thread, in the dark, very cold, a great stillness, with at its center a small line of embers standing and facing out.

---

The cold fire.

He'd been there once before, in his body, on an island that shrank every night. He'd stood at the edge of the quiet part in front of a curtain and held a door open nineteen hours for the walls of a Hunger, and the Sixth had burned thirty-one hours in the eyes of the Mouth, singing, and sixty soldiers of the Third Battalion had stood at the curtain and said *we can stand here a little longer*, and the island had gone home without them.

The Mouth was gone. The cold fire was what was left: a vast cold stillness, the shape of a place where a great deal of burning had been and had stopped. And at its edge, where the curtain had been, sixty embers in a line, facing out.

The thread to the deep water ran right over their heads.

The river of sparks slowed. Not because he told it to; because the ember at the front did. Old Seven stopped on the thread, a cold dim spark in the dark, and turned and looked down.

From the line of sixty one ember rose off the edge of the cold fire, slowly. A short one, square in the shoulder, if a spark can have shoulders, carrying something over that shoulder which, in the shape the dead keep even as embers, was very clearly a pick.

It came up onto the thread and stopped in front of Old Seven.

> **Huo says—**

Seed stopped. Yuanshu felt it stop, as it had on the battlement when an old adjutant said *message delivered*, as it did when a thing was too big to carry all at once and it had to set it down and pick it up again in pieces.

> **Huo says: sir. You lay down.**
>
> **He says he can tell. You're going light, and you only go light lying down. He saw you lie down once, in the Black Tide, on the wall, when the boy who'd lost his squad wouldn't take your hand. You lay down beside him so he wasn't the only one lying down. Nobody else saw. Huo did. He's never told anyone.**

Old Seven said nothing; embers don't. But he didn't move off the thread.

> **Huo says: show me the arm.**

---

He didn't know how a spark could show anything. He watched it happen anyway.

The ember that was Old Seven lifted something. Not an arm, only the shape the dead keep: the shape of an arm. The left. A sergeant's arm put on in a courtyard among potted plants by a boy who didn't know anatomy, with the shape of a hand at the end of it.

One finger and a thumb.

The ember that was Huo looked at it a long while.

> **Huo says you lost two of his fingers. Old Seven says: in the round chamber, both at once.**
>
> **Huo says those were good fingers. He had them forty years. Old Seven says: I know.**

Then something came through the staff and the thread and the place above his eyes that Seed didn't translate, because it wasn't words and didn't need to be. It was the sound, in the shape the dead keep, of a short square sergeant of the Third Battalion who'd stood months at the edge of a cold fire facing out, laughing.

> **Huo says: good. That means you were using them.**

---

Then the sergeant turned to him: the ember with the pick, to the boy on the thread who wasn't an ember but a raindrop on a line, with a small gray knot in his right hand and the warmth of his wife's hand on a chest a very long way down.

He'd said this name every morning, out loud, to nobody, to the room. On a riverbank in spring. On the north wall. In the banking hall. In a hotel in Port Halvard at three in the morning.

He'd never said it to his face.

"Huo Changsheng," said Zhou Yuanshu. "Sergeant. Third Battalion."

He couldn't say the rest. He'd said it every day for months, and now the man was in front of him and he couldn't.

The ember waited.

"You held," he said.

The sergeant stood on the thread in the dark over the cold fire with the shape of a pick on his shoulder and looked at him.

> **Huo says: I know. You told me every day. I heard you.**
>
> **He says you held too. The door, nineteen hours. He was there; he watched you. So you're even.**
>
> **He says: now. Where's this man who wants to go home?**

---

The Sixty came up off the cold fire.

All of them: fifty-nine embers in a line behind their sergeant, up from the edge of the stillness where they'd stood since the island left, onto the thread and into the river of sparks. The Spear Corps made room for them. The First Legion made room. Nobody told anybody to. The Third Battalion of the Eastern Line came into the middle of it behind its captain, where it had always marched.

Below them the cold fire lay empty. For the first time since the Mouth went, nobody stood at its edge facing out.

> **Huo says it's all right. There's nobody left in there to keep the cold off. The last ones went through the door months ago. They were only standing there because nobody told them they could stop.**

He looked down at the empty edge of the cold fire. Then ahead along the thread, past the end of the arm into the deep water between arms where there was no map, where the thread ran on into the dark, still and held, and somewhere at the far end of it something enormous and patient and complicated sat on eleven threads holding a burning man up to its face.

> **Forty-one minutes,** said Seed. **Eight hours nineteen to go.**
>
> **He says: forward.**

They went into the deep water.
