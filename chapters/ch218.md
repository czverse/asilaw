## Chapter 218: How to Tie a Question

Seed tied the first knot wrong, and it was Hush who told it.

They were in the back room of the Ossuary, where the light from the courtyard came in, eleven days before the rings. Yuanshu sat on the floor with his back to the wall and the staff across his knees and his eyes shut, because Seed worked better when he wasn't looking at anything. Hush sat on a folded tea towel on the sunny windowsill, all legs out and all eyes open, which was how it sat now whenever there was sun. Kwan sat at the little table by the window with a laptop she wasn't using, because nothing on it could see what Seed was doing.

Nothing on Blue Star could. Not even Yuanshu, really. He could feel it, the way you can hear someone in the next room working at something fiddly with their hands.

Unpressing.

> **It's very slow,** said Seed. **Walkers pressed this thread into glass a long time ago at a harvest site in the Wastes. It doesn't want to come loose. It was made not to.**
>
> **But a hundred and thirty-eight strands have said: we're giving ourselves. A strand that's given comes loose differently. Like untying a shoelace from the correct end.**
>
> **Twenty-six strands loose so far. Silver, very fine. I can feel the names going out of them as they loosen, like heat out of a cup. I don't know whose. I read them all out to Xiaoyu. They're on the wall.**
>
> **I'm going to tie the first knot.**

---

He felt it tie: a small, tight, complicated shape somewhere behind his eyes, made of twenty-six silver strands that had been the floor of something and were now something else. It reminded him of the scout's request, folded up at the end of a dying shout in the round chamber. It reminded him of the knots behind the walkers' blank faces, where every thread ran to one point.

It had the shape of a question. *WHAT DO YOU WANT.*

> **Done,** said Seed. **The same question you ask everybody, in their language, in their thread. I think it's right.**

On the windowsill, Hush raised its head. Every dark eye turned toward the boy against the wall and the spot just above his eyes.

> ***...it is not right.***

Seed went still.

> **What's wrong with it?**

> ***...it is a good question. They are the right words. But it is tied like a hook.***

Yuanshu opened his eyes. Hush's legs had drawn in a little, the way they did when it was concentrating.

> ***...in my world, before the ring came, we asked each other things all the time. It was what we were. The world that asks.***
>
> ***...there were two ways to ask. In one, you ask and you hold on to the question and you pull until they answer. Like a hook in a fish.***
>
> ***...in the other, you ask and let go. You put the question down between you and you wait. They pick it up when they are ready.***
>
> ***...your knot is the first kind. It holds on. It will go up the ring's thread and catch and pull until the ring answers. And a ring that has never been asked anything will not know how to answer. It will only know something has hooked it. It will do what hooked things do.***

"Fight," said Yuanshu.

> ***...pull back.***

> **It's right,** said Seed, after a moment. **I tied it the way the scout tied its request. The way walkers tie theirs. Every knot I've ever seen holds on. That's what knots are for. I didn't know there was another kind.**

> ***...there is. It is very old. My people used it for four billion years before the ring came, and then for four billion more at the bottom of a crack, playing statue, nobody asking anything, nobody letting anything go.***
>
> ***...I am the last one who remembers how.***

Kwan had given up pretending to use the laptop. "Can you teach Seed?"

Hush looked at the sun on its legs for a while.

> ***...I can show it. But it is not something you tie. It is something you stop doing.***
>
> ***...like the game. Statue. Except the other way round.***

---

It took four days.

Yuanshu spent most of them against the wall of the back room with his eyes shut while a small pale survivor of a world eaten four billion years ago sat on a windowsill and taught a sack made of walker-thread how to ask without holding on.

He couldn't follow most of it. It wasn't in words. It was in the gaps between them. Hush would say one soft lowercase sentence and then nothing for an hour, and Seed would hold perfectly still for the whole hour, then untie the knot and tie it again. Hush would look at it and say nothing. Seed would untie it again.

The first day, Seed tied it eleven times. The second day, thirty. It didn't complain. But at three in the morning on the second night it said, quietly, to him alone:

> **I'm bad at this.**
>
> **I've never been bad at anything made of counting. This isn't made of counting. It's made of not counting. Putting something down and not checking whether anyone picks it up.**
>
> **Everything in me is built to check.**

*You learned to stand in the open,* he thought. *With nothing in front of you.*

> **That was standing still while something looked at me. This is letting go of something and not looking at it. It's harder.**

---

On the third day Lingyue came in at noon with a bowl of noodles, because Bai Lu had told her he hadn't eaten, and stopped in the doorway.

Hush on the windowsill. Yuanshu against the wall. And between them, on the floor in a square of sunlight, a small white rice bowl, empty, that hadn't been there before.

"What's that?"

> ***...how my people taught it to children,*** said Hush. ***You put a bowl down between you and someone else. You do not put anything in it. You do not say anything. You just put it there and wait.***
>
> ***...after a while, the other one puts something in it. Or they do not. Either way, you have asked.***
>
> ***...you do not look in the bowl. That is the hardest part. Children always look in the bowl.***

Lingyue considered the empty bowl.

Then she put the noodles on the table, came over, sat cross-legged on the floor next to the bowl, and didn't look in it.

"Like this?"

> ***...yes. You are very good at it.***
>
> ***...the counting one keeps looking.***

> **I don't keep looking.**

> ***...you have looked eleven times since she sat down.***

> **Twelve.**

Yuanshu ate the noodles, which had gone cold, and decided he had never tasted anything better.

---

On the fourth night Seed tied the knot again, and Hush said nothing, and went on saying nothing, and after an hour Seed asked, very carefully:

> **Is it right?**

> ***...you are asking me if it is right. You are holding on to the question. Waiting for me to answer. Looking in the bowl.***

> **...yes.**

> ***...then I will not tell you.***

Seed went very still.

And then, for the first time in four days, it didn't untie the knot. It left it: a small, complicated shape of silver strands behind his eyes, not pulling, not holding. Just set down, in the place a question goes once you've asked it and let it go.

He felt it happen. It was like hearing someone you love in the next room finally put down something heavy they've been carrying all day.

> **Oh,** said Seed. **I didn't look. I didn't look in the bowl.**

In the dark on the windowsill, Hush lifted one thin leg and held it out toward the boy against the wall.

> ***...like that. That is how you ask something that has never been asked. You put the question down and let it decide whether to pick it up.***
>
> ***...that is what you did for me, in the fire. You did not pull. You came, and stood there, and let us decide.***

---

Seed tied the second knot by dawn and the third by noon, out of the last of the hundred and thirty-eight strands, and didn't look at either of them. Three small silver shapes behind his eyes, put down, waiting.

At the bottom of the well, a hundred and thirty-eight names weren't there any more. He felt the gap. Seed didn't mention it. He didn't ask.

That evening he went into the banking hall and stood in front of the last wall, past SHIOMI, round the corner, where Xiaoyu had written a hundred and thirty-eight names under a heading nobody had ever used before.

*THE FLOOR.*

His mother was in front of it in her wheelchair, reading them under her breath one at a time, the way you read names you've promised to remember for someone who can't. He stood beside her and read along.

> **Six days,** said Seed. **Three knots, tied, put down. I don't know if a ring will pick one up.**
>
> **I'm trying not to look.**

The empty white rice bowl was still on the back-room floor the next morning. Nobody moved it for a week. Fat Tan swept round it.
