## Chapter 262: The Eighth Candle

It talked for three hours before anyone answered it, and Seed listened to every second.

Not to them: to the dark. The folded thing hung in the sky above the keeper's field, low over the end of the world, the size of a building, not moving, and out of it, on every frequency Kwan's instruments could find and several they couldn't, came a voice. Not a person's. A recording. The same sequence over and over, slow and patient and very loud, like a ship's horn in a harbor at night: *I am here. I am here. I am here.*

> **It's a language,** said Seed. **It's teaching itself to me. That's what it's for. It starts with numbers. Then the names of the numbers. Then things that can be counted, and the names of those.**
>
> **It's very well made. Somebody's done this many times. Somebody's knocked on a great many doors.**
>
> **I need three hours. No data, no learning, and I'm getting the data.**

He sat on the vault steps at three in the morning with the staff across his knees and listened to Seed listen. The hall was full; nobody had gone back to bed. Lingyue on the stairs in a blanket with the sword. His mother at the long table. Xiaoyu on the stepladder, not writing. Su Mingli, who'd arrived at half past two with her arms folded. Gao Min, who'd come at a quarter to three with her thermos and a bag of steamed buns from the night market, which she handed round without comment. Kwan with the laptop. Old Seven at the bronze door, facing out, as he faced out when he didn't yet know which way the trouble was coming from.

At ten past six, as the sun came up over the river, Seed said:

> **I've got it. Not all of it. Enough.**
>
> **It's a human language. Not ours. Older. The grammar's built round a word that means *grade*. Every sentence about a person carries the grade the way ours carries the tense.**
>
> **I'd like to answer it. I'd like you to tell me what to say.**

---

They answered it from the north wall, because that was where they answered things.

Not by radio. The folded ship was listening on frequencies the radio didn't have, and Seed said the simplest way to be heard was the way the keeper heard: through the staff, along the gold thread, up into the field and out through the keeper's own eye, which was right there, a few hundred kilometers from the crinkle in the dark. The keeper said yes before it was asked. Something made of kept people was sitting in front of its eye, it said in its rough voice, and it would very much like to know why.

He stood in his old place on the Sector Nine battlement at seven in the morning and said, through Seed and the keeper, in a language he didn't know:

*This is Blue Star. My name is Zhou Yuanshu. We see you. Who are you?*

The answer came back in eleven seconds.

Not the recording. A person, a woman. Seed gave him her voice as it gave him the keeper's: in his head, in his own language, but with the shape of hers still in it. Young. Clear. Very exact. The voice of someone taught from the moment she could speak precisely how she was to be addressed, and never once addressed any other way.

> ***Iselde Varr-Anthos. SSS. Third daughter of the House of Anthos, of the Federation of the Lamplit Worlds. Listener-Captain of the folded ship Eighth Candle.***
>
> ***You have not given your grade.***

He stood on the battlement.

*I don't have one,* he said. *The stone couldn't rate me.*

Four seconds of silence. Seed counted.

> ***...that is not possible.***

*It's in the textbooks.*

---

> ***Unlisted Orchard Four-Four-Seven-One,*** said the Listener-Captain. ***That is what our charts call you. A world in the Shallows. Eleven stones. Ripening. Last marked two hundred and six years ago by a Federation listener who passed at the edge of your arm and noted you and did not stop.***
>
> ***Sixty-one days ago a flare was recorded from your world. Lord-bright. Five minutes. Every listener in the Federation within forty thousand folds noted it.***
>
> ***Six days after that, the Reaper stopped.***
>
> ***We have fought the Reaper ten thousand years. We have lost four hundred and twelve worlds to it. We have never once seen it stop. Not slow. Stop.***
>
> ***Now its threads are still, all of them, from one end of the deep water to the other. Our stones send marks up and nothing comes for them. And the last thread it read before it stopped leads here.***
>
> ***I have been sent to find out what you are.***

He didn't answer at once.

> **She's telling the truth, I think,** said Seed. **The way somebody tells the truth when they think it's a weapon.**
>
> **And Yuanshu, I need to tell you what I can see. Now. Before you say anything else to her.**

---

Seed had been looking at the ship since two in the morning. It told him now, on the battlement, in the place above his eyes, while the Listener-Captain of the Eighth Candle waited.

> **The ship's two hundred and twenty meters long and shaped like a candle, which I think is why it's called that. It's very well made. It folds the dark. I don't understand how yet. I'm learning.**
>
> **The folding's done by the engines. The engines are made of cores. The cores are made of people.**
>
> **Forty-one thousand six hundred of them. Pressed and packed into the walls of the engine, like the miners in the cores at Heishui. Burning. Not bright: low. A steady low burn, like a lamp turned down to last the night.**
>
> **They're awake. I can feel them through the keeper's eye. They're awake and burning and they can't move and nobody's asking them anything.**
>
> **I asked one. Just one, very quietly. I'm sorry. I didn't ask you first. I wanted to know if they could hear.**
>
> **She could hear.**

He stood very still.

*What did she say?*

> **She said: what grade are you. She said it the way you'd say hello.**
>
> **And then she said: are you the inspector? Has it been a hundred years? Can I come out now?**

---

He said nothing for so long that the Listener-Captain spoke again, cool and exact.

> ***You have not answered.***

*There are forty-one thousand six hundred people in your engines,* he said.

Nine seconds of silence this time.

> ***...you can see the cores.***

*Yes.*

> ***...nobody in the Shallows can see the cores. There is nothing in the Shallows that can see the cores.***

*There is now.*

> ***They are not people,*** said Iselde Varr-Anthos, as you'd state a fact about the weather. ***They are cores. They were graded F and E, from the fuel orchards on the inner worlds. They serve a term of one hundred years. Then they are released, and their families are paid, and they are honored at the Fold Festival. It is the oldest law of the Federation. Without fold-cores we could not fold. Without folding, the Reaper would have picked every world we have ten thousand years ago.***
>
> ***It is not cruelty. It is how we survived.***

He stood on the north wall of Lingzhou in the morning sun with the staff in his hand and thought about Callum Ward, twenty-five, an F, who'd climbed in through a window with a sledgehammer because a stone had told him what he was.

About a docker in the Port Halvard stone. About Wen Jia. About a girl in a bus driver's photograph, and a coal-yard man's son with a wooden spear, and a statistician's graph that went up a hair every year.

*F and E,* he thought. *The bottom of the ladder. They burn them.*

> **Yes. That's what the ladder's for. The ones at the top fight the Reaper. The ones at the bottom are fuel for the ships the ones at the top fight in.**
>
> **I think the stones taught them that too. MARK HOW BRIGHT. They were marked ten thousand years, and they learned that the dim ones are for burning.**

---

"Blue Star," he said aloud, on the battlement, and Seed carried it. "The Concordia Charter. No person, living or dead, may be raised, read, harvested, or held without their recorded consent."

The Listener-Captain didn't answer.

"You're in our sky," said Yuanshu. "Above our keeper, who learned to ask a few years ago and let everyone it had kept go home or rest or stay, whichever they wanted. It's the only keeper in the Shallows that asks. And you're sitting in front of its eye with forty-one thousand people in your walls that nobody's ever asked anything."

> ***We are not subject to your charter.***

"No," he said. "But I'm going to tell you what I'm going to do before I do it. That's how we do things here."

He looked up at the northern sky, where if you didn't know where to look there was nothing, and if you did there was a small white ring with a gold glow, a third smaller than it had been, and beside it, tiny, a crinkle in the morning.

"I'm going to ask them," he said. "All of them. What they want. Through the keeper. I won't touch your ship. I won't cut anything. I won't open any doors. I'm just going to ask." He gripped the staff. "And whatever they say, I'll tell you exactly. And then we'll talk."

Thirty-one seconds of silence from the Eighth Candle. Seed counted every one.

> ***...you cannot ask a core,*** said Iselde Varr-Anthos at last, and for the first time there was something in her exact young voice that wasn't exactness. ***It is not done. It has never been done. They cannot answer. They are graded.***

"I've heard that before," said Zhou Yuanshu. "About a keeper. And three rings. And a tree. And eleven stones."

He shut his eyes.

"Seed. Ask them."

---

It took nine minutes.

Not because they were slow, but because there were forty-one thousand six hundred of them, and Seed asked them as it had learned to ask the walls of the Mouth: each asks its nine. Through the keeper's eye, softly, into the engines of a folded ship over the north wall, into the walls where people were packed and pressed and burning low, a question none of them had ever been asked, in their own language, which Seed had learned in three hours from a recording made by people who'd knocked on a great many doors.

*My name is Seed. I am from Blue Star. I am telling you first: whatever you say, I will tell the captain exactly. I will not do anything else without asking you again.*

*What do you want?*

And the cores answered.

Everyone on the north wall felt it a little, as they'd learned to feel the keeper the way you feel weather. Out of the crinkle in the morning, out of the engines of the Eighth Candle, forty-one thousand six hundred voices that had burned low in the dark through a hundred-year term, all at once. Not words at first. The sound a crowd makes when it's stood a very long time in the cold and someone opens a door and nobody believes it.

Then words. Seed translated, and went on translating.

Most were the same.

> **Home. They say home. Forty-one thousand and some. Home. Out. Can I go home? Is it a hundred years? I want to see my mother. I want to see the sky. I want to stop.**
>
> **Some say: ask me again. They don't know. They've never been asked. They don't know how to want something with somebody listening.**
>
> **Some say: I want to be graded again. I want them to see I'm not an F.**
>
> **One says—**

Seed stopped.

> **One says: tell Iselde I'm here.**
>
> **She says: second core array, nineteenth wall, position four thousand and eleven. Tell my sister I'm here. They said I was an E. They said it was an honor. I've been here eleven years. Tell her I'm not angry. Tell her I just want to see her face.**
>
> **She says her name is Maelis Varr-Anthos. The fourth daughter of the House of Anthos. The stone marked her E, so the House gave her to the fold.**

---

He opened his eyes on the north wall of Lingzhou.

Lingyue had come up the stairs beside him without his hearing. She stood with her hand on the parapet and her face white. She'd heard; Seed had let the whole wall hear.

The crinkle in the northern sky didn't move. But at the other end of the keeper's eye, on the bridge of a folded ship made of kept people, there was a silence that went on and on and on.

Seed counted it, and didn't tell him the number.

Then, in a voice that had been taught from the moment it could speak exactly how it was to be addressed, and had never once been addressed any other way:

> ***...she is a core.***
>
> ***She was graded. She was given to the fold. It is an honor. It is the oldest law.***
>
> ***She was given eleven years ago. I was fifteen. I was told she had volunteered.***
>
> ***...I was told she could not hear.***

"She can hear," said Yuanshu. "She's been able to hear the whole time."

The silence came back.

> ***...what did she say?***

He told her exactly, as he'd said he would. *Tell Iselde I'm here. Tell her I'm not angry. Tell her I just want to see her face.*

The Listener-Captain of the Eighth Candle said nothing at all.

And then, out of the crinkle in the sky above the north wall, faint, for the first time, something Seed didn't need to translate, because it's the same in every language anyone has ever spoken:

A girl of twenty-six, SSS, third daughter of a House, in a chair on the bridge of a ship made of people, crying.
