## Chapter 256: Ask Them All

> **Two hours fifty-eight minutes,** said Seed. **I've counted what I can. I can't count them. I can count how long it would take to ask them.**
>
> **It's longer than we've got.**

He stood on the slack thread in the deep water and looked at the cloud.

It went on in every direction: small dim lights burning low, drifting out of the open hands of the stopped thing at the center of the web, slowly, as snow drifts when there's no wind at all. Some were so old they'd almost gone out. Some were still bright, new, picked from some world in some orchard only days ago and carried in a curled gray hand along a cable toward a house where nobody lived. None of them were going anywhere.

Nobody had told them they could.

*How long?*

> **If the dead ask them one at a time, twelve thousand and sixty dead, each asking one and then the next: eleven hundred years.**

He said nothing.

> **If we do it the way we did the walls of the Mouth, each asks its nine and each one who answers is asked to ask nine more: fourteen rounds. Maybe fifteen. Each round takes as long as the slowest answer.**
>
> **The walls of the Mouth took twelve rounds and nineteen hours. These are farther apart. More of them. Older. Some haven't been asked anything since before your sun.**
>
> **I don't know how long a round will be. I know there are two hours fifty-seven minutes.**

---

He did it anyway.

He hadn't known he was going to, and then he'd always known. He stood on the thread with twelve thousand embers round him and sixty more and a burning man gone warm and quiet between a captain and a sergeant, and went down into the Death Law, all fifty point six percent of it, to the bottom, to the cool dark place where the door stood a little open as it always did now.

And opened it.

Not all the way. Not yet. Enough. As you open your front door at night when you hear people outside in the cold, and stand in it with the light behind you so they can see there's a door.

"Ask them," he said. "Each asks its nine. Tell them first. Tell them there's a door, and it's open, and where it goes. Tell them the threads they came down still go home, if they want home. Tell them they can stay where they are, if they want that. Tell them nobody's going to carry them anywhere ever again unless they ask to be carried."

He looked at the cloud.

"And when they've answered, tell them to ask nine more. And tell them that first, too."

The dead went out.

---

He'd remember it as the strangest two hours of his life and the most beautiful, and never be able to say why. Xiaoyu would write *HE COULDN'T SAY* twice on the wall, and Seed would say that was correct.

Twelve thousand and sixty cold dim sparks went out from the slack thread into a cloud of small lights that went on forever, each stopping in front of one light, then another, then another. Nine. Asking. The Spear Corps in a knot, going out together as they stood round stone No. 7. The First Legion in rows of nine, rows of nine even here. Grid Nine, who'd never seen the sea and had now seen the deep water, asking, one of them told Seed, *very politely, because we don't know what language they speak.* The Third Battalion behind their sergeant, who asked every light he stopped in front of whether it had eaten. The Watch of the Shelf, the color of deep water, slow, who took the coldest and oldest and slowest of the lights, because, Nana Ampofo said, *we're used to waiting for an answer at the bottom of something.*

And the lights answered.

Not in words. Seed couldn't translate most of them: languages from worlds gone since before Blue Star had a sun, shapes that had never had sounds. But it didn't need words. It wasn't a hard question. *There is a door. There is the way home. There is staying. What do you want?* And the lights answered as the walls of the Mouth had: some at once, like a crowd that's been waiting in the cold a long time for somebody to ask; some slowly; some not at all, not yet.

Then the ones who'd answered turned and asked nine more.

---

The first round took nineteen minutes.

He felt it go out through the cloud nine by nine, as frost spreads across a window: from the slack thread outward, a widening ring of the asked, and at its edge the asked asking. He felt the door take the ones who said *door*. Most of them. Most of the old ones. He felt them come in out of the drift toward him, toward the cool dark place at the bottom of the Death Law, small lights burning low, and go past him on the threshold one after another, as the walls of the Mouth had gone past him at the cold fire. Through, to wherever finished things go.

He felt the ones who said *home*. Fewer. The new ones mostly, still bright, picked days or a season ago. Each turned in the dark toward a slack thread leading out of the stopped web in some direction, and went down it. Not carried. Going. As a soldier's want had gone down a river to a ferry when the thing that held it let go. Home, to some world in some orchard where a stone that had marked them stood quiet now, with nothing at the other end of its thread.

He didn't know what was waiting for them. He hoped something was.

And he felt the ones who said *stay*. A few. Lights that had drifted out of a hand into the deep water and found for the first time since they were picked that nobody was carrying them, and wanted to stay exactly where they were, being nobody's, for a while.

*Ask us again,* they said. Seed translated that one; it was the same in every language. *Ask us again tomorrow.*

> **Round two. Twenty-two minutes. Death Law: 50.6 to 51.9 percent.**

---

Round three. Round four. The ring of the asked spreading through the cloud nine by nine, the door taking them, and him standing in it without moving, holding it as you hold a door when the people coming through are old and slow and there are a great many of them, and you've told them it's open, and you're not going to let it close in their faces.

Round six. Round seven.

His nose was bleeding. He knew it the way he knew things about his body from a long way off: on a platform in Lingzhou a body lay against a warm black stone, bleeding onto a blue jacket, with a hand flat on its chest and fifteen hundred people holding the shoulders in front of them so it would stay put.

> **Death Law: 53.2 percent. One hour nine minutes.**

Round nine. Round ten.

The rounds were getting longer, as Seed had said they would. Farther out, the lights were older and slower. Some had been carried so long that when they were asked they didn't understand the question for ages, and the dead had to wait in front of them, as the Watch waited, at the bottom of something, until the answer came up.

> **Round eleven. Thirty-four minutes. Death Law: 54.8 percent. Twenty-one minutes.**

---

He knew at round twelve they wouldn't finish.

Seed didn't need to tell him. He could feel the ring of the asked going out through the cloud, wide now, wider than anything, and beyond it still the edge of the cloud: small dim lights, drifting, not asked yet. Hundreds of thousands. Millions. He didn't know. Seed didn't know.

> **Two more rounds. Maybe three. Each longer than the last. Twelve minutes.**

*I can hold it longer.*

> **No. You said nine hours. I said nine hours. Your body said nine hours. Lingyue said she'd pull.**
>
> **I'm not letting you hold the door open in the deep water until there's nothing on the platform for her to pull back.**

*There are people out there nobody's asked.*

> **I know. I know. I'm sorry. I've run it. Nine hours one minute is the number where there's still a body. Nine hours four minutes is the number where there isn't.**

He stood in the door at the bottom of the Death Law in the deep water, with the ring of the asked going out through the cloud and the edge of the cloud beyond it, and didn't know what to do, and for the first time in a very long while couldn't think of anything at all.

---

> **Huo says—** Seed stopped, then went on slowly. **No. Not Huo. Nana Ampofo.**

He turned the shape of his head. Far out across the deep water, at the edge of the ring where the oldest and slowest lights were, a cluster of cold dim embers the color of deep water had stopped asking and turned and were looking back at him along the slack thread.

The Watch of the Shelf. Eleven hundred and forty-six of the drowned off Kesse.

> **She says: we'll stay. They've lived at the bottom of the sea forty years, some of them a hundred. The deep water isn't the sea but it's close enough. It's dark and it's cold and nothing pushes back and you wait a long time for anything to answer. They're good at that.**
>
> **They'll keep asking, nine by nine, the ones at the edge, as long as it takes. You can't hold the door open from here; they know. But it's always a little open now, isn't it? You said. The ones who want it will find it. They'll show them where.**
>
> **She says: ask us again in a year.**

He stood on the thread.

*Nana. It's not your border. It's not even your sea.*

> **She says Yaw asked first, in the vote about the top: why does there have to be a top? Remember? This is the same. Why does there have to be a shore?**
>
> **She says that's not a question, boy. Go home. Your wife's counting.**

---

> **Four minutes.**

He did the only thing he could. He asked them, all eleven hundred and forty-six, through the net the slow way, nine by nine, as they asked everything: did they want this. Were they sure. He told them what it would be. The deep water forever, or until they asked to stop. The cold, the dark, the waiting. Nobody singing the names to them at night as Nana sang them at the bottom of the sea off Kesse.

The answer came back up the net in under a minute, faster than the Watch of the Shelf had ever answered anything.

> **Yes. Eleven hundred and forty-six.**
>
> **She says: we'll sing them to each other.**

> **Two minutes. Death Law: 55.9 percent. Yuanshu.**

*Callum,* he thought. *The captain's got him?*

> **He says: got him. Going down. The sergeant's got the other arm. We'll be slower than you. Go.**

He let the door ease back to a little open.

He felt it: the threshold, the cool dark place at the bottom of everything, going back to how it would always be now, ajar, as a door is left ajar in a house where somebody's expected. And far out at the edge of the cloud, eleven hundred and forty-six cold dim embers the color of deep water turned back to the lights they hadn't asked yet and began, slowly, nine by nine, to ask.

> **One minute.**

---

A hand on his chest pulled.

He felt it as he'd felt it at the end of the Port Halvard thread: warm, far away, very strong, a hand flat on a chest a long way down, closing and pulling, as you haul someone out of water. He didn't fight it. He let go of the thread and fell.

Back along it, through the slack web, past the stopped knot where the oldest thing anyone had ever made sat still for the first time since before the gardens with its thousands of hands hanging open on its cables. Down the cable past the threads from other orchards running slack and quiet alongside. Out of the deep water. Past the end of the arm and the empty edge of the cold fire. Past the branch with its gold bud, which turned. Past the keeper, warm and a third smaller, saying rough and close *THERE YOU ARE*. A docker's rough hand. An old man who liked the sea. A girl's voice, brisk: *got you, you idiot, got you*.

And down.

---

He opened his eyes on the platform of the Awakening Hall with blood in his mouth and his wife's face over his.

Her hand was still flat on his chest, pressing. The flame in her other palm was blazing the size of a lamp and she didn't seem to know it was there. Behind her Qin Hao, white to the lips, hands open, the white fire gone out of them because all of it had gone up the staff. Su Mingli at the corner of the platform with her arms unfolded. Xiaoyu at the foot of the steps with the marker in her hand, uncapped.

And on the bottom tier, Gao Min, holding up an old mechanical stopwatch so he could see.

*9:00:00.*

"You said nine hours," said Shen Lingyue. Her voice shook. She wasn't crying; not quite. "I said I'd pull. I pulled."

"You pulled," he said.

"Did you finish?"

He lay against the warm stone with the blue jacket over its shoulder and fifteen hundred people holding one another's shoulders up the aisle and out the door, and thought about the edge of the cloud, and eleven hundred and forty-six cold dim embers the color of deep water asking, nine by nine, in the dark, for as long as it took.

"No," he said. "Somebody else is finishing."
