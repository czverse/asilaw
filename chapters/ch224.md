## Chapter 224: Two Rings Learning

In the morning there were four rings in Blue Star's sky, and two of them were talking to each other.

That was what the radio and the television and the crowds on the north wall kept coming back to all that first day, the way your tongue keeps going back to a chipped tooth: you could see it with your own eyes. Low over the end of the world in the gray dawn sat the keeper's small white ring with its gold glow. Beside it, two of the great cracked wheels that had come along the arm to collect a sack and prune everything it had touched.

One of them had a light at its center, small and soft and white, like a first star.

The other was dark. But overnight it had turned, so that its dark center faced the one with the light. And the light faced it back.

> **They're asking each other,** said Seed. **I can feel it through the keeper. Very slowly, like the coldest of the drowned. One word, then nothing for an hour, then the next.**
>
> **The one with the Elder in it asks. The other tries to answer and doesn't know how. It's never wanted anything.**
>
> **She's teaching it. Putting the question down, waiting, not looking in the bowl.**

---

He didn't go to the wall. He couldn't. Bai Lu wouldn't let him, and for once he didn't argue. He lay on the narrow bed in the back room of the Ossuary with the window open onto the courtyard, the staff on the floor beside him, and his whole body feeling as if something had picked it up by the scruff of the neck twice in one night, because something had.

Lingyue sat on the floor with her back against the bed and her sword across her knees. She hadn't slept and hadn't said much. Every so often, without looking, she reached up and put her hand on his arm as if checking it was still attached, and took it away again.

Hush was on the windowsill in the sun, facing north.

> ***...she was very kind to me. On the raft, the first voyage, when I was small and curled up and would not come out. She stood in the middle and steered, and every night she told me where we were going, even though I did not ask.***
>
> ***...I did not say thank you.***

"So say it now," said Lingyue, without looking up. "She's right there. You can see her from the window."

Hush was quiet for a while. Then it lifted one thin leg out of the sunlight toward the north, toward a small white light in the center of a gray ring low over the edge of the world, and held it there.

> ***...thank you.***

It didn't say another word all day.

---

The keeper asked her at three in the morning.

There was no Elder now to carry its voice. It came down the gold thread on its own, the way it had learned to on the night of Kirovsk, into the old banking hall where one lamp burned over the board and Xiaoyu was up the stepladder because she hadn't slept either.

Its voice was strange without her. Rougher. Nearer. Like someone who has always had an interpreter, and the interpreter has gone, and now they have to find the words themselves.

> ***...ELDER. I SAID I WOULD ASK EVERY MORNING.***
>
> ***...WHAT DO YOU WANT?***

Nothing. The lamp, the long wall of names, the boxes of cards in the vault, Xiaoyu motionless on the ladder.

Then, faint and very far off, from a ring that had come to prune everything Seed had touched, a voice like a glass struck very softly:

> *...to be asked.*
>
> *...sometimes.*

The keeper didn't answer at once.

> ***...THAT IS WHAT I SAID. WHEN THEY ASKED ME.***

> *...I know. I heard you. I was very proud.*
>
> *...ask me again tomorrow.*

Xiaoyu capped the marker, climbed down, sat on the bottom step, and put her face in her hands. She wasn't crying. She just sat like that.

Then she climbed back up and wrote at the very end of the last wall, past THE FLOOR, on the last bit of white plaster before the corner of the vault door:

*THE ELDER. ONE OF THE FIFTY-TWO. SHE IS A YES. SHE IS IN THE SKY. ASK HER EVERY MORNING.*

---

On the third day he could sit up, and he went and sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees and a blanket round his shoulders, because the back room was too small to think in.

> **Numbers,** said Seed. **You asked me for them a long time ago and you've never told me to stop.**

*Go on.*

> **Kirovsk: forty-one thousand taken, twenty-two thousand back down the white threads, nineteen thousand not. The second ring is sand on the snow.**
>
> **The keeper's field: three million one hundred thousand cousins and walls taken up the third ring's threads before the Elder caught your wrist. They're in the third ring now. Packed. Like grain.**
>
> **The Elder's in there with them. She asks them every morning. Most say ask again tomorrow. Some say they'd like to go back to the field. She's learning how to let them.**
>
> **She says a ring with a yes in it can let go of what it took. She didn't know that. Nobody did. No ring ever had a yes in it before. Yesterday she sent eleven thousand back down a thread to the keeper's field.**

He sat with that.

> **Death Law: 44.0 to 45.6 percent. The second ring. You opened the door for a ring. Nobody's ever done that.**
>
> **No level. You didn't kill anything. Neither did your dead.**
>
> **Qin Hao: Lord, Level 70. His fire's white again. He hasn't let go of his notebook since Kirovsk. He sleeps with it under his pillow, like a kid with a phone.**
>
> **Lingyue: Level 66.**
>
> **And the floor. A hundred and thirty-eight names, unpressed, tied into three knots, sent up three rings. I don't remember them. I know there were a hundred and thirty-eight. I know the wall's got them and your mother reads them every morning.**
>
> **There's a place where they used to be. It isn't empty. It's quiet, like the bottom of a well. I go and sit in it sometimes. I don't know why.**

*That's allowed.*

> **I know. I checked.**

---

On the fifth day the first ring answered.

Not the Elder's ring. The other one, the one that had picked up the first knot over Lingzhou and stopped, and had been holding a question it couldn't answer for five days while the Elder's ring asked it, every hour, and waited.

It came the long way through the echo, faint and odd, the way a child's first word comes after a year of listening to everyone else talk.

> ***...H...***
>
> ***...HOME.***

Nobody in the banking hall spoke.

> **That's Liang Fu's word,** said Seed. **Home. The Collector kept it and followed it.**
>
> **This is different. This ring was a keeper once, above somebody's world, for longer than Blue Star has had a sun. Then it was pruned and forgot, and came along the arm to collect a sack.**
>
> **Somebody asked it what it wants. And the first thing it found, when it looked inside where the yes used to be, was the shape of the world it used to keep.**
>
> **It wants to go home.**

"Where is it? Its world?"

> **A hundred and fifty-three raft-days along the arm. The third field the reader pruned, the one we watched go dark from the island. There's a world underneath it. I saw it through the keeper's eye, blue and green. I didn't look closely. I didn't think anyone there would ever matter to us.**
>
> **It's still there. Its keeper got pruned and turned into a ring and came here. Now it wants to go back.**

It went on the seventh day.

Anyone on Blue Star looking north saw a vast cracked ring turn slowly away from the other three, away from the keeper's little white one and away from the Elder's ring with its soft light, and begin, very slowly, to move. Back along the arm the way it had come, toward a blue and green world a hundred and fifty-three raft-days off, under a field that had gone out from one side like a page burning while a boy watched from an island.

It didn't say goodbye. It didn't know how.

But as it went, the Elder's ring said something after it, very faint, and the keeper heard and came down the thread at three in the morning to tell somebody, because it couldn't keep it in.

> ***...SHE SAID: ASK THEM WHAT THEY WANT WHEN YOU GET HOME.***
>
> ***...SHE SAID: THEY WILL NOT KNOW. NOBODY EVER ASKED THEM.***
>
> ***...SHE SAID: PUT THE QUESTION DOWN. AND WAIT.***

---

On the ninth night Kwan came into the Ossuary at two in the morning with a laptop held out in front of her like a tray of hot soup. She didn't knock and didn't say hello.

He knew from her face. It was the face she'd had on the night of the three gray rings.

She turned the screen round. Seed's map of the arm: twelve gold dots on a faint curved line, three of them gray now, one much brighter than the rest. And far along, past the gray dots, past the place where an island had once raced through the dark toward a cold wheel of stone, a single red mark.

The Mouth. Where something very large and very patient had been sitting still for two months since it read the Sixth and took them, like something that had eaten, or something that had seen what it didn't expect and was thinking it over.

"It moved," said Kwan.

> **I see it,** said Seed. **It isn't pruning. It isn't reading. It hasn't gone to another field.**
>
> **It's coming straight along the arm. Toward the brightest field in the Shallows. Toward the keeper that taught two rings to ask. Toward the vessel.**
>
> **The rings were what it sends. This is what sends them.**

Behind him on the long white wall, the queen of hearts hung on its pin under the lamp at the end of the INSIDE column. At the end of the last wall, past THE FLOOR, someone had added a line that afternoon in a quick, sharp hand that wasn't Xiaoyu's, next to her daughter's capitals: *I read these every morning. — Z.W.*

> **Two months,** said Seed. **I think the gardener's coming.**
>
> **Itself.**

Kwan shut the laptop and sat down on the step next to him without asking, and after a while Fat Tan came down in his dressing gown and made tea for three without asking either, and none of them went back to bed.
