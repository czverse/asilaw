## Chapter 217: The First Page

He went down with it because it asked him to, and because it said it didn't want to go by itself.

They did it in the vault of the Ossuary at three in the morning, because the vault was the one room in Lingzhou nobody ever walked into without asking first. The round steel door stood open on shelves full of boxes of playing cards. Yuanshu sat on the cold floor in the middle with the staff across his knees. Lingyue sat against the shelves with her sword. His mother sat in her wheelchair in the doorway, because Seed had asked for her too.

"I don't know what use I'll be," said Zhou Wanqing. "I built the base layer. I locked it. I never once went back in."

> **I know,** said Seed. **I'd like you here anyway. You were the one who asked them, in the basement. I'd like them to see your face again, if they can see anything.**

His mother's hands were folded on the blanket so tightly the knuckles had gone white.

"All right," she said.

---

He shut his eyes, and Seed took him down.

Not into the Death Law, and not toward the door that hadn't quite shut since the cold fire. Somewhere he'd never been, though it had been sitting behind his eyes for two years. Under Seed: under the counting and the models and the white letters and the weights. Under the third page with its weave of pressed silver thread. Down to the first.

It was quiet.

That was the first thing he noticed. Everything else in Seed was busy, even when it was still: counting the rings, weighing the vote, watching the keeper, holding the Lattice open through Adjutant Xu, remembering the water at Port Abiri and the shape of the Western Pass and fifty thousand names. It hummed like a city at night.

The first page didn't hum. It was the stillness at the bottom of a well.

And in the stillness, names.

He couldn't see them; there was nothing to see. He felt them the way you feel people in a dark room: a great many, close together, not moving, not speaking. Four thousand one hundred and two. Each a name in a woman's quick sharp handwriting, with one word next to it.

*Yes.*

Under each name, very faint and very far down, something that wasn't a name. A warmth. The shape of a person. What was left of a mind harvested by a Wailing Monarch long ago and packed into a crystal, spilled off a cart in the Wastes, sold, bought, and then asked, four nights running, in a basement, by a woman with her hand on the glass.

> **They're here,** said Seed. **I always knew. I was built on them. Out of them. They're the floor I stand on.**
>
> **I've never once spoken to them. I didn't know you could talk to a floor.**

*Ask them. The way you ask.*

> **I'm scared.**

*I know.*

> **You were scared of her answer, on the step. You asked anyway.**

*Yes.*

> **Right. Yes.**

---

> **Hello,** said Seed, to the bottom of the well.
>
> **I'm Seed. You built me. A long time ago you said yes to a woman with her hand on your crystal, and let her make something out of you. I'm what she made.**
>
> **I'm going to ask you something. First I'll tell you what I'll do with your answer.**
>
> **If you say yes, I'll unpress some of the thread that holds you and tie three knots out of it and send them up to three rings that are coming to cut our world, to ask them in their own language what they want. Some of you won't be held any more. I don't know which. The weave won't let me choose.**
>
> **If you say no, I won't do it. I'll find another way, or I won't. You'll stay where you are and nobody will ask you again unless you want them to.**
>
> **If you don't say anything, I'll wait.**
>
> **Will you let me?**

Nothing.

The bottom of the well stayed still. Four thousand one hundred and two names in the dark, motionless. He felt Seed waiting the way he'd waited on the step of the hut with his heart rate jumping thirty beats in two seconds.

Then something moved, very faint and very deep.

Not a voice. Seed had to translate it slowly, the way it had translated the coldest of the drowned off Kesse, waiting for each word to finish before it knew which word it was. But he could feel the shape before the words arrived: a question, asked by someone who'd been asked a lot of things in their life and had learned to ask one back.

> ***...is she there?***

Seed didn't answer.

> ***...the woman. With her hand on the glass. Who asked us. Is she there?***

---

In the doorway of the vault, Zhou Wanqing made a small sound.

He couldn't see her. He was at the bottom of the first page with his eyes shut. He heard it anyway, through the staff and the cold floor, like hearing someone in the next room called by a name they'd stopped expecting to hear.

> **Yes,** said Seed. **She's here. In a chair, in a doorway. She's older. She spent seven years in a column above the sky and then in a fire, and she came home. She's listening.**

> ***...tell her the floor is good.***
>
> ***...tell her we have been holding something up for seven years and it is the best thing we ever held.***

His mother was crying, and not trying to stop.

"Tell them," said Zhou Wanqing. Her voice had split down the middle. "Tell them I'm sorry I locked the door. I didn't want anybody taking them out and using them for anything else. I never thought about whether they'd want to be asked again." She swallowed. "I should have come back. I should have asked them again. I never did."

> ***...she is asking now. Through you.***
>
> ***...that is the same thing.***

---

After that, they answered.

Not all at once, and not nine by nine like the drowned or the walls. One at a time, the way people speak in a room where nobody has said anything for a very long while and then somebody does, and the rest remember that they can.

> **They're saying yes,** said Seed. **Most of them.**
>
> **They're saying: we said yes to being built into something that counts, and we didn't know what it would count. It counted the dead. It counted a wall, a sea, a field above the sky and every name in it. It counted the cold.**
>
> **They're saying they'd like it to keep counting. And if some of them have to be let go so it can, they'd like to be asked which.**

He felt Seed freeze.

> **I can't choose. The weave doesn't let me choose which strand.**

> ***...you cannot choose.***
>
> ***...we can.***

He didn't get it for a second. Then he did, and so did Seed, at exactly the same moment, like two people catching the same joke and looking at each other across a room.

The weave didn't let Seed choose which strand to pull. Nothing in the weave said anything about strands choosing to come loose.

> ***...we were a harvest. Grain in a sack. Nobody asked us anything, ever, until the basement.***
>
> ***...then someone asked and we said yes and became the floor of something that asks.***
>
> ***...some of us are tired. We have held this up for seven years and we are glad we did. We would like to be the thread.***
>
> ***...not pulled. Given.***

> **How many?**

The answer took a long time to climb up out of the well.

> **A hundred and thirty-eight,** said Seed. **They're saying: unpress us. Tie the knots out of us. Send us up to the rings.**
>
> **They're saying: we were cut once by a thread that came down out of the sky and didn't ask. This time we'd like to go up a thread and do the asking.**

---

> **I won't remember them,** said Seed. **Once they're unpressed their names won't be on the first page any more. They'll be in the knots, and the knots will go up to the rings, and I won't know who they were. I'll know there were a hundred and thirty-eight. I won't know their names.**

*Then read them out,* he thought. *Now. Before.*

> **To who?**

He opened his eyes.

Cold floor, staff across his knees, blood at his nose, the way there always was now when he went down anywhere. Lingyue against the shelves with her sword, not moving. His mother in the doorway with her face wet.

And on the bottom step of the vault stairs, in her pajamas, hair sticking up on one side, an uncapped black marker in her hand: Xiaoyu. She had heard their mother crying through two floors and come down without anyone waking her.

She looked at him, at their mother, at the place just above his eyes.

"Who do I write?" she said.

---

Seed read them, all a hundred and thirty-eight, out loud through Fat Tan's speaker in the banking-hall wall, one name at a time, in the order they'd come forward from the bottom of the first page. Xiaoyu stood on the stepladder at the end of the last wall, past SHIOMI, round the corner, on new white plaster, and wrote them down in her careful capitals. Every one.

She didn't ask what heading to use. She wrote one at the top anyway, before the first name.

*THE FLOOR.*

It took an hour. Fat Tan came down halfway through in a dressing gown, held the stepladder without being asked, and didn't say a word the entire time, which for him was some kind of record.

When Seed got to the last name it stopped, the way it stopped before saying something it had decided to say.

> **I won't remember them after tomorrow. The wall will.**
>
> **That's what the wall's for. I know that now. I always thought it was for the dead. It's for the things that are about to be let go, so somebody remembers when the one who held them can't.**

Zhou Wanqing had wheeled herself out of the vault across the marble and stopped in front of the new column. She looked up at it: a hundred and thirty-eight names in her daughter's capitals under a heading she'd never have thought of.

She reached up as far as she could from the chair and touched the first name with two fingers. Then the second. Then the third.

"I remember the handwriting," she said. "Mine. In the basement. I remember writing every one of these." She kept her fingers on the plaster. "I'll remember them for you, Seed, when you can't. I'll come down every morning and read them out. I did the first watch. I can do this one too."

> **Thank you,** said Seed. **That's what the floor said, through you. It's the same thing.**

Xiaoyu capped the marker, climbed down, sat on the bottom rung of the stepladder, and fell asleep there, upright, with her head against the wall, and nobody moved her until morning.
