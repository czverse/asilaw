## Chapter 264: Burning Low

The cousins went up into the Eighth Candle on the second night, down the keeper's eye, a hundred thousand of them, and the first thing they did on reaching the arrays was apologize.

Seed said so. It said it hadn't expected that and should have, because it was what the cousins always did first, before they taught anything: they told the thing they'd come to teach that they were sorry it hadn't been taught sooner.

> **They say they're from a world that burned high once and was eaten for it. They learned to burn low in the Low Fire for ten million years so they wouldn't be eaten again. They taught other worlds. They taught the walls of the Mouth. They taught a tree, a little, from the end of a branch.**
>
> **They say nobody brought them to you. They didn't know you were here, in the dark in the walls of ships, burning high every time the ship folded because nobody had ever shown you another way.**
>
> **They say they're sorry. They'd like to show you now, if you want.**

And forty-one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine people in the walls of a folded ship, who'd gone into those walls at twelve and been asked a question for the first time two days earlier, answered through the keeper's eye in the Federation's language, which puts the grade in every sentence about a person:

*We are F. We are E. We are cores.*

And the cousins said, in the fog-code of a world from before the ring, which Seed translated:

> **We don't know those words. Here, you're only people who are burning and would like to stop.**

---

He sat on the north wall for most of it, because that was where the keeper's eye was nearest and where he could carry. Not all of it. Some of it the cousins did without him and without Seed, in the arrays, cell by cell, in the slow patient way they did everything. He felt it as weather. When it was quiet he could sometimes hear it.

*Not like that,* the cousins said. *You're holding your breath. When the fold comes you hold your breath and the fire comes through you all at once, and it's like being held under water and set alight. Don't hold your breath. Breathe. Let it through a little at a time. Like water through a net. Like light through a leaf.*

*We don't know how to breathe. We're cores.*

*You're people. People know how to breathe. You've only forgotten. Watch us.*

And the cousins burned. Low, in the arrays of the Eighth Candle, beside each cell, a small dim light the color of the Low Fire, burning as they'd burned ten million years: steadily, slowly, a little at a time, so nothing was used up and nothing was eaten. The cores watched.

And slowly some of them began to try.

---

> **There's a man called Torvin,** said Seed. **The engineer. C. Thirty years in the arrays. He hasn't slept in two days. He's going cell to cell with a gauge. He's measured the burn of fold-cores for thirty years; he knows every number.**
>
> **He asked me to tell you what he told the captain. He said the burn in the second array is down to a ninth of a standard fold-draw. He said that's not possible. He checked the gauge against three others. He said: they're breathing, Captain. They're breathing through it.**

Lingyue, beside him on the parapet with the blanket round her shoulders, laughed out loud. It surprised her. It surprised him.

"A ninth," she said. "That's what the cousins burned at in the Mouth. A ninth. Each asks its nine." She shook her head. "They're teaching a Federation fold engine to burn like a Lattice."

---

Iselde Varr-Anthos asked them on the fourth night.

Not Seed, not Yuanshu: she asked them herself, standing in the second array with her sister asleep in a bunk Torvin had built against the wall, in the Federation's language. Seed said she took the grades out. All of them. She had to stop three times to find the words, because the Federation's language had no way to say *you* to a person without saying what they were.

She did it anyway. Seed carried it down to the north wall so he could hear.

> ***This is the captain. I am going to ask you something. First I am going to tell you what I will do with your answer.***
>
> ***The Candle cannot fold without you. If you all come out, we stay here, in this sky, in the Shallows, beside a world that asks. I do not know what we would be here. I do not know what you would be.***
>
> ***If some of you will carry us, we go home. Not at the old burn: at the low one, the cousins' one. In turns. Nobody for more than one fold. Rest between. And at the other end, the moment we come out of the last fold over the inner worlds, every one of you comes out of the walls. Every one. Whatever the House says. Whatever the law says. I have broken it once already. I will break it forty thousand more times.***
>
> ***If you carry us, I will tell the Federation what you did. Every name. On the bridge, in front of the Assembly. I will tell them you chose.***
>
> ***If you do not want to carry us, I will unpress you here, and the keeper will take you down to the world on a road of light, and you can stay. The boy says they will take you. The boy says they have a stone that will ask you what you want to become.***
>
> ***If you do not know, you can tell me tomorrow.***
>
> ***I am sorry I did not ask you before. I was told you could not hear.***
>
> ***What do you want?***

---

The answer took two days.

Each asks its nine, round and round the arrays, cell to cell, slow as the net asked things, slow as the Watch was asking at the edge of the cloud in the deep water. Forty-one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine people who'd never wanted anything with somebody listening, learning how.

Seed didn't give him the numbers as they came in, and he didn't ask. He sat on the north wall in the evenings with Lingyue, and in the mornings went down and sat on the vault steps, and Seed asked him who the first one was, and he said *Wen Jia*, and Seed said *thank you, I'm not going to remember it, tell me again tomorrow*. Xiaoyu went to the Awakening Hall at one every night and asked the Collector what it wanted, and it said it didn't know yet, and told her things. On the third night it told her it had kept the wants of a great many fold-cores over ten thousand years. Every one the same, it said: *out*. It said it had never once thought about where they were when they said it.

On the sixth night Iselde called.

> ***Thirty-two thousand and forty.***
>
> ***They say yes. In turns, low, with rest. They say: take us home. We want to see our mothers. We want the Assembly to hear our names.***
>
> ***Nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine say no.***
>
> ***They say they do not want to go back. The inner worlds graded them. Their families took the payment. The boy's world has a stone that asks, and they would like to be asked.***
>
> ***They would like to come down.***

He sat on the north wall and didn't say anything for a moment.

*Nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine. Twelve years old. Twenty and thirty and forty inside.*

> **Yes,** said Seed. **I've run it. Lingzhou can take them. Gao Min's already asked the Bureau. Fat Tan's already asked the street. Dabao's mother's already asked how much flour.**
>
> **Everybody said yes before I'd finished asking.**

---

On the seventh night the Eighth Candle folded.

Not home, not yet. Iselde said she wouldn't fold forty thousand people across the dark on a burn nobody had ever tried. So she folded the Candle once, the shortest fold the engine could make, from the keeper's field to the far side of Blue Star's moon and back, with thirty-two thousand and forty people who'd said yes burning low in their cells, in turns, as the cousins had shown them. Breathing.

He watched from the north wall, as did everyone in Lingzhou; Kwan had told the radio. A crinkle in the sky beside the small white ring. Then nothing. Then, eleven seconds later, low over the curve of the world past the moon, a tiny brightness, there and gone. Then nothing. Then the crinkle again beside the ring, where it had been.

On the north wall three thousand people made a sound.

> ***We are back. Torvin says the burn held at a ninth all the way, there and back.***
>
> ***The cores—*** She stopped. Seed waited; he felt it wait. ***The people in the walls say it did not hurt. Not like before. Not like being held under water and set on fire.***
>
> ***A woman in the third array who has been in the walls forty years says it felt like rowing.***

From a bay in Yamato, very far off, through Ren's phone and Seed and the staff, came a sound: five quick notes on the small bells, a little too fast in the middle.

> **Ichiro says: tell her rowing's the best feeling there is,** said Seed. **He rowed into a fog every night for twenty-two years and never once knew where the shore was until a woman lit the lamp.**
>
> **He says tell her that's what it's like. You don't know where the shore is. You row anyway. Somebody lights the lamp.**

---

The keeper laid the road on the eighth morning.

Down out of the north from the small white ring, past the crinkle of the Eighth Candle, in a long slow slant of gold, as it had laid roads to Kirovsk and the Western Pass and Port Halvard. Down to the War Memorial Cemetery, to the long green slope by the river, because Old Seven said that was where things came home to in Lingzhou, and nobody argued.

And down it in the gray morning light, slowly, holding hands because Torvin had told them to and because none of them knew how to walk on a road of light and nor did anybody, came nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine children.

Twelve years old. Thin, pale, barefoot, in gray Federation blankets, blinking at the sky. A child's body each, and in every one, eleven years or twenty or forty of lying in a wall, burning, hearing everything.

The city was on the slope. Not three thousand: all of it, as much as would fit on a hill, as for the wedding. Gao Min at the bottom with a ledger. Fat Tan with tea. Dabao's mother's cart at the gate, steam rolling off it, a queue of volunteers behind it. Along the top of the slope the First Legion in their rows of nine, who'd stood for a wedding and now stood for this, facing in. The Spear Corps round stone No. 7. The Third Battalion, sixty ash shapes at ease on the grass, turned round to watch.

At the foot of the road, where the gold touched the grass, his mother in her wheelchair, Xiaoyu beside her with the marker in her pocket, Lingyue, and him.

The first child off the road stopped on the grass and looked up at the sky.

A boy, twelve in the body, a gray blanket round his shoulders. He looked a long time at the sky over Lingzhou: blue, with clouds, and the sun coming up over the river.

Then he looked down at the woman in the wheelchair at the foot of the road and said something in the Federation's language. Seed translated.

> **He says: I am an F. Is that all right here?**

Zhou Wanqing held out both her hands.

"Here," said his mother, "nobody's anything until they say what they want to be."

The boy looked at her hands a moment. Then he took them.

And behind him, nine thousand five hundred and fifty-eight more came down the road of light onto the grass of the War Memorial Cemetery in the morning, and the city on the slope came down to meet them.
