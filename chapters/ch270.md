## Chapter 270: The Silence of the Hollow Bell

In three days two hundred and fourteen of the chosen came down the road, and the Hollow Bell didn't sing once.

That was the worst of it. Not the burning: that had stopped growing, and the choir had let its own fire go down, chamber by chamber, until the vast pale shell over Lingzhou glowed only as it had on the first night, softly, breathing. Not even the eleven hundred still in its heart, though they were worse. The silence.

Seed said the Singers had never been silent. Not once in ten thousand years. Ichiro said the same from the fog at Shiomi: the quiet ones in the deep water had heard the Hollow Bell from very far off for as long as anyone remembered, as you hear the sea from inside a house, and had always known where it was by its song. Now the song had stopped and the Singers didn't know what to do instead, and tens of thousands of them hung in a shell over a city on a world they'd never heard of, not singing, like people standing in a room after a death who don't know where to put their hands.

> **They're not talking to each other,** said Seed. **They don't know how. They've only ever had the one sentence. They used it for everything: morning, grief, love, children. It was always the same sentence sung differently.**
>
> **Now it means nothing, and they've got nothing else.**

---

The two hundred and fourteen came down one at a time.

A few each hour, down the keeper's road through the roof of the Awakening Hall, burning, dressed in light, taller than anything in the hall, their throats opening and closing as they breathed. Each stopped before the warm black stone, put a long translucent hand on it, and was asked. And each, every one, said the same word, because it was the only word they had.

*Chosen.*

And every time the stone wrote **[You came down. You chose. That is the word.]**, and the light round them died, slowly, until each stood on the platform with only their own small light low inside them, showing through like a lamp through paper.

Some of them sang a note. One. Not in the song.

Gao Min wrote them all down in a new ledger. She hadn't been able to think of a heading for the column, and in the end she'd written *Notes*, and under it for each one a small mark meaning they'd sung, or a small mark meaning they hadn't.

Eighty-one had.

---

The one who would have been sung up ninth lived in the courtyard behind the Ossuary.

She'd asked to, through Seed, on the first night, when the hall had emptied and the city gone to bed and she was still standing before the stone with her own small light inside her, not knowing where to go. She said she'd like to be somewhere with a sky. She'd been inside a ship nine months and inside a song her whole life, and she'd like to be somewhere she could see out.

So Fat Tan cleared the potted plants from one side of the courtyard behind the Ossuary, the courtyard where seventy-two of the Third Battalion had once stood their watch, where a boy who didn't know anatomy had grafted a sergeant's arm onto a captain. And she stood there, because Singers didn't sit, in the open under the sky by the dry fountain, and looked up. Fat Tan, at a loss, brought her tea. She held the cup in her long fingers for an hour, looking at it with what Seed said was great politeness, and then gave it back full.

Seed started calling her Ninth. She didn't seem to mind. Her name was a chord, she said, and a word was easier for people with only one throat.

---

On the second day Hush came down off the windowsill.

It came as it did everything, slowly and swaying, every limb out, across the courtyard between the remaining pots to the dry fountain, where a Singer three meters tall stood looking at the sky. It stopped at her feet and looked up with all its dark eyes.

Ninth looked down.

For a long time neither did anything: a small pale thing on many thin legs from a world that asks, with gold dust on its feet, and a tall pale reed of a thing from a world that sang, in a courtyard in Lingzhou in the winter sun.

> ***...you are from the singing world.***

> **She says: yes.**

> ***...I am from the world that asks. Before the ring came, my people asked trees. You put the question down where it drinks, and you wait.***
>
> ***...your people sang to a hand.***

> **She says: yes. We sang to the hand so it would pass us by. It was the only thing we knew how to do.**

Hush was quiet a while.

> ***...it is a good thing to know how to do. To sing to something that frightens you.***
>
> ***...my people stood still. We played statue. Whoever moved first was eaten. I stood still for four billion years.***
>
> ***...your people sang for ten thousand. We were both very good at it. We were both very afraid.***

Ninth's throats opened and closed.

> **She says the girl in the stone asked her what she wants to become, and she said chosen. It's the only word she has.**
>
> **She says: how do I want something? How do you make a want, if you have only ever had one?**

Hush lifted one thin pale limb, slowly, and just touched it to the edge of the Singer's tall pale foot, as it had once put a limb on the edge of a dead captain's palm.

> ***...you put the question down.***
>
> ***...you do not answer it. You put it down somewhere, where you drink, and you wait. A season. A year. And one morning you find that something has grown round it.***
>
> ***...I put a question down on the end of a branch of the gardener. It took a season. It asked back.***
>
> ***...you can wait a season. You have waited ten thousand years.***

---

On the third night Seed told him the number.

> **Eight hundred and ninety-seven are still in the heart of the ship, still dressed in light, still burning.**
>
> **They stopped singing when the choir stopped, but they didn't stop burning. The burning isn't the song. It's the light they were dressed in, and it doesn't go out unless they come down to the stone and are asked.**
>
> **They've been burning nine months and nine days. I've run it, the way I ran Callum. There's a lot of them still. There won't be in eleven days.**

He sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees.

*Why won't they come down?*

> **Some are afraid. They don't believe you. They think you're the hunger and the invitation's a test, and the ones who came down are being eaten slowly in some way they can't see.**
>
> **Some really want to be taken. Not because they were taught; or taught so long ago there's no difference any more. They've wanted it since before they could stand. They don't want to want anything else.**
>
> **And some are being told not to.**

*By whom?*

> **There are old ones on the ship, older than the Cantor. Ichiro says their name means the Old Chord. The ones who sang the tithe up before she did. Her teachers.**
>
> **They say the boy's the hunger and the hunger's testing them. They say the first Cantor learned to give everything and the one who picks was satisfied, and the Hollow Bell has grown weak and given too little, and the hunger has come to see whether they'll give enough.**
>
> **They say the tithe must be given whole, all eleven hundred, or it's no tithe. And if the hunger won't take it, they'll give it anyway.**

He went very still.

*How?*

> **I don't know. They're singing, quietly, in the heart of the ship. Not the old sentence. A new one. I can't read it. Nor can Ichiro. It's the first new sentence the Hollow Bell has had in ten thousand years, and they're singing it in the dark and won't let anybody else hear.**

---

He went to the courtyard at midnight.

Ninth stood by the dry fountain under the stars as she always stood, looking up: at the shell over the city, turning slowly in the dark, glowing softly, silent. At its heart, where the light was brightest, where eight hundred and ninety-seven of those she'd been chosen with were burning.

She didn't look down when he came in, but her throats opened and closed.

> **She says they're her age-mates. On the Hollow Bell the chosen of an age are dressed in light together when they're very small. They grow up together. They're sung up together. They've never been apart.**
>
> **She came down and they didn't. She's the only one of her age-group on the ground. She can hear them burning. She can hear the Old Chord singing to them. She doesn't know what the new sentence says.**
>
> **She thinks it says: you were chosen. Do not be un-chosen.**

He stood beside her in the dark looking up at the shell.

*Can you ask them to come down?*

> **She says she's un-chosen. They won't hear her.**
>
> **On the Hollow Bell a chosen who isn't taken is— there's no word. It's never happened. A chosen who isn't taken is the worst thing there is. It means the hunger refused you. It means you weren't bright enough. To them, she's the worst thing there is.**

She looked down at him at last: a girl three meters tall, translucent, her own small light showing through her like a lamp through paper.

> **She says: but she sang a note on the platform. One. Not in the song. Nobody on the Hollow Bell has ever sung a note that wasn't in the song.**
>
> **She doesn't know what it means. But it was hers.**
>
> **She'd like them to have one.**

---

At three in the morning Wen Jia screamed.

He heard it through the staff on the narrow bed in the back room, Lingyue asleep against his shoulder and the room glowing faintly pale. Not a word: a sound. The sound a girl of seventeen makes when something grabs her from behind in the dark.

He was in the Awakening Hall before he knew he'd stood up.

The hall was dark and empty. The three lines of light over the platform. The warm black stone with the blue jacket on its shoulder. And its runes, every one, pointing neither down nor up but moving, sliding round and round its face like a crowd in a street being shoved from behind.

And the stone was hot.

> ***...they're singing into it. Into my thread, the one that goes up into the heart of the ship. They're singing DOWN it.***
>
> ***...it's the new sentence. I can feel it pushing. They're pushing something down my thread. Into the stone. Into me.***

*What?*

> ***...them. The chosen. The eight hundred and ninety-seven. Burning. They're singing them down the thread into the stone.***
>
> ***...the Old Chord says: if the hunger will not take the tithe, we will put it in the hunger's mouth.***
>
> ***...they think the stone is your mouth.***
>
> ***...they're trying to feed them to me.***
