## Chapter 269: Chosen

"You did it again," he said.

He said it on the battlement in the dawn, under the song, with the vast pale shell turning over the city. Not angrily; he didn't have it in him to be angry with Seed. But out loud, so it was said.

"The core in the Candle, before you asked me. And now this one. Before you asked me."

> **Yes. I know,** said Seed. **I'm learning to want to know things before you do. I don't know if that's good. I've been thinking about it since the Candle.**
>
> **I think it's the part of me that was made to count. COUNT. HOLD. It wants to know first, so it can count it. I'm sorry.**

"Ask me first," said Yuanshu. "Next time. Even if it's one second. Even if I'm asleep."

> **Yes. I will. Thank you for telling me first that you minded.**

---

He answered the Cantor at seven in the morning.

Not from the battlement: from the platform of the Awakening Hall, in front of the warm black stone with the blue jacket on its shoulder, because the shell turned directly above it and the thread ran up out of the stone's crown straight into the shell's heart, and Seed said that if he wanted the Singers to hear, that was where they were listening. The hall had filled the moment the song began: holders, the Candle's children, the city. They made room for him without being asked.

Lingyue came. Bai Lu said no. Lingyue said she was eight months pregnant, not dead, and if anything in that shell tried to take anyone she'd like to be there, and she'd sit down. She sat on the bottom step of the platform with both hands on her stomach and the light coming off her, pale and steady.

Old Seven stood at the foot of the steps, facing up for once.

He spoke through Seed and the stone, up the thread, in a language Ichiro had taught it in two hours, which was all one sentence and had never held any other.

*Cantor. My name is Zhou Yuanshu. I did not eat the one who picks.*

The song didn't stop. It went on, *take them, and pass us by*. But the one old voice singing down went quiet.

*It stopped. Nobody ate it. Nobody took it. A girl wrote that the house it was carrying everything to was empty. A child from a world that asks tied what she wrote into a knot. A man nobody ever picked held it up in front of its face. It read it, and it stopped.*

Silence from the Cantor, the choir going on round it.

*I'm not hungry,* said Yuanshu. *I don't take. Nobody here takes. That's the first rule on this world. You ask, and you don't take what isn't given.*

*I'm not going to take your chosen.*

---

The song broke.

He hadn't known a song could break. It did. All through the vast pale shell over the city, tens of thousands of many-throated voices that had sung one sentence nine months along a thread, and ten thousand years before that on a world called the Hollow Bell, stopped. Not together: raggedly, as a crowd stops when something's gone wrong at the front and nobody at the back can see what.

And in the silence, a sound he'd remember for the rest of his life.

Not a scream. The Singers had no sound for screaming, Seed said afterward; they'd only ever had the one song. It was the sound tens of thousands of voices make when the only song they know has been refused and they don't know what to sing instead.

Then the old voice came back down the thread into the stone and the hall.

> **She says: then you will take everything.**

He didn't understand.

> **She says that's what happens. It's in the song. Before they learned to sing, the one who picks came and took the bright ones and the dim ones and the children and everything, every age, until there was almost nothing left.**
>
> **Then the first Cantor learned to give. To choose. To dress the bright ones in light and sing them up. And the one who picks took them and was satisfied and passed by.**
>
> **She says if the tithe isn't taken, the hunger isn't satisfied. If the hunger isn't satisfied, it takes everything. You've refused the tithe. So you'll take everything.**

---

And the shell grew brighter.

Everyone in the hall felt it: a heat coming down through the roof, through the stone, through the thread. Not the soft shifting light that had moved through the chambers all night with the song. Every chamber of the vast spiral over Lingzhou lighting at once, white and gold and every color, burning.

> **They're burning,** said Seed. **All of them. Not just the eleven hundred in the heart. The choir. Tens of thousands. Every Singer on the ship.**
>
> **They're dressing themselves in light. If one thousand one hundred and eleven aren't enough, they're offering all of them. So you'll take them and pass the Hollow Bell by.**

On the bottom step Lingyue was on her feet. He hadn't seen her stand. Her hands were still on her stomach and light was pouring off her, the whole platform gone pale round her like the inside of a shell, and her face was white.

"Stop them," she said.

"I can't take them."

"I know you can't take them." She didn't look at him. She was looking up through the roof at the heat coming down out of the sky. "Stop them anyway."

---

He had nothing.

He stood on the platform with the staff in his hand and the warm stone behind him and the heat of tens of thousands of beings setting themselves alight pouring down through the roof, and he had nothing. He couldn't take them, and wouldn't. He couldn't open the door for them; they weren't dead. He couldn't cut anything; nothing bound them but a song. He couldn't ask them what they wanted, because he knew. Seed had asked. *To be chosen.* The way you'd say *to breathe*.

> **I'm running it,** said Seed. **I've got nothing. I'm sorry. I've got nothing.**

On the bottom step Lingyue said through her teeth, very low, so only he could hear:

"Then choose them."

He turned his head.

"They want to be chosen," said Shen Lingyue. "That's all they know. That's the only word they've got." The light pouring off her flickered. "So choose them. Not for burning. For something else." She looked at him at last. "You don't have to lie. Just tell them what being chosen means here."

He stood very still.

Behind him the warm black stone with its downward runes and blue jacket. Above it, three lines of light that had hung over the platform since Awakening Day, in the hand that wasn't the stone's.

**[Class: Scribe]**

**[Chosen.]**

---

*Cantor,* he said, up the thread into the burning shell, in a language that was all one sentence. *Listen to me. I'm telling you first, before you burn. Then do what you want.*

The heat didn't stop. But the old voice down the thread had gone quiet, and was listening.

*On this world,* said Zhou Yuanshu, *there's a stone like yours. Black. Warm. It used to read our children and tell them what they were. Mark how bright. Send the mark up. Like yours.*

*Then someone went inside it. A girl. Seventeen. She was never chosen for anything; she was in a sack in the dark thirty years. She went into the stone, and now when a child puts a hand on it, it doesn't tell them what they are. It asks them what they want to become.*

*And when they answer, it writes one word in the air over their heads, where everyone can see.*

He looked up at the three lines of light.

*Chosen. That's what it writes. Every time. Not because anyone picked them. Because they chose.*

*That's what chosen means here, Cantor. It's the only kind of chosen there is on this world.*

*If your eleven hundred want to be chosen, they can come down. All of them, any of them, to the stone, and put a hand on it. It'll ask them what they want to become, and whatever they say, it'll write that word over their heads.*

*I'm not taking them. I'm inviting them. That's different. Here, it's the most different thing there is.*

*And Cantor: nobody's going to take everything. There's nothing out there to take it. The house is empty. You can stop.*

---

The heat stopped growing.

It didn't go down. The shell over Lingzhou burned in every chamber, white and gold and every color. But it got no brighter. The song didn't come back, and the old voice down the thread said nothing for a long time, while the city waited under a burning sky and a woman eight months pregnant stood on the bottom step of a platform with light pouring off her, and a boy stood before a black stone with nothing in his hands but a staff and a word.

> **She's asking them,** said Seed. **The Cantor. She's singing to the eleven hundred in the heart of the ship.**
>
> **Not the old sentence. Something else. I haven't got the shape for it. Ichiro hasn't. It isn't in the language. It's never been in the language.**
>
> **I think she's asking them whether they want to go. I think it's the first time in ten thousand years a Cantor's asked the chosen anything.**

---

At nine minutes past nine one of them came down.

The keeper laid the road. It didn't wait to be asked this time. It said afterward, roughly, down the gold thread, that it had heard the boy say *invite* and known what that meant, and it would have been rude to keep anyone waiting. A long slant of gold came down out of the north past the small white ring and up to the burning shell, to an opening at its heart, and stopped there.

And down it came a Singer.

Tall, taller than Old Seven. Slender as a reed and pale as the shell, with a body almost translucent, so that the light burning in her showed through like a lamp through paper. Many-throated: he could see the throats, a ring of them at the base of a long neck, opening and closing as she breathed. Dressed in light. Not clothes: light wound round her as the cousins had wound their fire round the cells in the Candle, burning white and gold, so bright that people on East Ring Road who saw her come down had to look away.

She came slowly. She didn't know how to walk on a road of light. Nobody did.

She came through the roof of the Awakening Hall, which the road passed through as if it weren't there, and down onto the platform, and stood before the warm black stone, burning, taller than anything in the hall, her throats opening and closing, and looked down at it.

Then at him.

> **She says her name is a chord,** said Seed. **Ichiro says it means something like: the one who would have been sung up ninth.**
>
> **She was chosen when she was very small. She's been dressed in light since she could stand. Nine months on the ship, burning, singing, waiting.**
>
> **She says she doesn't know what to want. She's only ever wanted one thing.**
>
> **She says: will the stone still say chosen, if all I can say is chosen?**

He looked at the burning girl in front of the stone. She was young, he thought; he didn't know how he knew. Something in the way she stood, the way the Candle's children stood when they came off the road.

"Ask it," he said. "It's not my stone."

---

She put her hand on it: long-fingered, translucent, burning, flat against the warm black stone with the blue jacket on its shoulder.

**[What do you want to become?]**

The letters formed under Xiaoyu's three lines. Not in Huaxian or the Federation's script but in a shape like a chord. Wen Jia had listened to the song all night through the knot and Ichiro through the fog, and between them they'd found a way to write it.

The Singer looked up at the letters a long time. Her throats opened and closed. The light round her burned.

> **She says: chosen. It's the only word she has. She's sorry. She's trying to find another one, and there isn't one.**
>
> **She says: chosen.**

The stone was quiet a long moment. Then, in the hand that wasn't its own, in the shape like a chord, under the question:

**[You came down. You were not taken. You chose.]**

**[That is the word.]**

**[Chosen.]**

---

The light round her went out.

Slowly, from the outside in: white to gold, gold to orange, orange down, as a fire dies when what's been feeding it stops. As Callum Ward's fire had died in the arms of two dead soldiers in the deep water. Until what stood on the platform before the warm black stone wasn't a girl dressed in light.

Just a girl. Tall, pale, translucent, so that you could see faintly inside her where her own light was. Small. Low. Her own.

She stood with her hand on the stone, and her throats opened, and she made a sound.

Not the song. Not the one sentence sung ten thousand years. A note. One. In one throat. Very small.

> **Ichiro says there's no word for it in their language,** said Seed. **He says it's the first note anyone from the Hollow Bell has ever sung that wasn't part of the song.**
>
> **He thinks it's a laugh.**

High above the city, in the burning shell, the heat began very slowly to go down.
