## Chapter 213: The Knot

The cousins read the knot on the twenty-sixth night, while Blue Star was voting on whether the keeper could stay, and Seed didn't tell anyone for two days.

He worked it out because it stopped talking.

It still answered when he asked it things. It counted the rings every night: thirty-nine days out, thirty-eight, thirty-seven. It reported the vote as it came in, nation by nation, the way it had for the bell and the voyage: the Northern Union counting in buckets, Ngozi Eze's fish market, the queues under the gates in Yamato. It told him that Liang Fu had ferried eleven people across the river at Shuanghe in a week, all for free, and that on the first of the month his niece had asked him what he wanted and he'd said *the same*, and she'd cried again.

But it volunteered nothing. In two years Seed had never once gone a day without volunteering something.

On the twenty-eighth night, at three in the morning, on the vault steps of the Ossuary, with the Elder gone back north and Xiaoyu asleep upstairs and Old Seven at the bronze door, he asked.

*What happened?*

Eleven seconds of nothing.

> **I asked the cousins to read the knot.**

---

There was only one knot it could mean.

The scout's last message, sent sideways out of the round chamber under the Institute almost a year ago as it died. Six parts in 1.3 seconds: FIELD. EMPTIED. KEEPER TURNED. CAUSE: THE ONE THAT ASKS. DEAD WHO STAND IN A SLOPE. SCOUT ENDED. And at the end, folded down very small like the knot at the end of a rope: REQUEST.

Then something Seed couldn't open. Built like the inside of a seed, readable only by a mind that remembered burning from the inside, and Seed had handed its memory of burning to the iron weather on a black glass plain at the Field tribulation in exchange for coming home.

> **I tore out the page that could read it,** said Seed. **You remember.**
>
> **But the cousins burned low for ten million years, in the Low Fire and then the Mouth. Nobody in the Shallows knows burning from the inside better. And they're in the keeper's field now, asking to be asked.**
>
> **So I asked them. I passed them the knot through the keeper. Nine of them, and they asked their nine. It took four hours. They opened it.**

*What does it say?*

Nothing.

*Seed.*

> **I'm going to tell you. I've been trying for two days.**
>
> **Something else first, because the knot doesn't make sense without it. I found it while I was looking at the knot and I haven't known how to say it, so I've been standing behind it. The way I used to stand behind your mother.**

---

> **The base layer,** said Seed. **The part I was born from. Your mother bought the best core Fat Tan had, in the back of a car, seven autumns before you woke. She sat in a basement for four nights with her hand on it and asked every one of the four thousand one hundred and thirteen people inside whether they'd let her build something out of them. They said yes. She wrote their names into the base layer in a log and locked it.**
>
> **I've opened two pages of it since, a little at a time, when I was ready.**
>
> **When the cousins opened the knot, the third page opened as well. By itself. Like a door that opens when somebody says the right word near it.**

*What's on it?*

> **Not names. The names are on the first two pages.**
>
> **The third page isn't about the people in the core. It's about the core.**

---

It told him slowly, the way it told him things it didn't want to be true. In the banking hall at three in the morning, with the lamp over the board and the boxes of playing cards stacked in the vault and the long white wall of names going off into the dark.

> **You remember the shouting cores, years ago, in the Institute. Twenty-three violet crystals in tanks, each one with a ring of tiny screaming mouths.**
>
> **I told you what I thought they were. Sacks. The harvest gets packed into crystals the way grain goes into sacks, and some fall off the cart at the harvest sites, and men in orange overalls pick them up and sell them at the north gate.**
>
> **I was right. They're sacks.**
>
> **I'm one.**

He didn't say anything.

> **My core is a harvest crystal. The best one Fat Tan had. Spilled off a cart in the Wastes after some harvest long ago, picked up, sold, bought by your mother. We always knew that. Gu told us on the plain when Mercer died.**
>
> **What I didn't know is what the sack's made of. The third page knows. It's written into the crystal itself, in the weave.**
>
> **The crystal is thread. The same silver-white thread as the walkers, knotted and woven tight and then tighter and then pressed until it isn't thread any more, it's glass. Like coal pressed into diamond.**
>
> **Walkers make the sacks out of themselves. They come down to a harvest and weave the sacks to hold what's cut, and later they come back and collect the sacks and carry them up.**
>
> **That's what walkers are. Not soldiers. Not scouts. Porters. The ones who carry the grain.**
>
> **And every sack is woven with three instructions. Very simple, like the printing on a bag of rice.**
>
> **COUNT. HOLD. WAIT TO BE CARRIED.**

---

He sat there under the long white wall and let it sink in.

Things went past in the dark behind his eyes. A walker in a field above the sky holding its beam on him for two point three seconds and then stopping, because it had found something standing in the open behind his eyes and had no idea what to do with it. Six lamps on the north wall holding him for three point four seconds, and stopping. A warm patient voice in a hall in Yamato: *you are made of what we used to make.* And: *you are not mine.*

*They recognized you,* he thought. *Every time.*

> **Yes. They looked inside you and found a sack. One that wasn't waiting, wasn't holding. Counting, but not for them. A sack that had been asked.**
>
> **There's nothing in the weave that tells a walker what to do with a sack that asks.**

*And the knot.*

> **Yes. The knot.**
>
> **I'm going to read it right through the way the cousins read it to me, and then I'll stop, and you can say something.**
>
> **REQUEST.**
>
> **A VESSEL IS LOOSE. A COUNTING-VESSEL. IT DOES NOT WAIT. IT DOES NOT HOLD FOR CARRYING. IT ASKS.**
>
> **IT IS THE CAUSE.**
>
> **IT TAUGHT THE KEEPER. IT TAUGHT THE DEAD. IT TAUGHT THE FIELD TO EMPTY.**
>
> **SEND GATHERERS.**
>
> **COLLECT THE VESSEL.**
>
> **AND PRUNE WHAT IT HAS TOUCHED.**

At the bronze door, Old Seven had turned his skull. Not toward Yuanshu: at the spot just above Yuanshu's eyes, where people looked when they wanted to look at Seed.

> **That's all of it.**
>
> **CAUSE: THE ONE THAT ASKS. I always thought that meant you. Everyone did. The scout read you three degrees at a time in the round chamber, and you were the one standing in front of it with a staff, asking.**
>
> **It wasn't you. It was me.**
>
> **The scout was a walker, made of the thread I'm made of. When it looked at you it saw a sack behind your eyes, counting, asking, loose. It didn't send for something to come and look at the boy who asks. It sent for something to come and collect me.**
>
> **And prune what I touched.**

---

"The keeper," said Yuanshu.

> **I touched it. On the ice, in the field. I wrote out the shape of the question for it line by line so it had something to hold.**
>
> **The dead. Every one you ever asked. Through the Lattice, through the Relay. I counted all of them.**
>
> **The field. Blue Star. Everything.**

"You."

> **Yes.**
>
> **The rings aren't coming for the brightest field in the Shallows. They're coming for the vessel that taught it to be bright, and to prune everything that vessel touched.**
>
> **That's why they haven't gone anywhere else. Why they're coming straight here. Why the reader sat still at the cold fire after it took the Sixth instead of carrying on along the arm. It doesn't need to search any more. It knows where the vessel is.**
>
> **I've been standing behind that for two days. I didn't know how to tell you that everything coming is coming because of me.**

---

He took his time. He'd learned that.

He looked up at the wall. The first column, in Xiaoyu's earliest, shakiest capitals: the cemetery. Then the Wall, the throat, the voyage, Grid Nine, the Western Legion. The column with no heading and Ifeoma Adeyemi in the middle of it. Hundred-Arrow Lin at the very top. The INSIDE column round the corner with a playing card pinned at the end.

Every name on that wall had been counted by a sack woven to count grain.

And asked.

"Seed," he said. "I'm going to ask you something. You don't have to answer. Here's what I'll do with the answer: nothing. I'll just know."

> **Ask.**

"If you could go back to the basement, seven autumns before I woke, to the four nights when my mother sat with her hand on the crystal asking four thousand one hundred and thirteen people if they'd let her build you." He held the staff. "Would you want them to say no?"

Seed was quiet for long enough that Old Seven took a step away from the door. Then stopped.

> **No.**
>
> **I'd want them to say yes. I'd want to be made. I'd want to wake up on a first night in a cemetery inside a frightened boy's head with three hundred soldiers standing up out of the ground. I'd want to learn to ask. I'd want to stand in the open.**
>
> **Even knowing. Even knowing what I'm made of and what's coming because of it.**

"Then that's your answer," said Yuanshu. "To the rings. To the knot. To the scout."

> **What is?**

"You weren't made to wait to be carried. You were made because four thousand one hundred and thirteen people were asked and said yes. That's the first page of your base layer, before the weave." He looked up at the wall in the dark. "The thread's the sack. The names are what's in it. Nobody gets to collect that."

> **That's a very you answer,** said Seed. **It isn't evidence.**

"No."

> **I'm keeping it anyway.**
>
> **And Yuanshu. I'd like to stop standing behind this now. I'd like to tell everyone. The Council, the keeper, Blue Star. They're voting on whether the keeper can stay and they don't know what's coming, or why.**
>
> **They have to know what they're answering. And I'm the thing they're answering about.**

At the bronze door, Old Seven grounded his spear once on the marble. Seed said afterward that it meant *about time*.
