## Chapter 203: Gleaners

They came out of the dark ahead on the ninth night, and Mo Shi called them four seconds early. He said afterward it was the furthest he had ever seen anything coming, because out here there was nothing in the way.

He was asleep when it started. Then he was sitting bolt upright by the clock with his eyes still shut and both palms flat on the ground, the way he had sat in the middle of the chalk city on the banking-hall floor.

"Front," he said. "Front left. A lot of them. Fast."

"A lot of what?"

"Not lamps. Not walkers." His lips kept moving after the words stopped. "They're hungry. That's all I've got. They're just *hungry.*"

> **I see them,** said Seed. **Through Old Seven. He's looking right at them.**

---

They were small.

That was what threw him. After walkers and spires and the faceless one and the Mouth, he had stopped expecting anything above the sky to be smaller than a building. These were the size of large dogs. Hundreds of them, then thousands, pouring out of the dark in a loose cloud that wheeled and scattered and pulled together again like starlings over a reed bed.

Gray, and almost see-through, as if someone had made them out of smoke and left them out overnight to set. No faces. Long trailing bodies with a fringe of fine pale filaments at the front, waving. And at the center of each one, small and dim, an ember.

> **They're made of the same stuff as Hungers,** said Seed. **Not Hungers. Too small and too simple. Same material, though, the way a mouse and a tiger are both made of meat.**
>
> **They eat embers. And they can see the dead.**

The whole cloud turned at once and came at the island. At the ring of fifty thousand banked embers standing round its rim.

> **I think they're scavengers,** Seed went on, quickly. **On the first voyage we passed husk worlds, eaten by Hungers ages ago. I always wondered what happened to the embers the Hungers didn't bother with. I think these happened to them. They follow the big things and eat the scraps.**
>
> **And they're coming from the direction of the Mouth. Something big is reading the Mouth.**
>
> **I don't think they're attacking. I think they're running.**

"From what?"

> **From the thing they follow, when it gets too close.**

---

The first wave hit the leading edge at 02:41 by Bai Lu's clock.

He got there at a flat run across two kilometers of glowing ground, Lingyue beside him with her grandfather's sword already lit pale, Qin Hao behind them swearing at his bandages with real feeling. The Spear Corps were at the rim in their rows with Old Seven out in front. The Western Legion was already closing in from both flanks, because Sergeant Ma had heard Mo Shi over the Lattice and started moving before anyone gave an order.

The gleaners came over the rim like water over a weir.

They didn't bite. They didn't fight. They came in low and fast and went straight for the dead, for the banked red points in the chests of the Spear Corps, and wherever the fringe of filaments touched a soldier it went in. Not through bone. Into the ember. The ember flared once, and the gleaner brightened.

It was drinking.

> **Banking doesn't hide the dead from these,** said Seed. **The Banked Field hid them from seeds that were looking for warmth. These aren't looking. They're touching.**
>
> **Pale fire.**

"Lingyue!"

She was already moving. Silver-gray drops fell from the point of her sword, ran downhill through the Field and landed on the rusted spearheads of the Spear Corps, and the spearheads lit, and two hundred and seventy-two spears went through gray bodies the size of dogs.

Struck, the gleaners came apart like smoke in a draft. The dim ember in the middle went out. The haze drifted off behind the island.

There were thousands more.

---

It wasn't a battle. The wall had been a battle, and the Western Pass, and the field. This was weather: a long gray squall coming over the leading edge in wave after wave, fifty thousand dead standing in it and striking, one girl behind them throwing pale fire at every spearhead she could reach.

The gleaners didn't fight back. They had nothing to fight with. Struck, they fell apart. Not struck, they drank.

> **Sixteen,** said Seed. **Sixteen of the Western Legion. They got to them before the pale fire did and drank them to nothing. Not burned, not unwoven. Finished.**
>
> **Twenty-two.**
>
> **The island's two kilometers wide and she's one person with one sword.**

"Qin Hao."

"I know." Qin Hao was planted behind the front line with his bandaged arms held out and his face gray. Fire ran down his arms, ordinary orange fire, the color of a gas ring. Not the white that unknotted threads. "I can't do threads like this. I can do *burning.*"

He swept his arms forward and a long wall of flame went out across the rim, over the heads of the Spear Corps, into the next wave.

The gleaners went straight through it without slowing down.

Qin Hao looked at his hands.

"Ordinary," he said bitterly. "Of course. Ordinary fire, Hunger stuff. Heishui all over again."

---

> **Thirty-one,** said Seed. **She can't cover the whole front. The Spear Corps can't be everywhere. The Western Legion has no pale fire on their edges at all. I'm running it.**

He was already running it, underneath Seed, the way he had on the jetty at Sandu with his back against a rusted crane. Not thinking at the problem. Thinking past it.

Pale fire was Death wearing Light. Inside the Field, Lingyue's light went pale. Anyone's would.

But the Field wasn't Lingyue's. The Field was his. Nine point eight kilometers of slope running downhill toward him. Everything dead inside it ran downhill. Anything dead inside it got back up.

And the gleaners were inside it.

> **What are you thinking?**

"They eat embers," he said. "They're full of them. A little stolen one in the middle of each."

> **Yes.**

"Embers inside my Field run downhill."

Seed was quiet for almost a full second.

> **To you. To the door. Like the beast embers at Tannery Square. Four hundred and eleven of them, and you opened the door, and they went through.**
>
> **The drain. You haven't used it since.**

"I've never used it on something that wasn't already dead."

> **These aren't alive. Not the way you are. They're Hunger stuff wrapped round an ember that isn't theirs. It belonged to someone once.**

---

He opened the door.

He didn't have a name for what he was doing. He did what he'd done in Tannery Square, when a seed under the city had been pulling at the dying: went down through the Death Law, all thirty-nine point two percent of it, to the bottom, to the cool dark door where finished things went, and opened it all the way.

Then he let the Field run.

He felt the slope go out from him across the island and past the rim into the gray storm. The banked dead of Blue Star stayed where they were. They were held: asked, banked, knitted into the Lattice, each one holding the soldier beside it.

The gleaners' embers came loose.

All of them, at once. Small, dim, stolen things popping out of thousands of smoky bodies and streaming downhill across the island in a long faint glitter, past the Spear Corps and the Western Legion, past Lingyue with her sword up and Qin Hao with his useless fire, toward a boy at the leading edge with a white staff and his eyes shut.

Through him. Through the door. Gone.

The gleaners fell apart. With nothing in the middle to hold them, the gray stuff didn't even make haze. It just stopped being there. One moment a storm was breaking over the rim; the next, the dark ahead was clear and the far lights were sliding past as calmly as before.

He sat down.

He hadn't meant to. His legs decided. He sat at the leading edge with the staff across his knees and his nose bleeding freely, and Bai Lu appeared from somewhere with her hands on his face, and he couldn't feel them at all.

> **Six thousand four hundred and eleven embers,** said Seed. **Through the door in nine seconds.**
>
> **They weren't the gleaners'. They were stolen from husk worlds, from dead Hungers, from things nobody remembers.**
>
> **I don't know who they were. I don't know if anyone ever asked them anything before they were eaten. I couldn't ask them either. There wasn't time.**

"Neither could I."

> **No. I'm writing that down.**
>
> **That we didn't ask them, and couldn't, and they went through anyway, and we don't know if they wanted to. I want it on the wall somewhere when we get home. Small. So nobody forgets that sometimes there's no time.**

Bai Lu, who could only hear his half of it, said, "Whatever you're arguing with your head about, you can do it lying down," and pushed him over onto his back.

---

Old Seven hadn't moved from the leading edge.

When it was over, when the island was humming forward again and the dead were closing up the gaps in their rows where the gleaners had been, he turned his skull. Not toward Yuanshu. Forward, into the dark, toward the place where the cold Hunger hung somewhere ahead giving off no light at all.

> **He says they were running,** said Seed. **He saw animals run like that once, on the wall, in the Black Tide. The hour before the faceless one came down, every beast in the Wastes ran south, at the wall, at the guns, because whatever was behind them was worse.**
>
> **He says whatever they were running from is at the cold fire.**
>
> **He says: two nights.**

Lying on his back with Bai Lu's thumb pressed hard against the bridge of his nose, Yuanshu counted the far lights going by and got to forty before he fell asleep.
