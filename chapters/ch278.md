## Chapter 278: The Lamp in the Lens

> **The Lens is a stone,** said Seed. **The oldest one. The first that fell, on the first human world, ten thousand years ago. Every other stone in every human orchard was cut from it, or from stones cut from it, or from stones cut from those. The Federation doesn't know that. I think the House of Severin does.**
>
> **It isn't a stone like ours. It doesn't read one child at a time with a hand on it. It was ground into a lens so it could read a whole world at once. Everyone. Everything that breathes. In one night.**
>
> **It reads everything at once because it's the root. Every stone cut from it is still a little part of it. Every cell in every wall of every ship. Every face on every chain round every throat in the Federation. When it reads, it reads through all of them.**

He sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees, and the banking hall was full, and nobody said anything.

> **On the ninth night the Fleet Marshal will turn it on Blue Star, and it'll read eight billion people and mark them.**
>
> **Unless there's somebody in it.**
>
> **Wen Jia went into our stone, and it stopped reading and started asking. The Old Chord will go into the stones of the Hall of Throats. If somebody goes into the Lens, then on the ninth night, when the Fleet Marshal turns it on Blue Star, it won't read.**
>
> **It'll ask. Eight billion people, at once. What do you want to become?**

Gao Min had put down her thermos.

> **And because it's the root, because every cell in every wall of every ship is cut from it, whoever's in the Lens could ask them too. All of them at once, through the layer that stops their ears. Not as a sound: as a mark, the way Seri read Tam's.**
>
> **Four million one hundred and eighty thousand, in one night.**
>
> **That's the idea.**

---

"And whoever goes in," said Su Mingli flatly, "doesn't come back."

> **No. The Lens is in the bow of the Severin flagship. Whoever goes into it will be in the Fleet Marshal's ship, in his House's oldest treasure, at the heart of the Ladder. When he understands what's happened he'll try to take them out, or break the Lens. I don't know if he can. Nobody's ever tried to break the first stone.**
>
> **Wen Jia can leave her stone. The door's always a little open; she only has to ask. Whoever goes into the Lens may not be able to reach the door. They'll be very far away in a ship that folds, and I don't know where the ship will go.**
>
> **They may be in the Lens a very long time. I don't want it to be anyone.**

"It has to be someone," said Lingyue from the stairs. "Or two billion people get collected."

> **I know. I still don't want it to be anyone.**

---

"Who?" said Yuanshu.

> **Someone on the net. Someone who can go along the threads sideways from our stone into the Lens, the way the eleven went into the stones. Someone who isn't alive, or not alive the way you are. The living can't go into a stone. The base layer can. The dead can, I think. Minds that are kept, or were.**
>
> **And someone who can ask. Not just sit in a knot and hold. Ask eight billion people at once, and four million in the walls, all in one night.**
>
> **Someone very good at talking.**

He went very still.

> **I didn't want to say it. I haven't asked him and I'm not going to. I'm going to tell you who I thought of, and then you decide whether anyone asks him, and who.**
>
> **He was quiet for nine hundred years. He came home six months ago. His sister lit a lamp for him on the shore.**
>
> **He talks to fish.**

---

He flew to Yamato at dawn.

Alone. He told Lingyue he wanted to ask himself, and she looked at him a long moment from the stairs with Xiaodeng asleep against her chest and said *yes, obviously, go*, and then *be back by tonight or I'm coming after you with the baby and the sword and you can explain that to Bai Lu*. Old Seven didn't ask. He was at the top of the Sector Nine stairs on the north wall when Yuanshu got there, facing east, and he didn't step onto any road. He said through Seed that he'd wait; this wasn't a thing a captain could go out ahead of the line for. He said: *go and ask the fog-ringer. Tell him I said he was always too fast in the middle.*

He came down at Shiomi bay at noon.

The fog lay on the water as it always did now, white and still, standing off the end of the old breakwater from the point to the shore as fog doesn't stand in noon sun. The town's fishermen had stopped minding it. They rowed round it. They said it was lucky; they said the fish had been better since it came.

And at the end of the breakwater, sitting cross-legged on the stones with a lamp in her lap as she'd sat every day and night for six months: Sister Kiyo of Shiomi.

She didn't look up when he landed.

> **She says you think very loudly. You thought loudly all the way across the sea.**
>
> **She says he heard you coming. He's been talking since dawn. He says he knows what you want.**
>
> **She says: sit down. You're making the fog nervous.**

---

He sat on the stones beside her.

The lamp in her lap burned pale and steady, the color of whale oil in fog: Lingyue's light, held in the dry cupped hands of a nun of twelve hundred years. The fog stood close round them, and in it the small dim lights of a village, three hundred and eleven, home.

*Ichiro,* he thought. *I came to tell you something. And then I'm going to ask you. Or not. I don't know yet.*

> **He says you came to ask him to go into the Lens.**
>
> **Tam told the Candle's children, and the Candle's children told the city, and the city told Ren, and Ren told the monks, and the monks told Kiyo, and Kiyo told him. He says news goes faster than the net. It always did, in Shiomi.**
>
> **He says: yes.**

*I haven't asked.*

> **He says he knows. He's saying it anyway, so you don't have to.**
>
> **He says you asked the walker. You asked the rings. You asked the tree. You asked a thousand children who only knew one word. You asked a lady from the Ladder whether she'd asked her daughter. You asked a stone.**
>
> **He says you don't have to ask him. He's telling you first. That's your rule; he learned it. He's good at rules when they're about talking.**

He sat on the stones and said nothing for a long time.

*It's in his ship,* he thought. *The Fleet Marshal's. In the bow. You'd be in the heart of the Ladder. He'd try to get you out, or break it. And the ship folds, and I don't know where it would go. You might never reach the door. You might be in the Lens a thousand years.*

> **He says he was in a column three hundred years and in the deep water nine hundred. Quiet. He says you've no idea how quiet. Him. Quiet. Nine hundred years.**
>
> **A thousand years in a Lens talking to everyone at once? He says that sounds like the best job anybody ever had.**

---

Sister Kiyo hadn't said anything.

She sat with the lamp in her lap and her paper face turned to the fog, listening to her brother, and he understood that she'd known since dawn, since Tam told the Candle's children, since before. That she'd sat at the end of the breakwater six months with a lamp in her lap beside the brother she'd waited twelve hundred years for, knowing he'd never in his life be able to stay quiet when there was something to say.

*Sister,* he said.

> **She says he talks too much. He always did. When he was small he talked to the fish. When he was taken he talked to himself in the column three hundred years so he wouldn't forget how. When everyone in the deep water said be quiet, he rang the bell anyway, because he saw a keeper learn to ask.**
>
> **She says he's the only person in Shiomi who can't ring a boat call properly.**
>
> **She says: of course it's him.**

Her dry hands moved on the lamp.

> **She says: one condition.**

*Anything.*

> **She says: I am coming too.**

---

He didn't understand at first. Then he did.

> **She says a Lens is a thing for seeing. He's going to sit in it and talk to eight billion people and four million in the walls. He'll talk all night. He won't stop. He never stops.**
>
> **She says somebody should hold a lamp, so that when they're asked, in the dark, in the walls, with their ears stopped, they can see where the shore is.**
>
> **She says that was always her job. She sat on the shore and held the lamp and he rowed in. Every night for twenty-two years, until the night he didn't.**
>
> **She says she's not sitting on the shore again while he goes out.**

She lifted the lamp in her dry cupped hands: Lingyue's light, pale, steady, held.

> **She says she's been dead twelve hundred years. She's not afraid of a ship.**
>
> **She says: ask Lingyue if she may take her light. It's hers. She's only holding it.**

---

He rang Lingyue from the breakwater. She answered on the first ring; she'd been holding the phone.

He told her all of it: Ichiro, the Lens, Kiyo, the lamp.

She said nothing for a long time. At the other end, very small, Xiaodeng made a sound, and he heard Lingyue's hand settle on her back.

"Tell her yes," said Shen Lingyue. "Tell her it was always hers. I just lit it." A pause. "Tell her: make it bright enough that they can see it. But not like the wall. Not everything at once. Just enough."

"She knows."

"Tell her anyway," said his wife. "So it's said."

> **She says thank you,** said Seed. **She says tell Lingyue she held it up nine hours for a thousand children of the Hollow Bell, and then couldn't put it down, and then the baby came. Tell her the whole of Yamato felt it, under the gates. The monks woke up.**
>
> **She says: tell her I won't hold it for nine hours. I'll hold it as long as it takes.**

---

The headman's bell rang at dusk.

Once: the slow deep one, the bell that had told three hundred and eleven people to be quiet nine hundred years ago in the deep water when the gleaners came looking for what had fallen.

Then every bell in Shiomi rang. Not the warning, not the name, not the boat calls. Something else. Ichiro said through Seed that it hadn't been rung since before the cutting. It had no name. It was what the shore rang when a boat went out that wasn't coming back. Not grief; not quite. The sound a village makes on a shore when it knows exactly where one of its boats is going, and why, and is proud.

> **He says they're ringing him out. He never thought they would. He always thought he'd be the one ringing someone else out. He was going to be very good at it.**
>
> **He says tell the Fleet Marshal he's coming. Tell him he's going to talk to everyone on his ship: his engines and his crew and his Lens and his House. He's not going to stop, and the Marshal had better get comfortable.**
>
> **He says tell Old Seven he knows he was always too fast in the middle.**
>
> **This time he's going to take it slow.**

---

> **Four days,** said Seed. **They'll go along the threads on the eighth night. From our stone, through Wen Jia, into the net, across, into the Lens. Ichiro and Kiyo and a lamp.**
>
> **I've run it. I don't know if it'll work. I've never run anything with a nun and a fog-ringer and a lamp in it.**
>
> **I'd like to stop being the one who doesn't know.**
>
> **I'd like it to work.**
