## Chapter 293: The Reading Morning

The reading-hall below the House of Anthos was older than the Assembly, and smaller, and it had been built so the children couldn't see the door.

He understood that as soon as he walked in. A round room of black stone cut down into the rock beneath the House, domed, windowless, with a single stair curving down into it so that by the time you reached the bottom you couldn't see where you'd come in. Tiers of stone benches round the walls for the families. A box above them, carved and gilded, for the House. And in the center, on a round platform, driven point-down into the floor, ten meters high: a stone.

Black. Warm. Its runes turned down.

And beside it, with her hand flat on it, a woman of sixty-one in deep red who'd been standing there since dawn.

---

It was the first reading morning since the Assembly.

Every year on the first day of the cold season, the children of Anthos who'd turned twelve came down the curving stair one at a time in white, put their hands on the stone, and were read and marked and given a face cut from the stone on a fine chain, while their families watched from the benches and the House from the box. Nine thousand years of it. Corvane had come down that stair. So had Iselde. So had Maelis, at twelve, and been marked E and given to the fold.

Today four hundred and six children of Anthos were on the stair.

The families were on the benches, as always. The box above them, carved and gilded, was empty.

Corvane Varr-Anthos had locked it. She said so when he came down the stair with Lingyue and the baby and Old Seven one-armed behind him, and Iselde, and Maelis holding her sister's hand. Anyone who wanted to watch the children from above could watch from the roof, she said, in the Federation's language without the grade, and didn't take her hand off the stone.

"He is quiet," she said, looking at the stone. "He has been quiet since dawn. I keep asking him if he is ready."

> **He says he's ready,** said Seed. **He's been ready since dawn. She keeps asking. He says tell her to stop asking and let them come down.**

"He says you can let them come down," said Yuanshu.

The Matriarch of the House of Anthos looked at the stone a long moment.

"He would," she said. "He was never patient." And then quietly to the stone, as you'd say it to someone standing beside you: "I am going to ask them first. Before you. That is what I said in the Assembly. You will have to wait your turn."

> **He says: I know. I've never had to wait my turn in my life. I'd like to try.**

---

The first child came down the stair at nine.

A boy of twelve in white. Thin, hair combed flat, hands clenched at his sides as children's hands had been clenched on reading mornings on every world of the Federation for nine thousand years. He came round the last curve and stopped at the bottom and looked at the stone, and the benches full of families, and the empty box above them, and then at the woman in deep red beside the stone with her hand on it.

He didn't know who she was, Seed said. He was from a street of tailors on the east side of the city, his family graded C, and he'd only ever seen a Matriarch on screens.

He started to kneel.

"Don't," said Corvane Varr-Anthos.

The boy stopped halfway down.

"Please," said the Matriarch, without the grade. "You do not have to. Stand up. I am going to ask you something. First I am going to tell you what I will do with the answer."

The boy stood up, very slowly, staring at her.

"Whatever you say, I will tell the stone. And then the stone will ask you too. There is someone in it now. He will ask what you want to become, and whatever you say, he will open that. Not what he reads. What you say." She didn't take her hand off the stone. "And if you do not want to touch it, you do not have to. You can go back up the stair. Nobody will mark you. Nobody will give you a face."

The boy stared at her.

"What do you want?" said the Matriarch of the House of Anthos.

---

He didn't answer at once, and nobody hurried him. The families on the benches didn't move. Lingyue stood at the foot of the stair with Xiaodeng awake in the sling, gazing round with the cloudy unfocused eyes of a two-month-old at a round room full of people. Old Seven stood at the bottom of the stair facing out, up it, toward four hundred and five children still waiting on the curve.

"I don't know," said the boy at last, very small. "Nobody said we'd be asked. They said the stone would tell us."

"That is all right," said Corvane. "Do you want to touch it?"

He looked at the stone.

"Is it going to tell me what I am?"

"No," said the Matriarch. "It is going to ask."

The boy looked at the black stone a long moment. Then he climbed the three steps slowly with his hands clenched, stopped before it beside the woman in red, and put his hand on it.

**[What do you want to become?]**

The letters formed in the air above the stone, in the reading-hall below the House of Anthos, in the Federation's script, in a hand that wasn't the stone's. Seed said it was a hand that had signed ten thousand orders and was learning, slowly, to write something else.

The boy looked up at them.

"I want to make coats," he said. "Like my mother. She makes the best coats on the street; everybody says." His voice went very small. "I was afraid it'd say I was too bright to make coats. My cousin was S. They took her to the Academy. She doesn't make coats."

The stone was quiet a moment.

**[Then make coats.]**

**[Chosen.]**

**[Talent: — not marked —]**

And under it, smaller, in the same hand that had signed ten thousand orders:

**[I'd like one. Gray. Like a C's. If you have time.]**

---

The boy took his hand off the stone and stood staring at the letters. Then he turned and looked at the benches, where a woman in a tailor's apron had stood up with both hands over her mouth.

He went down the steps and across the floor to her. Not running; he was twelve and in white on a reading morning, and nobody on Anthos had ever run on a reading morning. Walking. Fast.

And she caught him.

> **He says: one,** said Seed. **Four hundred and five to go. He says tell Corvane she asks them too slowly.**

"Tell him," said Corvane Varr-Anthos, her hand on the stone and her face doing what he'd seen it do in a courtyard on Blue Star when a woman in a wheelchair asked whether she'd asked her daughter anything, "that I am going to ask every one of them exactly that slowly. And so is he."

---

They came all morning and all afternoon.

Four hundred and six children of Anthos down the curving stair one at a time in white. At the foot of the stair a woman in deep red asked each of them first what they wanted, and told them they didn't have to touch the stone. Some didn't, and went back up the stair, and their families came down off the benches and met them at the top. Some did, and the stone asked them what they wanted to become, and they said.

*A healer. A pilot. Something that makes things grow. I don't know.*

*A dancer. My father says it isn't a real thing. The stone said it is.*

*I want to work in the yards, with the children who came out of the walls. I want to teach them to swim.*

*I don't know.*

*I want to be first.*

That too. Twice; Seed counted. And each time the stone was quiet a long while and then wrote, in the hand that had signed ten thousand orders: **[I was first for two hundred years. Ask me what it was like. Then decide.]**

Both of them, Seed said, stood with their hands on the stone and asked, and the stone told them. And neither chose *first* in the end. One chose *I don't know yet*. The other chose something Seed didn't translate, because the girl who said it asked it not to. Seed said it was the first time anyone had asked it not to translate something, and it had liked being asked.

---

At five in the afternoon, when the last of the four hundred and six had climbed the curving stair and the families with them, and the reading-hall was empty except for a woman in red with her hand on a stone, a boy with a staff, a woman with a sword and a baby, a one-armed dead captain, and a former Listener-Captain without a face, Maelis let go of her sister's hand.

She crossed the floor to the platform.

She didn't climb the steps. She stopped at the foot of them and looked at the stone: the stone that had read her at twelve, on a reading morning twelve years ago, with her mother in the box, and marked her E, so that the House gave her to the fold.

Her mother, beside the stone, didn't move.

"Mother," said Maelis.

Corvane Varr-Anthos looked down at her daughter at the foot of the steps, twelve in the body and twenty-three inside.

"You asked me what I want," said Maelis. "Ninety-one days. Ninety-four now. Every day. And on the seventy-first I told you." She looked at the stone. "I'd like to tell him too."

The Matriarch of the House of Anthos stood very still with her hand on the stone.

"He is in there," she said. "The First. Severin. He asked to be. He has been asking all day."

"I know," said Maelis. "I watched."

She climbed the steps and stood before the stone beside her mother and looked at it. Then she laid her hand on it: small, twelve years old, flat, beside her mother's.

**[What do you want to become?]**

Maelis Varr-Anthos looked up at the letters in the air above the stone that had marked her E.

"Thirteen," she said.

---

The stone was quiet a long time.

Seed said afterward it didn't know what happened in the stone in that time, and nor did anybody. It was between a girl who'd been twelve for twelve years and a man who'd been first for two hundred, inside a black stone below the House of Anthos where both had been read.

Then the letters came.

**[I can't open that. It isn't a Law. Nothing on the tree inside me makes anyone thirteen.]**

Maelis didn't take her hand off the stone.

**[But you don't need a stone for it.]**

**[You only need a year.]**

**[So I'm going to open nothing.]**

**[Chosen.]**

**[Talent: — not marked —]**

And under it, smaller, in the same hand that had signed ten thousand orders and one last one in a corridor, badly, like a word in a book:

**[Come back next year and tell me what thirteen was like.]**

**[I'll ask.]**
