## Chapter 228: Graft

The answer was in a courtyard full of potted plants two years back, and it was Jiang Lan who remembered it.

"You've done this before," said the dry voice from the staff at five in the morning, after Lingyue had said *cut it* and Hush had said *find where it drinks* and everyone in the banking hall had sat for an hour in silence staring at a gray tree on a laptop. "Young man. You have done exactly this before. Are you really going to make me say it?"

He didn't know what she meant, and said so.

"The courtyard behind this building. Your second night back from the stalk. Fat Tan's ridiculous potted plants. A sergeant with two arms and a captain with one. You knelt on the paving and asked the Death Law to do something nobody had ever asked it to do. Not raise. Not release. Give." A pause. "I told you the joint was wrong. I told you nobody had ever taught you anatomy. You did it anyway."

He stopped breathing for a second.

"Graft," he said.

"Graft," said the schoolteacher in the staff. "Thank you. I was starting to think the Lord realm had made you stupid."

> **The graft,** said Seed. **Bone to bone, ember to ember. Huo's arm onto Old Seven. Death Law 26.9 to 27.5.**
>
> **I ran it four thousand times before you did it. Most runs, the two embers fought over the arm like two dogs over a coat. In every run that worked, Huo let go first.**

Kwan spoke first, slowly, as if reading it off the inside of her own head. "A graft. On a tree."

"You cut a branch," said Lingyue, very still now. "And put something else on the cut. And the tree grows it."

"My grandma grafted plums," said Xiaoyu from the bottom step. "In the old flat. Onto a wild tree on the balcony. She said the wild tree couldn't tell the difference. It just grew whatever was on the end."

> **And the wild tree grows plums,** said Seed. **From that branch. For good. Not because it decided to. Because there's a plum on the end, and the tree feeds whatever's on the end.**

"So we cut the tip," said Lingyue. "The bit reaching for us."

"And put something on the cut," said Yuanshu, "that asks."

---

They worked on it for nine days.

Not the way Seed had worked on water at Port Abiri, or the walkers, or the knots. Those had been Seed's. This was everyone's. Kwan carried a stack of botany textbooks waist-high back from the university library and read about grafting aloud in the banking hall at two in the morning: scion and rootstock, cambium and callus, the thin green living layer under the bark where the two pieces have to meet or nothing takes. Xiaoyu dug her grandmother's old grafting knife out of a box in the vault, wrapped in newspaper from before the Black Tide, and put it on the long table, where nobody used it and everybody kept looking at it. Hush sat on the windowsill and, very occasionally, said something soft about the trees above the crack on its world, before the ring.

And Seed held the shape.

> **Three problems,** it said. **One: the cut. The branch isn't wood. It's thread, like the walkers and the rings and me. Pressed, grown, very thick near the tip.**
>
> **Pale fire cuts thread one strand at a time. Qin Hao's white fire unknots it. Lingyue's light can hold something down. None of that cuts a branch the size of a mountain range on its own.**
>
> **All of it together, plus fifty thousand dead who can walk up to things that can't see them, plus Su Mingli and Orlov: I've run that. It's the only tree plan I've run that comes out better than even.**

"Two?"

> **The scion. The thing we put on the cut. It has to be alive. It has to be made of something the tree can grow: thread, or a yes, or both. And it has to ask.**

Nobody needed to say what was alive and made of a yes and asked, and hung low over the end of the world every night in a gold haze.

> **Three. A graft only takes if the two pieces meet properly, the living layer to the living layer. Kwan read us that bit.**
>
> **In Fat Tan's courtyard, Huo let go first. That's what made it take. The one being given has to let go. The tree can't. It doesn't know how. It's never been asked anything.**
>
> **So the scion has to.**

---

He asked the keeper on the tenth night.

He did it from the vault steps at three in the morning, the way he did everything now, with the gold thread running down out of the north into the banking hall and no Elder to carry it, just the keeper's own rough voice in the lamplight. He told it everything first: what a graft was, what would be cut, what would go on the cut, what it would cost.

> ***...A PIECE OF ME.***

"Yes."

> ***...LIKE THE ISLAND.***

"Not like the island. The island came back. You grew it back into the field." He turned the staff on his knees. "This won't come back. It'll be on the end of the branch. Part of the tree. The tree will grow it and grow out of it, and whatever grows will be—" He stopped. "We don't know. Something that asks. Maybe."

> ***...A PIECE OF ME, ON THE GARDENER. FOREVER.***

"Yes."

> ***...HOW MUCH?***

> **Kwan's done the sums from the textbooks,** said Seed. **A scion has to be big enough to have living layer the whole way round the cut. The cut's going to be very big.**
>
> **About a third of your field.**

The gold thread dimmed, just a little, like a lamp when someone opens the door of a cold house.

> ***...A THIRD.***
>
> ***...THE COUSINS. THE WALLS. THE FEW WHO STAYED. WHERE WOULD THEY GO?***

"They'd be asked," said Yuanshu. "Every one, nine by nine. Stay in the two-thirds you keep, or go with the third onto the tree. Or rest."

> ***...ONTO THE TREE. WHY WOULD ANYONE WANT TO GO ONTO THE TREE?***

> **Because they're from the world that asks.**

It came through the staff in Seed's voice, but the words weren't Seed's. Seed was carrying them, the way the Elder used to carry the keeper's.

> ***...counting one, let me.***

Hush had climbed down off the windowsill. It stood on the marble floor in the lamplight under the gold thread with all its eyes turned up toward the north.

> ***...keeper. The cousins are my people. The same people, once, before the ring. They burned low in the Low Fire for longer than anyone remembers. Seven million of them burned high in the Mouth's eyes with the Sixth so the rest of us could get away. They came to your field because they wanted to see where you learned.***
>
> ***...that is what they are. They go to where something has not learned yet, and they teach it.***
>
> ***...ask them. I think some of them will want to go onto the tree. I think they will want to be the scion.***

The thread didn't move. Then:

> ***...AND YOU, SMALL ONE? WHAT DO YOU WANT?***

Hush lifted one thin leg toward the gold.

> ***...I want to move first. I want to put the question down at the cut myself, with my own legs, where the tree drinks.***
>
> ***...and then I want to come home and sit in the sun.***
>
> ***...that is what I want. You asked.***

---

The keeper asked the cousins in the morning, nine by nine by nine. It took all day. They answered the way they always did, slowly, and most of them said *ask again tomorrow*, and this time the keeper couldn't, and told them so before they answered, the way it had learned. *I am sorry. I am asking you to decide today. I know that is not fair. I am telling you first.*

> ***...SEVENTY-ONE MILLION.***
>
> ***...SEVENTY-ONE MILLION OF THE COUSINS SAY: ONTO THE TREE. AND FOUR MILLION OF THE WALLS, THE ONES WHO WANTED TO FIND THINGS OUT.***
>
> ***...THEY SAY: WE CAME TO SEE WHERE YOU LEARNED. NOW WE WOULD LIKE TO SEE IF SOMETHING ELSE CAN.***
>
> ***...THEY SAY: WE ARE GOOD AT BEING THE FIRST THING SOMETHING HAS NEVER SEEN BEFORE.***

Then, after a long pause, quieter:

> ***...I SAY YES TOO. A THIRD OF ME, ON THE TREE, FOREVER.***
>
> ***...I WOULD RATHER BE A THIRD SMALLER AND HAVE SOMETHING ON THE GARDENER THAT ASKS THAN BE WHOLE AND PRUNED.***
>
> ***...I AM STILL AFRAID.***

"I know," said Yuanshu. "Me too."

---

On the eleventh night he went up to the Sector Nine battlement by himself.

Not quite by himself: Old Seven stood at the top of the stairs, and Lingyue was somewhere below in the dark. She'd said she'd give him an hour, and he knew she was timing it. He sat in his old place with his back to the parapet, the staff across his knees, the walker-thread ring on his finger, and looked north.

Two rings over the end of the world, where there had been four. The keeper's, small and white in its gold haze. The Elder's, gray and huge, with one soft white light at its center. The first had gone home along the arm. The second was sand on the snow at Kirovsk. And beyond them, invisible, a patch of dark where the far lights bent.

Coming.

> **Nineteen days,** said Seed. **And there's a fourth problem. I didn't say it in the banking hall. I wanted to tell you first.**

*Go on.*

> **A graft is bone to bone, between two dead who both said yes. That's the technique. That's what you learned in the courtyard. That's what the Death Law did.**
>
> **The tree hasn't said yes. It can't. It's never been asked. Hush is going to put the question down at the cut, and the tree might drink it, in a season, in a year. Not in the hour we've got before the cut closes.**
>
> **So when you do the graft, only one side will have said yes.**
>
> **You've opened the door without asking before. Tannery Square. The gleaners at the rim of the island, six thousand four hundred and eleven embers in nine seconds. I asked for a small line on the wall about that: sometimes there's no time.**
>
> **But the door only lets things go. You've never put anything into anything that didn't say yes. Not once since the cemetery.**
>
> **That's what the Collector does. Puts things in. Keeps them there. Without asking.**

He didn't answer.

Below him in the dark: the old quarter, the river, the long green slope of the War Memorial Cemetery, where two years ago a boy with a training spear had knelt on the grass at three in the morning and asked a dead captain if he wanted to get up. Old Seven had said yes. Everyone since had said yes, or no, or ask me tomorrow. A widow on a hill. Eighteen thousand edges. Ninety million names in a staff. A keeper, two rings, a sack made of thread.

Every one asked first.

*Rule One,* he thought. *Every raise asks first.*

> **It tells you what to do when someone says yes,** said Seed. **And when someone says no. Article Ten says where your yes stops. Article Eleven says what to tell them before they answer. There's the line I asked for, about no time, and that's about letting go.**
>
> **There's nothing about putting in. Nobody ever wrote it. Not the Abbot, not the keeper's makers, not you. I've got every word you've ever said about asking. I went through all of it tonight.**
>
> **There's nothing in there for a tree.**

The wind came down off the wall, cold, smelling of snow from very far away. Somewhere below on the stairs he heard a footstep stop and not come on, and knew Lingyue had been counting the hour and had decided not to finish.

*What happens if I don't?*

> **Then we cut the tip without a graft. Lingyue's answer. And the tree grows another way, toward the next brightest field, with nobody there.**

*And if I do?*

> **Then you'll have done something to something without its yes, for the first time. And you'll have to decide what that makes you. I can't tell you. I've run it. It isn't a number.**

He looked down at his hand on the staff. At the plain gray ring on his finger: walker-thread, made of something that came to take and had been turned, slowly, into a thing two people gave each other.

Nobody had asked the walker-thread, either.

He hadn't thought of that until now.

> **No,** said Seed. **I thought of it on your wedding day. I didn't tell you. It wasn't the day for it.**
>
> **Nineteen days, Yuanshu. I'm telling you now so you've got them.**

On the stairs, the footstep started up again and came the rest of the way, and Lingyue sat down next to him in the cold without saying anything at all, and took the hand with the ring on it, and held it in both of hers to warm it up.
