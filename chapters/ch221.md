## Chapter 221: Fire That Burns What Binds

Qin Hao had been standing at the top of the fortress stairs the whole time, and nobody had noticed him, because he wasn't doing anything.

He'd come down the gold road at a run with his bandaged arms and his notebook in his breast pocket and his ordinary fire. He had stood in the knee-deep snow and watched Lingyue go down the wall into the rain cutting threads, watched the Spear Corps hold their spears over living heads, watched Orlov freeze the air over an entire city with his bare hands. Then he'd looked at his own hands, bandaged shoulder to wrist, with orange fire guttering in the palms. Ordinary. The color of a gas ring.

Threads went straight through ordinary fire. He'd learned that on the island with the gleaners, and at Heishui watching shells pass through walkers. Fire that burned what was. Not fire that burned what bound.

So he'd stood at the top of the stairs and done nothing, and hated it, for an hour.

He was still standing there when Yuanshu went down on his knees in the snow with a silver thread on the crown of his head and his face turned to the sky and something starting, slowly, to lift.

---

Yuanshu didn't see what happened next. He was looking at the sky, the way you look at the sky when something has you by the back of the neck and is pulling, and everything you are (a cemetery, a voyage, a field, your mother, a girl on the step of a groundskeeper's hut saying *yes, obviously*) is starting to go up a thread into the dark.

Seed told him afterward, every word. It had watched through the Field, through the dead on the wall, through Old Seven, who had turned his skull toward the fortress halfway down the battlements and stopped.

> **Qin Hao came down the stairs,** said Seed. **He didn't run. He walked through the snow to you. He didn't say anything to anybody.**
>
> **He took his notebook out of his pocket and put it on the snow next to you. Open, at the last page. Very carefully.**
>
> **Then he took hold of the thread.**
>
> **With his bare hands. He'd ripped the bandages off on the stairs. The thread was running up from your head into the dark, and he got both hands round it a meter above you, like a rope.**
>
> **It didn't notice. Threads don't notice the living unless they're reading them. It wasn't reading him. It was busy with you.**
>
> **The fire on his hands was orange. It went straight through the thread like the shells through the walkers.**
>
> **He didn't let go.**

On his knees, face to the sky, the cold drag behind his eyes getting colder, Yuanshu heard a voice right next to him. Rough. Swearing. Not at him. At the thread, at the sky, at a colonel who'd held the inner gate of the Western Pass for four hours with ordinary fire and written in his report that he could hear something on the other side of the rift, laughing.

"Come on," said Qin Hao through his teeth, hands locked round a thread he couldn't burn. "Come on. You came back on the wall. You came back at the river gate. You come back when it matters." His voice cracked. "It *matters.*"

---

> **It came back,** said Seed. **Not from his Law. His Law's Fire, and Fire was burning orange and doing nothing.**
>
> **From the other place. The one that isn't his Law. Where you keep the things you'd sooner die than fail at.**
>
> **I couldn't find it for four months. I looked every day. So did he. So did Bai Lu. It wasn't lost. It was waiting for something worth coming back for.**
>
> **It went white.**

He felt it even then, with his mind going up a thread: a sudden heat a meter over his head. Not on him. On the thread. White. Not the white of light or ice or a King's tribulation. The white of a fire that doesn't burn what is, but what binds. That goes into a tangle of string the way water does and loosens it, so each strand lets go of the next.

The thread went white, and the white went up it.

> **It's climbing the thread,** said Seed. **Like the pale fire went down the trunk at Port Abiri. The thread's a binding and white fire burns bindings, so it's going up the binding. All of it. Toward the other end. Toward the ring.**

The pull on the back of his neck stopped.

Not slackened. Stopped. There was no thread above him any more, just a line of white fire racing up into the dark like a lit fuse, and nothing holding him at all.

His head came down. He pitched forward onto his hands in the snow, gasping, blood running from his nose and ears, the notebook open on the snow in front of him. He couldn't read it. His eyes wouldn't focus.

He looked up.

---

Qin Hao was standing over him with both arms raised, hands open, white fire pouring out of them into the sky.

It had gone up the one thread into the ring's dark center and found the ring full of threads, millions of them running down, each bound to the ring at one end and to a living mind at the other. And it had done what white fire does. It had gone along every binding it could reach.

Everybody in Kirovsk saw it. Out of the ring's dark center the silver rain over the city turned white from the top down, all at once, a slow wave of fire coming down every thread toward the ground like frost spreading down a windowpane. Where it reached the bottom of a thread, the thread let go.

And what had gone up came down.

Light, faint and gold, falling out of the white threads in long slow drifts like snow. Faces, meals, songs, first words, dead mothers' hands, drifting down over the wall and the city toward the heads still turned to the sky. Toward the people they'd been taken from an hour ago, five minutes ago, one breath ago.

The young soldier nearest the fortress, the one with frost in his eyebrows who'd been about to say something, had his face turned up when the light reached him. It went back in the way it had come out, through the crown of his head.

His head came down.

"Mama?" he said.

---

> **Not all of them,** said Seed. **The ones taken at the start, at nine o'clock, went too far up. They're in the ring, packed away. Their threads were cut at the bottom when they were taken. There's nothing for the fire to bring down.**
>
> **But anyone whose thread was still attached, still being lifted, is coming back.**
>
> **Eighteen thousand. Nineteen. Twenty-two. They're coming home.**

Qin Hao dropped to his knees in the snow next to Yuanshu, arms still up, fire still running, face gray, blood at his own nose now.

"Is it enough?" he said, to nobody. "Did it go far enough up?"

> **All the way,** said Seed. **Into the ring, to the dark center, where the knot is.**
>
> **It was too full of threads to find the knot. It didn't know it had been asked. It isn't full any more.**

High over the polar Wastes, the second ring had stopped turning. Every thread hanging from it was white and coming loose one by one from the top. At the center, where they had all come from and where the fire had all gone, nothing was pulling anything any more.

Just a pruned ring with nothing in it that had ever asked. And a small silver shape of fifty-six strands, set down in the middle of it like an empty bowl.

> **I'm still not looking,** said Seed. **But I think it's found it.**

---

Eleven seconds.

Then something came down the last of the white threads that wasn't light or fire. It came the long way, very faint, and Seed didn't try to translate it because it wasn't a language. It was a shape: something asked a question for the first time in its existence, looking inside itself for an answer, and finding it only had one.

> ***...ST...***
>
> ***...STOP.***

On his knees in the snow, Yuanshu looked up.

"That's rest," he said. His voice sounded like someone else's. "Isn't it. It wants to rest."

> **I think so,** said Seed. **I think it's the only thing it knows how to want. It was somebody's keeper once. Its yes got pulled out and it's been cutting for the gardener ever since, not knowing it could do anything else.**
>
> **Somebody asked it what it wanted. It wants to stop.**

He'd opened the door for four thousand on the Western Pass. For a major in a column. For six thousand gleaner embers in nine seconds. For more minds than had ever lived on Blue Star, for nineteen hours in the dark of the cold fire.

Never for a ring.

He did it on his knees in the snow above the main gate of Kirovsk, Qin Hao beside him with fire still pouring from his hands, Orlov standing over them with his arms frosted to the shoulder, Lingyue taking the fortress stairs three at a time with her sword. He went down through the Death Law to the door at the bottom, and it was easier than it should have been, because the door was already standing slightly open, like the back door of a house when someone in the family isn't home yet.

He opened it the rest of the way, and asked the ring.

*You can rest. If you want. Nobody will ask you again.*

---

The second ring came apart over the Northern Union at 23:41.

Not like a walker or a trunk. Like a cloud: the whole vast cracked wheel going soft at the edges the way a cloud goes soft at sunset, then thin, then gone. Out of the place where it had been, drifting down through the white-lit night over Kirovsk, came something that wasn't rain or thread or light.

Sand.

Fine and gray, falling in long soft drifts over the wall and the city and the snow, as the spire at Port Abiri had fallen, and the trunks off the Southern Compact, and the walkers in the field. It fell for an hour and laid a thin gray skin over the snow on every battlement in Kirovsk, like ash after a fire.

Nobody swept it up. Orlov wouldn't allow it. He said the snow would take it in the spring. He said it had been somebody's keeper once.

> **I'm counting,** said Seed. **Forty-one thousand taken before we got here. Twenty-two thousand came back down the white threads. Nineteen thousand didn't. They went up too far, into the ring, and the ring is sand now.**
>
> **I don't know where they are. I don't know if they went through the door with it or somewhere else. I'm writing down that I don't know.**

---

Qin Hao sat in the gray sand with his bare, blistered hands in his lap and didn't move for a long time. Lingyue crouched beside him and started, without asking, to rewrap his hands with strips torn off the bottom of her own shirt, and he let her, which told everyone exactly how bad it was.

Yuanshu picked up the notebook.

The last page had a single line on it, months old, in Yuanshu's own handwriting, from a cot in the gatekeeper's lodge at the river gate, where he'd written it for Qin Hao because Qin Hao's hands had been too bandaged to hold a pen.

*ORDINARY FIRE. UNTIL.*

He took the pen out of the spine and wrote underneath it, while Qin Hao watched and didn't say anything:

*UNTIL KIROVSK.*

And under that:

*FIRST.*

Qin Hao looked at it for a while. Then he said, hoarsely, "Your handwriting's worse than hers," and nodded at Lingyue, and Lingyue hit him on the arm, very gently, which was the first time anybody had seen her do it gently.
