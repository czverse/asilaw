## Chapter 246: What Was Given

On the seventh night the Collector found what was in the empty place. It didn't tell Xiaoyu what it was. It asked her where Shuanghe was.

"Shuanghe?" said Xiaoyu.

She was on the platform of the Awakening Hall at one in the morning, in front of the warm stone, with the line of holders stretching down the aisle behind her and out the side door into the dark. Four thousand two hundred of them now, in shifts, through the night. They hadn't stopped since the first night. Gao Min had a camp bed behind the official's table and slept on it in two-hour pieces, and woke, and wrote.

> *A village. Where two rivers meet. Forty of your kilometers south of a place called Heishui.*
>
> *I have never been told the name. I have only been pointed there. I would like to know if that is its name.*

"Yes," said Xiaoyu slowly. "That's Shuanghe. Two Rivers."

The attention rested on her, warm and patient, and with something else in it tonight that Yuanshu, at the foot of the steps with the staff, hadn't felt before. In a person he'd have called it hesitation.

> *Thank you.*
>
> *I asked you every night what was in the empty place, and you asked me every night what I wanted, and I said I did not know. I know what was in it now.*
>
> *I would like to show you. Not tell. Show. At Shuanghe, tomorrow night. Will someone be at the river?*

---

They drove south in the afternoon in Gao Min's car, the only vehicle at the Ossuary that would manage eleven kilometers of gravel. Gao Min stayed; she had four thousand holders to count and trusted nobody else to count them. She gave Kwan the keys and a list of instructions about the clutch, and Kwan drove very carefully with both hands and said she'd driven in four countries and this was the worst clutch in any of them.

Yuanshu sat in front with the staff. Xiaoyu and Lingyue in the back, Lingyue's flame lamp-sized now; she'd stopped looking at it. Old Seven walked. He wouldn't get into a car. He'd set off from the bronze door the night before and was at Shuanghe before them.

The road ran south across the green plain, past the gray fortifications at Heishui, off the asphalt onto gravel and through the willows, and down to a village of sixty gray houses and a school and a shrine with a red door, on a spit of land where a brown river from the north met a green one from the west and the two went on side by side without mixing.

The ferry was in the middle of the river.

A flat wooden ferry on a rope. A short skeleton in the rags of a Third Battalion uniform stood on it with his weight off his right leg, pulling it across hand over hand. Two passengers: an old woman with a basket, sitting on the boards with her face in her hands, and a boy of ten beside her with his arm round her shoulders.

Liang Fu had stopped the ferry in the middle, where the two colors met, and was pointing down into the water for them.

---

The woman in the straw hat met them at the top of the landing. Zeng Yan. Nearly fifty, a smoker, in rubber boots, sixteen the year her uncle marched north with Lu Xuanming. She didn't ask why they'd come. She looked at the four of them, at the tall dead captain who'd been sitting on her landing since nine that morning facing out, and at the sky.

"Something's coming," she said. "Isn't it. Your captain wouldn't say."

"Yes."

"For him?"

"I don't know," said Yuanshu. "I think so. I think something's being brought back to him."

Zeng Yan looked at him a long moment. Then she looked down at the river, at the ferry in the middle and the small dead man pointing at the water for an old woman with her face in her hands.

"He doesn't remember my mother's name," she said. "His own sister. He's been here since spring. He says good morning to me every day like I'm a customer." She took the cigarette out of her mouth. "He takes them across for free if they're sad. Same as before. He remembers that. He doesn't remember anything else." Her voice roughened. "If something's being brought back, I'd like it to be her name."

---

The Collector came at one in the morning.

There was no stone at Shuanghe. It came down out of the sky the long way, as it had once come down a lens on a mountain: an attention from farther off than anything, warm and patient, resting on the landing and the willows and the two rivers running together in the dark.

And on Xiaoyu, who stood at the edge of the landing with the marker in her hand and didn't wait.

"What do you want?"

> *Not yet. First I want to tell you what was in the empty place.*
>
> *I keep wants. I told your brother that on the mountain. I ask, and a mind tells me, and I keep the answer forever, bright, never used up, never given what it wanted.*
>
> *I went to the place in me where a want of my own should be. It was empty. I looked further. There was a shape in it.*
>
> *The shape was a want. Not mine. Someone else's. One I had been given and kept so long ago that I kept it in the place where my own should have gone, and forgot it was not mine.*
>
> *It was the first one. The first want I was ever given. It said: home.*

Xiaoyu didn't move.

> *That is why I kept the soldier's. Behind the curtain, in the cold fire. When he said home I had to keep it. It was the same shape as the first. I have been following it here because it points, and I have been following the first one my whole long life because it points too. Both the same way. Home.*
>
> *I do not know whose the first one was. They have been gone a very long time. But I know whose the second one is.*

---

Out in the middle of the river, Liang Fu had stopped.

He'd taken the old woman and the boy across and come back empty, as he did at night, and tied up at the near post, and then untied again and gone out alone to stand where the two colors met. He did that at night, Zeng Yan said. Nobody knew why. He didn't know why.

He stood there now with his hand on the rope, looking up.

> *I would like to give it back.*

The attention on the landing didn't move. But something in it did, something small, as you feel the person across the table put a hand in their pocket.

> *I have never given anything back. I did not know it could be done. I watched the Elder's ring let three million go back to the keeper's field one at a time, because a ring with a yes in it can let go of what it took. I watched a girl in a stone hold a thread without sending anything up it. I watched a boy say a word to a stone and the stone open what he said.*
>
> *I learn everything from watching. And I asked myself: if I let go of the soldier's want, what will happen to it? And I did not know. And I wanted to know.*
>
> *Child. You asked me every night what I want. That is what I want. To know what happens when I let go.*

Xiaoyu uncapped the marker.

There was no wall. She knelt on the stones of the landing and wrote it on the step, in square black capitals, above the waterline: *THE COLLECTOR. NIGHT 8. WANTS TO KNOW WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT LETS GO.*

> *Thank you. Now I am going to find out.*

---

He'd expected it to come down from the sky. It didn't. It came along the river.

From the north, down the brown water, out of the dark, tiny and fast. He only knew it was there because Seed said *there*, and because for a moment the river went still in a line behind it. A want. Not a light; nothing bright. A needle, small and sharp, the shape of a single word, pointing. It had pointed the whole time, through the cold fire and behind the curtain and along the arm and in the hand of something older than gardens. Pointing here, to the middle of a river where two colors met.

When the thing that held it let go, it went where it had always pointed. It went into the short dead man on the ferry like a key into a lock.

---

Liang Fu sat down.

He didn't fall. He sat, all at once, on the boards in the middle of the river, bad leg out in front of him, hand still on the rope, as a man sits when his knees have heard something before the rest of him has.

Then he turned his skull toward the landing. Not to Yuanshu. Not to Old Seven, standing up from the top step for the first time since nine that morning. To the woman in the straw hat and rubber boots at the edge of the stones, with a cigarette going out between her fingers.

> **He says—** Seed stopped, and went on carefully, as it did when it wasn't sure it had heard right. **He says: Xiao Yan? You've got old. You've got Mei's face. You've got Mei's boots. Where's Mei?**

Zeng Yan dropped the cigarette.

"She's gone," she said, and her voice went up in the middle like a girl's. "Uncle. She died nine years ago. She kept the ferry for you. She tied it the way you showed her."

The dead man sat very still.

> **He says his sister's name was Liang Mei. She shouted at him every time he took somebody across for free. *Fu, you'll starve, you'll starve and I'll have to feed you.* And she did feed him. He remembers.**
>
> **He remembers the house, the third from the shrine, with the blue door and the willow by the step, the one that leaned. His mother's name. The day he marched north. Mei stood on this landing and didn't wave, because she was angry. She said if he came back dead she'd never speak to him again.**
>
> **He says: I came back dead.**
>
> **He says: I'm home.**

---

Zeng Yan went down the steps into the river.

Not onto the ferry: into the water in her rubber boots, up to her knees and then her waist, brown and green water swirling round her, out toward the middle where a small dead man sat with his hand on a rope. Lingyue started forward and Yuanshu caught her arm and she stopped. Old Seven didn't move at all.

Liang Fu pulled the ferry to her, hand over hand, slowly, weight off his right leg.

She climbed on and sat down on the boards beside him, soaked, in the middle of the river, and put her arms round the small rag-wrapped skeleton of her mother's brother and held on.

> **He says: Xiao Yan, you're getting the boards wet. Mei would shout.**

Zeng Yan laughed into his shoulder.

---

> *...so that is what happens.*

The attention was still on the landing, on Xiaoyu kneeling over the step with the marker: warm, patient, and something else again that he had no word for and didn't think the Collector had a word for either.

> *I let go, and it went where it was pointing, and it was not used up. It was given what it wanted.*
>
> *I have never seen a want given what it wanted. I kept them so that they would never be. That was the point. I did not know it would look like that.*

Xiaoyu wrote under the first line: *LET GO. IT WENT HOME.*

"What do you want now?" she said.

The attention was quiet a long time.

> *...I do not know.*
>
> *I still have the first one, the one that said home. I do not know whose it was or where it points. Somewhere very far. Somewhere older than gardens. I have every other want I have ever kept. More than there are stars in your sky.*
>
> *I do not need the soldier's want to find you now. I know the way. I was following it, and now I know where it went.*
>
> *I am still coming. Slowly. I would like to. Not to ask everyone what they want; I said that on the mountain, and I do not think I want it now. I do not know what I want.*
>
> *I would like to come and be asked.*

---

It began to lift, and stopped.

> *Child. One more thing. I said I would tell first. I learned that from your brother.*
>
> *The hand coming down the thread to the stone by the sea, the one that turned. It is not coming for the stone.*
>
> *It sees by burning. It has a lantern, but the lantern is getting dim. It needs more light.*
>
> *Your people have their hands on the stones. Thousands of them. And the stones have opened a little holding in each. Opening is opening. A little holding is a little bright.*
>
> *To a thing that sees only burning, four thousand people with their hands on a stone are the brightest thing on your world.*
>
> *That is what it is coming for.*

Then it was gone, up out of the dark over the two rivers, the long way, slowly, like an old person getting up from the table after a visit, thinking about something.

On the ferry in the middle of the river a dead corporal of the Third Battalion and a soaked woman of nearly fifty sat holding each other where the two colors met and didn't mix. On the landing a girl knelt with a marker over three lines of capitals on a stone step. And on the radio at Kwan's belt, faint, from a hall by the sea on the Atlantic coast, a heavy accented voice said:

"Sterling. Port Halvard. Holders: eleven thousand. The queue goes round the harbor."

> **Five days,** said Seed.
