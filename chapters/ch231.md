## Chapter 231: What Grows

He couldn't get there.

That was the first thing he understood, kneeling on the battlement with Lingyue gray and half-conscious against his shoulder and the empty chair three meters away. The cut was four kilometers up. The gold road the dead had climbed was a road for the dead. Su Mingli was slumped against the parapet with blood running out of both nostrils and her eyes shut, and when he looked at her she said, without opening them, "No. I can't. Not even you. Not one meter."

And a graft was bone to bone, a hand on each. That was the technique. He'd done it once, kneeling in a courtyard with one hand on a sergeant and one on a captain, and that was the only way he'd ever done it.

> **Fifty minutes,** said Seed. **Yuanshu. You've done this before too. Not the graft. The other thing.**
>
> **The training hall. The first time I read the dead. You asked him and he let you, and you went down through the Death Law and came up inside him, on the eastern wall, on his last night. A spear in your hands that wasn't yours. A left forearm that had healed crooked.**
>
> **That was his memory. This would be him now. Same door. He's never shut it.**

He hadn't forgotten. He'd put it away, the way you put away the first time you do anything frightening: Old Seven's body round him like a coat that didn't fit, the smell of smoke and wet concrete and, from somewhere down in the camp, dumplings.

He looked up. Four kilometers above the north wall, at the edge of a sheet of pale gold coming down the sky, a tall dark figure had stopped walking.

> **He says: I'm here, boy. You know the way in.**

---

He shut his eyes and went down: through the staff, through the Death Law to the cool dark place at the bottom where the door stood slightly open, as it always did now. Then along. Not through the door. Past it, along the oldest thread he had, the very first one, the one that ran from a boy with a training spear to a gravestone at three in the morning.

And up.

He was standing on gold, and it moved under him like a boat deck. He was tall. Much too tall; the sky was closer than it should be. A spear in his right hand. His left hand wasn't his, and wasn't even Old Seven's. It was a sergeant's arm, two fingers missing, and at the shoulder he could feel, faintly, the seam where it had been joined on.

The first graft. He was wearing it.

In front of him, past the leading edge of the gold, a kilometer across: the cut.

Up close it didn't look like the inside of a green stick. It didn't look like anything. A cliff of wet gold, shining, pulsing slowly like a throat swallowing. Everything the gardener had was pouring into it. He could feel the pull through Old Seven's boots, the whole tree back along the arm leaning toward this one open place.

And Hush was already on it. Out on the face of the cut, the size of a cat on a cliff, clinging to the wet gold with every one of its thin legs, walking slowly toward the middle.

> ***...counting one. Captain. I am going to the middle, where it drinks hardest.***
>
> ***...do the graft. I will put the question down when it takes. Not before. If I put it down first, it will drink the question instead of the scion, and the scion will fall.***
>
> ***...you go first. Then me.***

---

He knelt on the edge of the gold.

Old Seven's knees on the scion: the keeper's third, warm as the field had always been warm, with seventy-one million cousins and four million of the walls inside it, all of whom had said *onto the tree*. Old Seven's right hand flat on the gold behind him. Huo's arm, the left, with its finger and thumb, reaching across the last half-meter of air to the cut.

He asked the Death Law, very gently, to do what it had done once in a courtyard among Fat Tan's potted plants. Not raise. Not release. Give.

It went wrong immediately.

Seed had said that in most of four thousand runs the two embers had fought over the arm like two dogs over a coat. This was worse. The moment Huo's hand touched the cut, the tree pulled. Not at the hand. At everything behind it: the scion, the cousins, Old Seven, him, the thread running down to a boy on a battlement, the Lattice under the boy, the staff in his hands with ninety million names in it. The gardener didn't graft. It had never grafted. It drank. And the scion pulled back, because it was a third of a keeper and a keeper's field was a thing that held, so it held. The two halves of the graft hung at the edge of the gold, hauling against each other, and the living layers slid past one another and wouldn't meet and wouldn't take.

> **It's going to tear,** said Seed. **The scion has to let go first. That's the only way it's ever worked.**

*I know. I can't ask them. They already said yes. Asking again now would be pushing.*

> **Then don't ask them. Tell them what you'll do with it.**

---

He didn't know he was going to say it out loud until he was saying it.

Through his own throat, four kilometers down on the battlement, on his knees with his eyes shut and blood dripping onto his wife's hair. Through the radio at his belt, which was open. Through the staff into ninety million names. Up the gold thread into a small white ring over the end of the world, and down the gold into the scion, into seventy-one million cousins and four million walls, all holding on.

"This is Zhou Yuanshu," he said. "I'm about to do something to the gardener that it didn't say yes to."

His voice was very thin. Everyone on the wall heard it. Everyone on Blue Star with a radio heard it.

"It can't answer. It isn't anyone. Not yet. So I've decided. Nobody said yes. I'm saying that out loud so nobody can ever claim afterward that it did." He breathed. "Article Twelve. I owe it the question. Every day. Until it can answer. And after."

He opened his eyes, four kilometers up, inside a dead captain's skull at the edge of a field of gold.

"Cousins. Walls. You don't have to hold on to the keeper any more. It said yes too. It's letting you go, onto the tree. I'll ask you every morning, through it. I promise."

From the small white ring in the north, rough and close, in a voice that had learned to speak with no one to carry it:

> ***...GO ON. I AM LETTING GO.***
>
> ***...I AM NOT AFRAID OF THIS PART.***

The scion let go.

He felt it the way he'd felt Huo let go in the courtyard. Not giving way: giving. Seventy-one million cousins and four million walls of the Mouth, burning low as the Low Fire had taught them, opened their hands. Not falling. Leaning onto the cut, the way you lean on a door you know is about to open.

The living layers met.

Gold on gold, thread on thread, the whole kilometer of the cut at once. Huo's hand on the cut and Old Seven's knees on the scion and between them, through bone and Death Law and him, the two sides touched, slid, caught.

And took.

> **Death Law: 45.6 to 47.1 percent,** said Seed. **It's taking.**
>
> **Hush. Now.**

---

Hush was in the middle of the cut. From the edge, through Old Seven's eyes, a kilometer off, it was a tiny pale thing on many legs at the center of a gold cliff that was beginning, at the rim, to dull and roughen. To heal.

It sat down, legs drawn in, the way it had sat in the crack for four billion years playing statue.

Then it did the one thing it had never once done in four billion years, because whoever moved first got eaten.

It moved.

It put one thin leg down flat on the living gold of the gardener of the Shallows, where it drank hardest, as its people had once put questions down at the roots of old trees. And it put the question down. Not in words; trees don't have words. In the shape Seed had learned from it and used on three rings: something held out and then let go of, a bowl set on the ground and the hand taken away and the eyes turned aside so as not to look.

*What do you want?*

The gold drank it.

He felt it go: down into the cut, back along the branch toward the tree, a small, light thing sinking into a large dark. Not held. Not kept. Given.

Hush got up.

---

The callus was closing.

From the rim inward, the gold was going dull and gray and hard, like a tide coming in over a mudflat. Fast. Faster than anything in Kwan's textbooks. The tree had never been cut before and it healed like a child's knee after its first fall: all at once, too hard, with everything it had.

Hush was in the middle, a kilometer from the edge.

It started walking. Swaying, slowly, every leg out. It didn't run. He didn't think it knew how.

> **It isn't going to make the edge,** said Seed. **I've run it.**

At the edge of the gold, Old Seven stood up.

He didn't need Yuanshu for this, and Yuanshu felt him not need him: felt the dead captain's will close gently round his own, like a big hand round a small one, and set it to one side. Old Seven laid his spear down on the scion. He stepped off the gold onto the cut, onto the gray callus, hard under his boots, and then the wet gold beyond, which took him to the ankles like a riverbank.

He walked out to the middle, toward the small pale thing walking toward him, two meters ten and slow, as he'd walked out of the cemetery on the first night. The way he walked everywhere. Not fast. Not stopping.

He reached it with the gray coming in behind him like a wave.

He crouched, as a tall man crouches to talk to a child, and held out his hand. Huo's hand, flat and open, one finger and a thumb.

Hush looked at it.

> ***...captain. It is morning.***

It climbed in.

---

He came back into his own body on the battlement with a noise like someone surfacing from a swimming pool.

Lingyue had her hand on his face and was saying his name. Qin Hao was kneeling on his other side with his notebook in one hand and the other flat on Yuanshu's chest as if to stop him floating off. Four kilometers above the north wall an enormous silver-gray branch hung in the dark, faintly gold along its underside where it carried the scion, its end rounded and gray and closed.

And walking down the gold road out of the sky, with his spear back over his shoulder and something small and pale held to his chest in one hand: Old Seven.

The gold under his feet went out behind him as he came, step by step, as if the road were rolling itself up. He didn't hurry. He never did. He came down the last of it onto the battlement, onto the stone beside the empty chair, and stopped, and opened his hand.

Hush sat in it with its legs drawn in and all its eyes shut. Gray with exhaustion. Gold dust on its thin legs from the cut.

> ***...counting one. I put it down. I did not look.***
>
> ***...I would like to sit in the sun now, if that is all right.***

It was half past two in the morning. There was no sun.

"Tomorrow," said Yuanshu. His voice wasn't working. "First thing."

> ***...yes. Tomorrow is fine.***

---

The tip came down at a quarter past three.

It had been falling for an hour, so slowly it barely seemed to move, coming apart as it fell. By the time it reached the ground forty kilometers past the north wall there wasn't much left: silver-gray thread settling over the Wastes in long soft drifts, like snow that had forgotten how to be cold.

> **Most of what it drank went down the branch to the tree long ago,** said Seed. **But a tip drinks faster than it passes things on. What it took at the Mouth was still in it.**
>
> **Yuanshu. The Sixth are in the snow.**

He didn't open the door. Not yet. On his knees on the battlement with Lingyue propping him up, he asked first, down through the Death Law and across the long cold dark to the north, to something lying in thread on the snow, burning faintly, like a dropped match.

*Sixth. It's me. I'm asking.*

The answer came back the long way, faint, in many voices, and underneath it, still, the singing.

> ***...counting one. We were very bright.***
>
> ***...we would like, now, for a while, to be dark. Is the door open?***

"Yes," he said. "It's always a bit open now."

He opened it the rest of the way, and they went through like sparks up a chimney: seven million cousins and the Sixth and the one part in a hundred of the walls that had gone with them, out of the snow and through the door to wherever finished things go.

And one more. He felt it go past him on the threshold. Not bright. Ash-gray and stubborn. An old man with a cane who'd been pulled up a thread into a branch and come down with it, and had been sitting in the snow at the back of the queue, letting everyone else go first, the way adjutants do.

> ***...dead, not deaf, boy. I heard all of it. Good rule, the twelfth one.***
>
> ***...tell him about the dumplings.***

Then the door was only a little open again, as it always was now, and the old man was through it.

> **Death Law: 47.1 to 47.9 percent.**

---

They took the dumplings to stone No. 7 in the morning.

Dabao's mother made them: nineteen, in a paper boat, steaming, from the red cart on the corner of East Market Road. She wouldn't take money for them and she wouldn't let Dabao carry them. She walked up the cemetery slope herself in her apron, between the rows of little gray stones, to the eastern field.

Old Seven was waiting at the stone. He took the paper boat from her in both hands, the one with a finger and a thumb and the one that was a sergeant's, knelt, and set it on the grass in front of stone No. 7, next to a fresh patch where the turf had been cut that morning for an adjutant who had asked, twice, to be buried beside it.

He stayed kneeling a long while. The steam went up into the cold air.

> **He says: paid,** said Seed.

Hush was on top of the stone in the sun. First thing, as promised. Every leg out.

---

The bud appeared on the ninth day.

Kwan saw it first, through the keeper's eye, and then everyone did, because it gave off light. The branch itself had gone dark again, back to a place in the north where the stars bent. But at its end, where it had been cut and grafted and healed, there was a small point of gold, low over the end of the world beside the keeper's ring, a third smaller now, and the Elder's ring with its soft white light. A new star. Very faint.

It didn't say anything.

Every morning at three the keeper asked it, down the long dark of the branch, as it had promised. Yuanshu asked with it. *What do you want?* Then nothing. The bowl set down, the eyes turned away.

It didn't answer.

> **It won't for a long time,** said Seed. **Hush says a season, a year, maybe longer. But the rest of the tree has stopped growing toward us. Kwan's checked every night. The branches that were turning our way have stopped turning.**
>
> **I don't know why. The question, or the cut, or it's thinking. I don't know anything about it except that somebody asked it.**

On the long white wall of the Ossuary, at the top of the last column round the corner of the vault door, under ARTICLE TWELVE and Sterling's red *no* and *is this what they did to us*, Xiaoyu climbed the stepladder and wrote:

*THE BRANCH. ASKED EVERY MORNING. NO ANSWER YET.*

And under it, smaller:

*ADJ. XU DEHAI. CARRIED THE MESSAGE. NEXT TO STONE NO. 7. PAID IN FULL.*
