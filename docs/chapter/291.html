Book 4: Other Orchards

Chapter 291The Bow

czverse11 min read

They came at midnight, and they didn't come to talk.

Eleven ships, said Seed. Of the twenty-seven who walked out, eleven are Paragons. Each has a ship, and each ship is moving. Not folding: coming, across the orbit of Anthos, toward us.

They're not burning their cores harder. They're burning them the hardest I've ever felt. The old way. All at once.

Tam can hear them screaming.

He was in the Lens chamber. Everyone was. At eight, when the twenty-seven walked out of the Assembly, Seed had said the Lens chamber was the only place on the ship that mattered, and everyone who meant to stand anywhere should stand there. So they stood there: in the bow of the ship that had been the House of Severin's, in a round chamber of black stone the size of a courtyard, before a disc of black stone the width of the chamber, ground so thin you could see through it, with a lamp burning in it and a fog-ringer talking.

Ichiro says it's all right. He can see them coming, through their faces. Eleven Paragons. He says they're very angry. One of them's crying and doesn't know it.

He says Kiyo says if anyone breaks this stone she'll be very cross.

At five past twelve, standing before the Lens, Seed told him the rest.

I didn't tell anyone before. I wasn't sure. I'm sure now. I read it in the Lens when Ichiro went in, and again when the mark went out.

If the Lens breaks, every face breaks. Severin said that, and it's true. But it isn't only faces.

Every stone in every human orchard was cut from the Lens or from stones cut from it. Every Class any stone ever opened in anyone was opened by a stone that's still a little part of it.

If the root breaks, I don't know if the Classes stay open.

He went very still.

Every Class.

In the Federation: eight thousand five hundred and eighty-eight worlds. Every Paragon. Every C. Every A and S and F.

And on Blue Star. Our stones fell out of the crack forty-two years ago, like seeds. I thought they came from nowhere. I think now they were cut from stones cut from stones cut from this, and thrown out along the threads, the way the gardener sends out a branch, into an orchard that didn't have stones yet.

Lingyue's light. Su Mingli's gravity. Qin Hao's fire. Shi Lei's spear. Xiaoyu's Scribe. Tam's hearing.

Mine, maybe. I don't know what I'm cut from.

If the Lens breaks, I don't know if any of it stays.

Lingyue stood beside him with Xiaodeng asleep in the sling, the sword on her back, the lamp in her hand. She'd heard; Seed had let her.

She looked down at the lamp. Pale. Steady. Held.

"Then it doesn't break," said Shen Lingyue.

They came in through the hull at twelve minutes past.

Not through a door. Paragons of the Great Houses didn't use doors. Eleven ships drew up alongside the flagship in orbit, burning, and eleven people stepped off them into the dark between, onto nothing, as Severin had walked down out of the sky over East Ring Road, and walked through the flagship's hull as if it weren't there.

And through the holes they left came the guard.

Ladder Guard, said Seed. Four thousand. S and A, all of them. Marked. Armed.

And drones. I didn't know they had them. Nor did Iselde. They've never been used. They were built for the Reaper: constructs of thread and steel with nobody in them.

Except a core. One each, in the chest, burning.

Eleven thousand.

The First of the Ladder met them in the long corridor that led to the bow.

Alone. He'd asked to. At eight, when the twenty-seven walked out, he'd said the long corridor was his: he'd walked down it to the arrays eighty times in ninety days, and back up it, and if anyone was coming down it to the Lens, they'd come past him. He hadn't said it as an order. He'd said it as he said everything now, badly, like a word in a book, and asked if anyone minded. Nobody had.

He stood in the middle of the long corridor in a C's gray coat with his hands at his sides.

Down the corridor toward him came the first of the eleven.

Yuanshu felt it through Seed, standing in the Lens chamber: a Paragon in iron-gray with a disc of four gold marks at her throat, lit, two lines in it, true. Tall. Old. Walking at an even pace, unhurried, hands at her sides, as Severin walked. The Paragon of the House of Iron.

She stopped ten meters from him.

"Dacian," she said.

"Ostrava," said the man in the gray coat.

"Stand aside."

"No."

"You were First."

"There is no First," said Dacian Severin. "There never was. There was a stone that said so. A girl in an orchard wrote that it was not our face. You read it this morning. You know it is true."

The Paragon of the House of Iron didn't move.

"I know it is true," she said. "That is why I am going to break it."

He didn't see the fight in the corridor. Nobody could have. Seed said afterward that two Star Monarchs fighting in a corridor two hundred meters long wasn't a thing eyes were made for; it was like watching two storms decide which of them was the sky. He felt it. The whole ship felt it. The deck under his feet in the Lens chamber bucked, and the walls rang like a struck bell. Far back along the ship in the arrays, two hundred and forty thousand rowers felt their ship shake round them and didn't stop rowing, because nobody had told them to, and Halvarde went from cell to cell asking each by name whether they wanted to go on.

They did.

And the other ten came round.

They're not going down the corridor, said Seed. Severin's in the corridor. They're going through the ship: through walls, through decks, toward the bow from every side. With the guard, and the drones.

Six minutes. Maybe seven.

"Third Battalion," said Yuanshu.

He didn't shout it. He said it as Huo said things on a wall.

Sixty shapes of gray ash, standing at ease in a line along the curved wall of the Lens chamber, stood up.

Huo says: sir. They're coming through the walls, said Seed. Old Seven says: then we'll be the wall.

They went out through the chamber's three doors into the ship, all sixty shapes of ash and a tall one-armed dead captain, to meet ten Paragons of the Great Houses and four thousand Ladder Guard and eleven thousand drones coming through the decks.

He went with them.

He didn't decide to. He was in the doorway before he knew he'd moved, staff in hand, and Lingyue caught his arm.

"No," she said.

"They're my dead."

"They're Huo's," said his wife. "And Old Seven's. They know what they're doing. You don't. You've never fought a Paragon." Her grip was very tight. "You stay here, in front of the stone, with me. If anything comes through that door, it comes through you, and then me, and then a fog-ringer and a nun." She didn't let go. "Give them the fire. From here. Like the pier."

He looked at her. At Xiaodeng asleep in the sling against a sword that weighed a hundred kilograms. At the lamp in her hand.

He gave them the fire.

Down through the staff into the Death Law, all fifty-six point nine percent of it, and out through the bone of the staff and the bones of the dead like water through a riverbed. Pale fire: Lingyue's light wound with his Law. She laid her hand on the staff beside his, as at Port Halvard, and it went pale.

Into sixty shapes of ash and a one-armed dead captain in the corridors of the ship.

He felt them meet, not with his eyes but with Seed's, through the dead, as he'd felt the First Legion meet a branch the size of a mountain range over Lingzhou. Corridors. Decks. The gray of the Third Battalion moving through the ship as they'd moved along the eastern wall in the Black Tide, as they'd stood at the edge of the cold fire: not fast, not stopping. And coming at them through the walls, burning, eleven thousand drones of thread and steel with a core in each chest, built ten thousand years ago to fight the Reaper and never once used.

The Third Battalion went into them.

They can't see us, said Seed. The drones were built to fight the Reaper's hands. They see bright. They see burning. They see marks.

The dead aren't bright and aren't marked. The drones can't see the Third Battalion.

And sixty shapes of ash, pale fire on the shapes of their spears and swords and one pick, went through eleven thousand drones in the corridors of the flagship as the gleaners had gone for the dead at the rim of the island: low, fast, in between. And where they went, constructs of thread and steel came apart.

And the cores came out.

He felt every one.

He hadn't expected that. Each drone the Third Battalion took apart had a core in its chest: a person, pressed, burning, put in a construct ten thousand years ago or a hundred or ten, and never asked. When the construct came apart round them the core didn't fall. It hung in the corridor in the dark, burning, held by nothing for the first time.

Not dead. Not alive. Pressed, like the cells in the walls.

Ask them, he thought.

I am. Ichiro is, through the Lens. Every one, as it comes free. Most of them say: the door.

They've been in the drones a very long time.

He opened it. From the Lens chamber, standing before the stone with his wife's hand on the staff beside his: down at the bottom of the Death Law, the cool dark place, the door a little open. He opened it the rest of the way and held it, and they came out of the corridors of the flagship, out of drones of thread and steel, small burning lights going past him on the threshold one after another, as the old ones had gone past him from the palm of the hand at Port Halvard.

Level 72 to 73, said Seed. 73 to 74. They count, the drones. They're constructs with a weave, like walkers.

Death Law: 56.9 to 57.8 percent. Level 74 to 75.

Two minutes.

Then the guard.

Four thousand, S and A, marked, armed, living, coming through the decks behind the drones toward the bow. And they could see the Third Battalion, because they weren't drones. They were people, and they knew what the dead were.

The Third Battalion didn't go into them.

Huo says: sir, they're alive, said Seed. Old Seven says: I know. Huo says: they're coming for the stone. Old Seven says: I know.

Huo says: Article Seven, the boy's rule. The dead may defend. So we defend. We don't conquer. We don't kill anyone who doesn't have to be killed.

Old Seven says: that's not a question, sergeant.

And sixty shapes of ash and a one-armed dead captain stood in the corridors of the Severin flagship before four thousand living soldiers of the Ladder Guard, and didn't strike.

They held.

As they'd held the eastern wall. As they'd stood at the edge of the cold fire so the cold came to them first. In the corridors, facing out, with pale fire on their weapons and their weapons not moving, so that four thousand Ladder Guard, S and A, marked, armed, coming through the walls to break the root of every face in the Federation, came round a corner and found themselves facing a line of the dead that would neither give way nor strike.

Huo says you can go back, said Seed, carrying it in the Federation's language without the grade. Or you can try to go through us. We're ash; we've stood in front of worse. But if you go through us, you're going through the Third Battalion of the Eastern Line, and nobody's ever once gone through us.

Huo says: I'm telling you first.

The Ladder Guard stopped.

One minute, said Seed. Yuanshu. The ten Paragons are past the guard. They didn't come through the corridors. They came round the bow, through the hull, from outside.

They're in the walls of this chamber.

The curved black stone of the Lens chamber's wall, behind the disc, behind the lamp, behind the fog-ringer, cracked.

He turned.

Lingyue had already turned. She took her hand off the staff and put it on her grandfather's sword and drew it, all hundred kilograms, one-handed, with Xiaodeng asleep in the sling against her chest, and stood in front of the Lens.

The wall came apart.

And through it, out of the dark of orbit, burning, came ten Paragons of the Great Houses of the Lamplit Worlds, with faces at their throats that weren't their faces and two lines of true writing in each that they'd read at dawn and couldn't unread.

They stopped at the edge of the broken wall, ten of them, looking at the disc of black stone the width of the chamber with a lamp burning in it. At a woman before it with a sword in one hand and a sleeping baby in a sling. At a boy beside her with a white staff, who'd stood on a platform in the Assembly the day before and said every one of their wants back to them, exactly, so they were heard twice.

"Move," said the first of them, a man in the blue of the House of Teller. Young, for a Paragon: a hundred, perhaps. His voice was shaking.

"No," said Shen Lingyue.

"You cannot stop ten of us."

"I know," said Lingyue. "I'm not moving anyway."

And from behind them, down the long corridor, at an even pace, unhurried, out of a fight between two storms deciding which was the sky, came the First of the Ladder.

In a torn gray coat. Blood on his face. One arm hanging.

Walking.
