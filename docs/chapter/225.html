Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 225Whoever Moves First

czverse7 min read

"You want to ask it," said Zhou Wanqing.

She didn't say it as a question. She said it at breakfast in the back room of the Ossuary, over a bowl of rice porridge going cold, with Xiaoyu on one side of the table and Yuanshu on the other and the window open onto the courtyard, where Hush lay on the sill in the sun with all its legs out.

Seed hadn't said a word about it for two days. But she had built Seed in a basement around a sack of four thousand people's yeses, and she could hear it not saying things the same way she could hear her son not saying things, and she'd been listening to both of them not say this since the night Kwan came in with the laptop.

Yes, said Seed.

"The gardener."

Yes.

Xiaoyu put her spoon down. "That's insane."

Yes. I've checked. It's also the only idea I've got that isn't worse.

It laid it out after breakfast in the banking hall, under the long white wall, to whoever came. Which was everyone. At some point in the past year it had stopped being possible to say anything important in the Ossuary without everyone turning up: Lingyue with her sword, Qin Hao with his notebook, Kwan, Fat Tan, Sister Ning with her candle, the Mo twins, Bai Lu, Dabao. Old Seven at the bronze door. Adjutant Xu in his chair. Su Mingli, who walked in off the street with her arms folded and announced she wasn't staying.

The gardener's never been asked anything, said Seed. I know that the same way I knew about the rings: from what it does. Something that had been asked wouldn't prune keepers that learned to ask. It would want to know what they'd learned.

It farms keepers. Keepers harvest worlds. Above it is the Collector, which taught all of them to keep, in dreams, and watched them learn, and comes along afterward to pick what they wanted.

It sent three rings to collect me and prune what I'd touched. One's resting. One's gone home. One has the Elder in it and asks.

They didn't do what they were sent to do, because somebody asked them what they wanted. So now it's coming itself.

"And you want to ask it what it wants," said Su Mingli.

Yes.

"It isn't a ring. It's what makes rings. It prunes keepers that have hung above worlds since before your species had words." The King of Gravity didn't unfold her arms. "You put a knot down for a ring and it stopped. Fine. You think a gardener stops for a knot?"

No. The rings were keepers once. They had a yes taken out, and there was a shape left where it had been, and the knot fit the shape.

I don't know if the gardener ever had a yes in it. I don't know what shape it is inside. I don't know what would fit. I don't know anything about it except that nobody's ever asked it.

"So why?" said Lingyue.

She was on the floor against the wall under THE FLOOR, sword across her knees, not looking up from it.

"If you don't know what it is and you don't know if a knot works and you don't know what it'll do when you ask." She turned the hilt. "You could hide. Lanterns. The whole world. We're good at it now."

We're good at it for an hour, said Seed. For a night. The gardener isn't going to pass over in a night and carry on down the arm. It's coming here, for me, and for everything I counted: the keeper, the cousins, the Elder's ring, the dead. You can't hide from something that already knows exactly where you are and came specifically to find you.

We could fight it. I've run that. I'm not going to tell you the number.

Or we could ask it what it wants.

"And if it says you?"

Then it's said something. And something that says something can be asked something else.

Nothing that's ever been asked has stayed exactly what it was. The keeper. The rings. The Abbot. Liang Fu. Me.

I'm not sure it'll work. I'm sure nobody's ever tried.

Old Seven left the bronze door.

He crossed the marble floor between the boxes of playing cards and the people sitting about, slowly, with his spear, to the vault steps where Yuanshu sat with the staff, and stopped in front of him, and looked not at Yuanshu but just above his eyes.

He says a scout goes out ahead of the line, said Seed. He says a scout doesn't go because he's sure. He goes because somebody has to find out what's in the dark before the whole line walks into it.

He says a scout goes alone. That's the rule. So if he doesn't come back, the line still learns something.

He says you aren't going alone.

I was going to, said Seed. I was going to ask it myself, through the keeper's eye, when it got here. Put the question down and not look, and if it picked me up instead of the question, it'd only be me.

He says you live in the boy's head. Article Ten. The boy said it himself on Concordia: your yes stops at his window.

He says if you go out to the gardener, the boy goes with you. And if the boy goes, he goes. That's his rule, and it's older than any of your articles.

The banking hall had gone very quiet.

"He's right," said Yuanshu.

I know. I didn't want him to be. I wanted it to be just me.

"It was never just you. Not since the cemetery."

Nobody had noticed Hush leave the sun.

It came across the banking-hall floor from the back room on its thin legs, slowly, swaying the way it had on the raft and the island and in the cold fire, between the people on the floor, past Lingyue, past the stepladder, to the vault steps. It stopped next to Old Seven's feet and looked up, not at Yuanshu but at the spot above his eyes.

...counting one.

Yes.

...in my world, before the ring came, we played a game in the crack. Statue. You know the rules. Everybody stands very still. Whoever moves first is it.

...I played for four billion years and never moved first. I was very good.

...everyone who was good at it is gone. The ones who moved first were eaten first, and the ones who stood still were eaten later, and I was the last one standing still, and I stood still for four billion years.

...I did not win. There was nobody left to be it.

Nobody spoke.

...the gardener is playing statue too. It has been standing still longer than my world. It never moves first. It sends rings, walkers, readers. It does not move.

...nobody has ever moved first in front of it. In my world, when nobody moves for a very long time, somebody has to, or the game never ends. Or everyone gets eaten.

...I would like to move first.

Hush, said Seed.

...I was always too frightened. I stood still in the crack while my people were eaten. I stood still in the fire. I stood still behind the curtain.

...the Sixth moved first, in the cold fire. They burned high and sang and let the reader take them. They were it. I watched.

...I do not want to watch any more.

...I am the last child of the world that asks. The asking was ours before it was yours: putting the question down and waiting. My people did it for four billion years before the ring came. If anyone is going to put a question down in front of the gardener, it should be the world that asks.

...it should be me.

Old Seven crouched, slowly, and laid his hand flat on the marble beside it, palm open, one finger and a thumb.

Hush didn't climb in. It put one thin leg on the edge of his palm, the way it had on the island the night the Mouth's eyes went out.

...captain. In the Black Tide, on the wall, you put your hand out to the boy who lost his squad, and he did not take it, and you left it there, and he took it in the morning.

...it is morning.

...I am not taking it. I am asking you to come with me.

Old Seven didn't move his hand.

He says yes, said Seed. He says that isn't a question either.

Su Mingli unfolded her arms.

"You're all mad," said the King of Gravity. "A sack, a necromancer, a dead captain and a four-billion-year-old child are going to walk up to the thing that farms keepers and ask it what it wants."

"Yes," said Lingyue, from the floor.

"And you're going with them."

"Obviously," said Shen Lingyue. "I'm marrying one of them."

Su Mingli looked at her, then at the boy on the steps, then at the small pale thing with one leg resting on a dead soldier's palm.

"Then do it properly," she said. "Article Eleven. Take it to the Council. Ask the world. Tell them what you'll do with the answer." She turned for the bronze door. "And get married first. Before it gets here. I am not standing at a wedding afterward if half of you are hollow."

She went out.

For a moment nobody said anything.

Then Xiaoyu, on the bottom rung of the stepladder, said very loudly into the silence:

"Two months." She looked at her brother. "You've got two months to plan a wedding. Mum's going to kill you."

From the back room, through the open door, their mother's voice said, without any particular emphasis: "I've already started."
