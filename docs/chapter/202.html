Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 202What It Costs to Hurry

czverse8 min read

The dead said yes in four minutes. It was the fastest the Lattice had ever answered anything.

Adjutant Xu sang the question from his camp chair by the clock, and it went round the rim of the island nine by nine, through fifty thousand of the dead of Blue Star standing shoulder to shoulder against the dark. The keeper can go faster. It will hurt the keeper and the island. If the ground comes apart under you, it may hurt you too. Something is going to reach the cold fire before us. You can say no.

The answers came back in Seed's flat summary, one unit at a time.

Sergeant Ma: a miner who hears the roof creaking doesn't stop for a vote. But he likes being asked. Yes.

Ma Lianhua: we came home once through a throat of fire. Faster is better.

The forty from the Watch: a pilot runs for harbor before the squall, not after.

The Spear Corps didn't answer. Old Seven says they don't need to. They've been marching faster since the third night anyway.

Fifty thousand two hundred and thirty-one. Yes.

Then he asked the keeper, and that took longer.

...I HAVE NEVER BEEN ASKED IF I WANT TO BE HURT.

...THINGS HAPPENED TO ME. I DID NOT KNOW THERE COULD BE SOMETHING THAT ASKED FIRST.

The thin gold thread running north from the Elder trembled along its whole length. Kwan, who had a sensor on it, swore softly at her screen.

...THE CHILD IS COLD.

...YES.

The island sped up at the next tick of Bai Lu's clock, and it was nothing like the boat at Port Abiri.

Ifeoma had asked the water ahead of her boat to get out of the way, and they had skimmed across a flat black sea at forty-one knots, smooth as a highway. This was the opposite. The keeper wasn't asking the dark for anything. It was hauling a piece of itself through it by main force, on the rope of its own attention, like a man dragging a handcart uphill.

He felt it through his feet first. The ground, which had been as steady as a kitchen floor for three nights, started to hum. Not a sound exactly. More like standing on a platform when the train is still two stations away.

Then the far lights moved.

For three nights they had hung perfectly still: fields, worlds, husks, like lights on a coast seen from a ship that isn't going anywhere. Now they slid backward. The nearest ones went first, gold and blue and green, drifting past on either side.

"It's working," said Kwan. She was kneeling among her instrument cases with three screens open and a headlamp on, although the ground was lit from underneath and she didn't need one. Nobody had told her. "Seed, how fast?"

Three times what we were. Thirty-six raft-days becomes twelve, and we've already done one. Eleven nights to the cold fire.

"And the pruning?"

Thirteen nights, if it holds its speed. We get there first. By two nights.

Kwan sat back on her heels.

"Two nights," she said, "to get two hundred and eighty-one million minds out of a dead Hunger."

And sixty-one soldiers, and the Sixth, and the cousins, and a corporal, and a child.

"Thank you, Seed, that's very helpful."

You're welcome. A pause. That was sarcasm. I noticed afterward.

On the fourth night the rim started to come away.

He was at the leading edge with Old Seven when it began: a hairline crack a meter in from the edge, so fine it could have been a line drawn in pencil. The Spear Corps standing on the far side of it looked down. Then, without a word from anyone, they stepped back over it in their rows, and the strip beyond broke off in long flat slabs that drifted away behind them, still lit from inside, shrinking like ice coming off a floe.

...I AM SORRY.

...I AM HOLDING ON AS HARD AS I CAN.

...IT IS VERY FAR FROM ME NOW. THE FURTHER IT GOES, THE THINNER MY GRIP.

About two hundred meters of rim a night, said Seed. After eleven nights, two kilometers across instead of four.

That's room for fifty thousand, standing close.

It isn't room to carry two hundred and eighty-one million home.

He'd known that already. He had known it since the night of the forty days, sitting on the Sector Nine battlement with his sister's capitals drying on the wall behind him. Two hundred and eighty-one million minds didn't stand on islands. They weren't bodies. They were what the Low Fire had kept and the Mouth had eaten, burning low inside two Hungers for ten million years until they went cold. You didn't carry that on a slab of pale ground. You carried it the way the keeper had carried its columns. In a field.

And the keeper's field was empty.

I've been turning it over every night, said Seed. If they want to come, the keeper can hold them. In columns, in its field. And ask them every morning what they want, the way it asks the ones who stayed.

Then the keeper's field won't be empty. It'll be the brightest field in the Shallows.

With something pruning its way along the arm toward it, one bright field at a time.

On the fifth night Lingyue found him at the leading edge after Bai Lu's clock had said bed, standing next to Old Seven and staring at nothing.

She didn't sit. She stood next to him with her shoulder against his arm, which on the island counted as a long conversation.

"You're thinking about the field," she said.

"Yes."

"If we bring them home, the field lights up and the gardener comes for the keeper."

"Yes."

"And if we don't, they stay in a cold Hunger with something pruning its way toward them, and the gardener comes for the keeper anyway, because it was coming anyway." She kept her eyes on the dark. "So it's not them safe or the keeper safe. It's everyone in danger together, or everyone in danger separately."

He turned his head.

"I've been thinking about it too," said Lingyue. "Since the forty days. I'm not stupid."

"I know you're not stupid."

"Then stop carrying it round like it's yours." She jerked her chin at the dark ahead. "When we get there, tell them. All two hundred and eighty-one million. The keeper, the field, the pruning, the lot. And ask the keeper again. It said yes to being hurt. Ask if it'll say yes to being bright."

She's right, said Seed, after a moment. I keep thinking of it as something we'd be doing to the keeper. It isn't a thing we do things to any more. It gets to choose whether it wants to be bright.

Lingyue hit him on the arm, not hard, and went back to the clock.

On the seventh night a message came from the cold fire.

It came faster now that they were nearer. Instead of crawling for a month and arriving as a blur, it ran back along the keeper's attention, which reached out ahead of the island toward the Mouth, and it reached them in three hours. Seed read it aloud by the clock to everyone awake, which was everyone. Nobody slept well on the island any more.

Day 440.

We can see you better. You are a small gold light, getting bigger.

The sergeant has stood the men up at the edge of the quiet part, facing your light. He says that is the right way to face.

Something else.

Something is looking at the cold fire. From far off, from the other side to you. It is very large and very slow. It is not like the voice behind the curtain. That voice was kind. This is not kind or unkind. It is like weather.

It is reading the fire from the edge inward. The cousins at the edge can feel it. They say it is like being looked at by the sky.

The Sixth say: it is not looking at us. It is looking at the fire, to see if there is anything in it worth having. When it finds us, it will decide.

I still have the queen of hearts.

Tell the captain. — H.

Nobody spoke.

At the leading edge Old Seven didn't turn round. But the gray flame in his chest, which had been banked to a single dull red point for seven nights, came up bright and steady, like a soldier who has just heard the fighting started without him.

It's already there, said Seed. It isn't passing the Mouth in six nights. It's reading the Mouth now.

I got its speed wrong again. I keep assuming it's not in a hurry, because it never was before. Nothing ever made it hurry.

We did.

"How long until it finishes reading?"

I don't know. Hush says slowly, from the edge in. The Mouth is huge, two Hungers fused, bigger than any field. And the cousins have had ten million years to learn how to be dim. If they're dim enough, it might take a while to find them.

If it takes four nights, we arrive while it's still reading. If it takes two, we arrive after it's decided.

He looked ahead into the dark, where there was nothing to see, because the Mouth was cold and gave off no light at all.

"Keeper."

...I KNOW.

"Can you go faster?"

...YES. IT WILL HURT MORE. THE ISLAND WILL BE SMALLER.

...DO NOT ASK THE DEAD AGAIN. THEY ALREADY SAID FASTER IS BETTER.

...DO NOT ASK ME AGAIN EITHER. I ALREADY SAID.

...THE CHILD IS COLD.

The ground hummed harder under his feet. The far lights streamed past. Behind them the rim kept breaking off in long pale slabs, glowing, falling away into the black one after another, a trail of lit pieces leading back toward home.

Four nights, said Seed. That's the best I can do. It's the best it can do.

Dabao, who had been listening from beside his shield, said, "Four nights is fine. Four nights is basically tomorrow," in the voice of someone who had decided to believe that, and went back to polishing a shield that was already clean.
