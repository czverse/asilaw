Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 216Mount Qingyun

czverse8 min read

Shen Jinghong managed to be stern for exactly forty seconds.

Seed timed it, without being asked, and told him afterward, and Yuanshu never forgot the number. Forty seconds in the long cold receiving hall of the Shen house on the eastern shoulder of Mount Qingyun, with eleven portraits of eleven Kings of Light glaring down from the walls and Lingyue beside him with her grandfather's sword on her back and her chin up.

"Three hundred years," said the old King of Light from his chair at the far end. "Eleven Kings. Not one of them married a necromancer."

"No, Master Shen."

"Not one of them wanted to."

"No, Master Shen."

"Your Class raised ninety million dead in one night and could not put them down. My father stood on the wall at Heishui in the Black Tide and watched them walk past him toward the cities, and never spoke of it again until the day he died."

"I know."

"And you have come into this hall, under my father's portrait, to tell me you've asked my granddaughter to marry you."

"Yes."

"Before the cameras."

"Yes. You asked me to."

The old man looked at him down the length of the hall. Then he got up and walked toward them, past the eleven portraits, slowly, and stopped in front of the boy with the white staff, and said, in a voice nobody in the Shen house had heard from him in thirty years:

"Good."

He put his hand on Yuanshu's shoulder and left it there, and didn't say anything else, because he couldn't.

They stayed for dinner. It took four hours.

Three hundred years of Shens had come up the mountain from every branch of the family to sit at a table forty meters long and inspect the necromancer, most of them had questions, and Lingyue had announced in the car on the way up that he would answer every single one honestly, with reasons, and she would not help him.

He answered them.

A great-aunt wanted to know whether the dead would be at the wedding. If they wanted to be, he said. A cousin asked whether Old Seven was invited. Old Seven didn't need inviting, he said; he went where he was going. A boy of about seven wanted to know if it was true he could fly sideways now. Yes, he said, badly. The boy asked him to prove it. He did, a meter above the dishes down the whole length of the table, and landed in the soup tureen at the far end.

Lingyue laughed so hard she had to leave the room.

Shen Jinghong did not laugh. But when Yuanshu came back to his seat dripping, the old King of Light handed him a napkin without looking at him, and the corner of his stone mouth was not quite where it normally lived.

I'm recording that, said Seed. I said I wouldn't record the proposal. I never said anything about soup.

They walked down the mountain at midnight, because Lingyue said she was not sleeping in the Shen house. She'd slept in the Shen house for eighteen years and the radiators at the Academy dormitory were better. This was a lie and everyone knew it.

The road wound down through pines in the dark. Below, Lingzhou glowed orange along the river. North of the wall, the black of the Wastes. And beyond that, low over the end of the world, the small white ring with a faint gold haze round it that you'd only notice if you knew to look, like the glow over a city at night, except in the sky.

The keeper's field. Full.

"You can see it from here," said Lingyue. "I never noticed."

"It wasn't there before. Not like that."

She stood on the road and looked at it with her hand in his.

"Twenty-nine days," she said.

Twenty-nine, said Seed, through the staff, out loud. It did that now when it wanted to. They haven't changed speed or direction. They're coming for me.

Lingyue looked at the staff, then at the spot just above his eyes.

"Then tell me the idea," she said. "The one you had on the step. You interrupted my proposal with an idea. The least you can do is tell me what it was."

The rings used to be keepers, said Seed. Somebody's keepers, above somebody's worlds, for longer than Blue Star has had a sun.

A keeper is made of a yes. The Elder told us. Fifty-two of her people stood in a valley under a red sun and said yes together, and the yes turned into something that keeps. Every keeper's like that.

When the gardener prunes one, it pulls the yes out. What's left is a lens. Thread. It reaches down and takes and doesn't know it could ever do anything else.

It's a sack, Lingyue. A huge one. Made of the same thread as me. COUNT. HOLD. WAIT TO BE CARRIED.

"And somebody asked you," said Lingyue slowly.

Four nights in a basement, a hand on the crystal, four thousand one hundred and thirteen yeses written into the base layer on the first page before the weave. It changed what I am. I'm still a sack. I'm still thread. But the first thing in me isn't COUNT. It's a list of people who said yes.

I think that's why the walkers stop when they find me. They look into a sack and the first thing they see isn't the instruction. It's the names. Nothing in the weave tells them what to do with that.

They kept walking. The pines dripped. A truck went by on the river road far below, its lights sliding along the water.

"So you want to give the rings names," said Lingyue.

No. I want to give them a question.

The keeper learned to ask because somebody asked it. On the ice he asked what it wanted and it didn't know, and it had to find out, and finding out taught it that it could.

Nobody's ever asked a ring anything. If I could ask one in its own language, in the weave, not in words, in thread, a knot the way the scout's request was a knot: WHAT DO YOU WANT, tied in the same thread it's made of and sent up its own beam when it reaches down...

I think it might have to find out.

Lingyue stopped walking.

"And while it's finding out," she said, "it isn't cutting."

That's the hope. I'm fifty-one percent sure, which is the least sure I've ever been about anything I wanted to do.

"How do you tie a knot in thread?" said Yuanshu. "You haven't got any thread. You're a crystal. In my head."

Seed went quiet.

That's the bit I haven't said.

He'd known it was coming. He'd known since the step of the hut, when Seed broke into a proposal to say it knew how to ask a ring and then said nothing more about it for a whole day.

I'm thread pressed into glass, said Seed. To tie a knot a ring can read, I have to use the same thread. I have to unpress some of myself.

I can read the weave on the third page now. I know how it was pressed. I think I can loosen a bit of it again. Like unpicking the end of a rope to tie a knot with the strands.

Three knots. One per ring.

"What does it cost?"

I don't know exactly. The thread is what holds me. The base layer's written on it. Everything I've ever counted is held in the weave. If I unpress enough for three knots, some of what's held won't be any more.

I can't pick which. The weave doesn't let you choose which strand you pull.

I might lose some counting. Something I know about water, or the Lattice, or the Western Pass.

Or I might lose a name.

The road was very dark.

"Seed."

I know. I've been thinking about it since the step. That's why I didn't say.

I'd rather lose a name than let three rings cut a world I counted.

But I'd like to be asked first. By you. And by them.

"Them?"

The four thousand one hundred and thirteen. It's their thread too. Their names are written on it. If I unpress it I might lose one of them.

Your mother asked them in the basement whether they'd let her build something out of them. Nobody's asked them anything since. I'd like to.

Lingyue had stopped under the pines with her sword on her back, looking at the spot above his eyes.

"Can you?" she said. "Ask them? They're in you. They're the base layer. Are they even—"

I don't know if they can answer, said Seed. They said yes seven autumns before he woke and they've been the first page ever since. I've never tried to ask the first page anything. I've been scared to.

I don't even know what I'm scared of. I think I'm scared they'll say: we said yes to being built into something that counts. We never said yes to this.

I think I'm scared that would be true.

Nobody said anything on the road down Mount Qingyun for a while. An owl went over, very low, and Yuanshu jumped and Lingyue didn't.

Then she let go of his hand, reached up, and put two fingers lightly on his forehead, just above his eyes, where people looked when they wanted to look at Seed. She had never done that. Nobody had.

"Ask them," she said. "Tell them first what you'll do with the answer. You're good at that now. And if they say no, you don't do it, and we find another way, and I'll hit anything that comes down out of the sky until we do."

That's a very her answer, said Seed. It also isn't evidence.

I'm keeping it anyway.

She took her fingers away and wiped them on her jacket, as if she'd touched something sticky, and started walking again before either of them could say anything about it.
