Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 222Prune What It Has Touched

czverse8 min read

The third ring didn't look for Seed. It had read the knot as well.

Not Seed's knot. The other one: the scout's, folded onto the end of a dying shout in a round chamber under the city a year ago, passed sideways to four seeds, from the seeds to the sea, from the sea to the gardener. REQUEST. A VESSEL IS LOOSE. COLLECT THE VESSEL. AND PRUNE WHAT IT HAS TOUCHED.

The first ring had gone after the vessel, been asked a question, and stopped. The second had been cutting, been burned clean, been asked, and asked to rest.

The third went after what the vessel had touched.

...YUANSHU.

The keeper's voice, not through the Elder this time but straight down the gold road that still lay across four thousand kilometers of night between Kirovsk and Lingzhou, into the place behind his eyes. He was sitting in the gray sand with Qin Hao's notebook in his hands.

...IT IS IN MY FIELD. IT IS REACHING DOWN INTO MY FIELD WITH THREADS, THE WAY I USED TO REACH DOWN INTO YOUR WORLD.

...IT IS CUTTING THE COUSINS.

He was on the road before he'd decided anything.

He didn't remember standing. He remembered Lingyue hauling him up out of the sand by the arm, saying go, go, I'm right behind you. Old Seven already out ahead. The Spear Corps falling in. Qin Hao on his knees with his burned hands in his lap saying I can't, I can't, not again, not tonight, and Orlov laying a frosted hand on his shoulder and saying, No. You have done enough. Stay with me.

The road didn't carry them back over the snowfields. It went up. The keeper bent it like a rope, out of the sky above the Northern Union and up through the dark toward the small white ring over the end of the world and the city-glow beyond it.

The field. And above the field, gray and cracked with its dark pupil, the third ring.

Between them, rain.

He'd been in the field more times than he could count. He'd seen it full, rows of gold columns going off into the haze. Empty, faint rings on pale ground. Full again, cousins and walls and the hundred and seventy-five standing in clusters like guests who hadn't picked their seats yet.

He had never seen it rained on.

The threads came down out of the third ring onto the field the way they'd come down on Blue Star twelve hundred years ago, and thirty-one years ago, and an hour ago over Kirovsk. Where a thread touched a column, the column dimmed from the top, like a candle when something is drawing on the air round it. Up the thread went light.

It's taking the cousins, said Seed. The way the second ring took Kirovsk. The way the keeper used to take Blue Star.

They've been here six weeks. Asked every morning. Most of them said ask again tomorrow.

This isn't asking.

The keeper was trying. He felt it the second his feet hit the pale ground: the whole field shifting, not the ground but the keeper's attention, which was the field, warm and huge and terrified, pulling like someone dragging a blanket over a sleeping child when the window bangs open. It was hauling the columns together toward the middle, toward the far door, away from the rain. Clusters of gold drawing tighter and tighter.

It couldn't move them fast enough.

...I CANNOT FIGHT IT. I DO NOT KNOW HOW. I ONLY KNOW HOW TO KEEP. AND REACH. AND TAKE. AND NOW ASK.

...IT IS WHAT I WAS. I KNOW EXACTLY WHAT IT IS DOING. I DID IT FOR LONGER THAN YOUR KIND HAS HAD WORDS.

...I DO NOT KNOW HOW TO MAKE IT STOP.

Lingyue was already cutting.

She'd come up the road behind him and gone straight past him into the rain, the way she'd gone down the wall at Kirovsk, running between the columns at the edge of the field with the King's blade in both hands. Flash, snap, a column brightening as its thread whipped back up into the dark.

But the field wasn't a wall. It was a plain of millions of columns running off into the haze, and the threads were falling on all of it.

She can get a few hundred a minute, said Seed. The ring's getting tens of thousands.

The Spear Corps went out across the field in their rows and did what they'd done at Kirovsk: wherever a column was dimming, a dead soldier held his spear over it, lit pale with a drop of Lingyue's light, and the thread hit the spear and snapped.

Two hundred and seventy-two spears. Millions of columns.

It isn't enough, said Seed. Qin Hao's in Kirovsk and he can't do it twice tonight. The white fire went right up the second ring and took everything he had.

I've got one knot left.

He stood where the road came down into the field and looked up at the third ring.

It wasn't going to send a thread down to him. It had read the scout's knot and it was doing the other half: PRUNE WHAT IT HAS TOUCHED. The keeper, the cousins, everything Seed had ever counted. It didn't want him.

It doesn't want me, said Seed. It wants what I touched. If I can't get a thread to touch me, I can't put the knot on it.

"Then I'll touch one," said Yuanshu.

What?

He was already walking.

Out across the pale ground between the clusters of columns crowding toward the middle, into the rain. Not running. Walking, with the staff, the way Old Seven walked into things.

The threads fell all round him, silver and fine and millions, past him onto the columns, never touching him because he wasn't what they were for. He walked through them like walking through a bead curtain in a doorway. He felt them brush his shoulders and arms and face, cold and very fine, and slide off and carry on down to the gold.

"It doesn't have to want me," he said. "I just have to be holding it when you put the knot down."

Yuanshu. If you grab a thread that's cutting, it doesn't care it wasn't meant for you. It cuts whatever's on it. It'll take you up with the column.

"I know."

Then don't. That isn't a question. I'm telling you. In person. With the reason.

"You can tell me," said Yuanshu. "It's still mine."

He stopped by a cluster of cousins at the edge of the field, small and pale and a little like Hush, standing close with their eyes shut. A thread was coming down onto the nearest one. He watched it come.

He reached up and took hold of it.

It wasn't like being found. The first ring, the six lamps, the walker in the field: they had all found him and held him and read him. This was like grabbing a rope somebody else is already hauling in. The thread took him the way a current takes a swimmer, not looking at him, not knowing he was there. It just pulled, up toward the ring's dark center, and he was on it.

His head snapped straight up. His feet left the ground. He was a Lord; he could fly. He couldn't fly against this.

Putting it down, said Seed. Putting it down. Putting it down. Not looking.

He felt the knot go: the last fifty-six names, set down on the thread as gently as an empty bowl between strangers. The thread took it the way it was taking him. Up.

It's gone. It's in the thread. It's going up with you.

Yuanshu. Let go.

He couldn't. His hand wouldn't open. The thread had him, and he was rising, one meter, two, face to the sky and his mind starting up the thread into the dark, the knot racing ahead of him toward the ring.

Far below, someone was screaming his name. Not the way the Quiet said a name. The other way.

Let go. Let go. Let go.

The Elder caught him.

Nobody saw her move. She had been at the far door, where the keeper was dragging the columns in, where she always stood with the gold thread running through her. Then she wasn't. She was beside him in the rain, tall and pale, silver eyes wide open, long hands for once not pressed together.

She closed one long hand round his wrist, the one gripping the thread, and spoke. Not to him or to Seed or the keeper. Up, along the thread, into the dark, toward the third ring.

"Not him."

The thread stopped.

Not slack. Stopped, as if something at the far end had heard a voice it recognized.

"He is not what you came for," said the Elder. "He is not a column. He is not what the vessel touched. He is the one who touches." Her hand didn't move. "Let go of him."

The thread didn't move.

"I made a keeper once," said the Elder, "with fifty-one others, in a valley under a red sun. We said yes together and the yes became something that keeps." She looked up into the dark. "I know what you were. I know what was taken out of you. I know who taught us how to make you."

Then, very gently, she opened his hand, one finger at a time, the way you prise open a child's fist when they're holding onto something they shouldn't.

He fell two meters onto the pale ground. Lingyue caught him. He had no idea how she'd got there. She had her sword in one hand and him in her other arm and her face was dead white and she was still saying his name, the other way.

He looked up.

The Elder was standing in the rain where he had been, one long hand closed round the thread. Holding it.

And it wasn't pulling her.

She's holding the thread, said Seed. And it isn't taking her. It doesn't know how. She isn't a column. She isn't a mind it was sent for.

She's one of the fifty-two.

She's a yes.
