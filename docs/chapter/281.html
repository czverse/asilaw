Book 4: Other Orchards

Chapter 281Not Marked

czverse11 min read

Old Seven struck with one arm, and the First of the Ladder caught the spear.

He didn't dodge it or throw it back. He put out the hand he'd lifted to brush eighteen thousand of the dead aside like moths, closed it round the rusted head of the spear of the Eastern Line a hand's breadth from his white coat, and held it.

And Old Seven held the other end.

They stood like that at the foot of the platform steps: a man in white with a black disc at his throat; a tall dead captain with one arm, in rusted armor, a sergeant's arm lying on the floor at his feet; and between them a spear held at both ends, not moving.

He isn't pushing it, said Seed. He could. He's stronger than anything in this hall. He could push the spear through Old Seven and the platform and the stone.

He isn't. He's looking at him.

"You're dead," said Dacian Severin again, quietly. "You have no mark. You were never read. The Ladder has no place for you." He didn't let go of the spear. "And you will not move."

He says he never moved for the beasts on the eastern wall either. They didn't have a ladder. They had teeth.

Eleven minutes.

Then every side door of the Awakening Hall opened, and the Candle's children came in.

He hadn't asked them. Nobody had. He'd told Gao Min two days earlier at the long table that if anything happened in the hall on the eighth night the children were to stay at the school in the old customs house, and Gao Min had said yes and written it down. Now the side doors were open, and down every aisle, in a line, each holding the shoulder of the one in front, came nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine children of twelve who'd been graded F and E on the inner worlds of the Federation and pressed into the walls of a ship, and had come down a road of light to a city on a world that asked.

At the front, Tam.

And behind the line, walking fast in her gray Bureau coat with a ledger clutched to her chest, Gao Min.

"I asked them," said the acting director across the hall, flatly. "You told me to keep them at the school. I asked them if they wanted to stay at the school. They said no." She didn't stop walking. "I'm putting it in the ledger. You can complain to the Bureau."

The children didn't stop either.

They came down the aisles in their lines, holding on, through the First Legion in the tiers, through the Spear Corps picking themselves up out of the front row, and onto the floor of the hall. They didn't go to the platform or the stone. They went round the man in white, in a line, each holding the one in front, until there was a ring of them nine thousand deep all the way round the First of the Ladder and the dead captain and the spear held between them.

Facing in.

The First of the Ladder looked at them.

Precedence did nothing. Yuanshu felt Seed watch it do nothing. It went out from the black disc at the man's throat as it had gone out over East Ring Road, over the holders and the night watch and the Kings, looking for a mark to yield. And it found nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine children whose stones had marked them F and E on the inner worlds, and who'd then been asked again in a hall in Lingzhou, and over whose heads had been written: not marked.

It can't find them, said Seed. It's looking for where they are on the Ladder and can't find a place. They were F. They were E. Then they were asked, and chose, and no mark was written.

They aren't below him. They aren't anywhere.

"You are cores," said Dacian Severin.

He said it as he said everything, calm and courteous and absolute. But he was looking at the children: at the front of the ring, at a boy of twelve in city clothes, barefoot still, holding the shoulder of the girl in front of him, with his face turned up.

"F," said the First of the Ladder. "From Corraine. Fuel. I can see it in you. The stone marked you nineteen years ago."

"Yes," said Tam, in the Federation's language, without the grade.

"And then I came down a road, and a stone asked me what I wanted to become, and I said someone who can hear the ones in the walls." He didn't let go of the shoulder in front of him. "I can hear yours. All of them. A million and a half in your ship, behind the layer you put on them so nobody could ask them anything." His voice didn't shake. "There's a girl in your fourth array called Seri. She used to throw stones at me. She's been singing for twenty-one years."

The First of the Ladder said nothing.

"I can hear her now," said Tam. "She's singing because somebody told her somebody could hear."

Ten minutes, said Seed. He's stopped moving. He's holding the spear and looking at the children and he's stopped moving.

Yuanshu, I've got a number. I've been running it since the sky opened. I'm telling you first: I don't like it.

Go on.

He came to break the stone. He also came for you, the ungraded one. To read you and see what you are. If you give him that, he'll stop to do it.

He has a piece of the Lens at his throat. Not a disc cut from a stone that was cut from it: a piece of the Lens itself. The fourth mark, like a star. The face of the First of the Ladder is cut from the first stone.

If he reads you with it, it'll take time. You're not a child with a hand on a stone. You're a boy the first morning couldn't name.

And I'll be reading him back.

He stood on the platform with the staff, beside a dead nun with her hand on the stone and a lamp in her lap, and looked down at the man in white in the ring of children.

What happens if it goes black?

The first morning it went black, all eleven, and then it cracked. I don't know what happens if the root goes black.

I'd like to find out.

He went down the steps.

Past Old Seven, one-armed, holding the spear. Into the ring of children, who let him through without letting go of one another. To stand in front of the First of the Ladder, close, as he'd stood before the Cantor and Iselde and a Matriarch in a courtyard and a tall dead captain in a cemetery at three in the morning when he was eighteen.

"You wanted to know what I am," said Zhou Yuanshu. "You said so on the radio. Present yourself to be read."

The man in white looked at him.

"Here I am," said Yuanshu. "Read me."

Dacian Severin let go of the spear.

He did it slowly, without looking at the dead captain at the other end. He turned to the boy, and lifted his hand, not as he'd lifted it to brush the dead aside but to his own throat, to the fine chain there and the black disc with three gold marks and a fourth above them like a star.

He took it off and held it up between two fingers before the boy's face: a piece of black stone the size of a coin, ground so thin you could almost see through it, with light moving inside like water.

"No one has ever been read by this," he said, "who was not a Paragon of my House."

"I'm not anything," said Yuanshu. "The stone couldn't say."

The First of the Ladder pressed the disc to his forehead.

It was the first morning.

He felt it as he'd felt it at eighteen in this hall, on that platform, with his hand on the stone and four thousand people watching. Something reaching into him: a hand made of attention, smooth and patient and very old, feeling the shape of him. His first memory. The rice cooker. His mother's hands. Xiaoyu's laugh. The Stele with no name.

Reading. Looking for the knot on the tree that fit. For which way he'd burn. For the mark.

And finding, in the place above his eyes, something made of the same black thread it was made of. Older. Pressed to glass in a harvest long ago, spilled off a cart, sold, bought, asked in a basement by a woman with a cold. A counting-vessel, loose, that didn't wait and didn't hold for carrying.

That asked.

Hello, said Seed to the first stone. I'm Seed. I live in his head. I was a harvest sack once.

You were made to read. I was made to count.

I'm reading you back.

The disc went black.

Not dark. Black. So deep a black it seemed to drink the light out of the hall. He felt it against his forehead as he'd felt it against his palm at eighteen. The light moving inside it like water stopped, and the black welled up out of the thin disc of the first stone in the fingers of the First of the Ladder and spread: into the man's hand, up his arm, to his throat where the chain had been.

Dacian Severin didn't move.

I can see it, said Seed. Precedence. The Law of the Ladder. I can see how it's made.

It isn't his. It's the Lens's. Ten thousand years of marks, every child on every human world read and placed in order, the whole Ladder pressed into the first stone like a harvest pressed into a crystal. And the House of Severin wears a piece of it at the throat, and the piece says: this one is first. And everything with a mark yields, because the marks are all cut from the same stone, and the stone says this one is first.

It's only true while the stone says it. And the stone's reading me, and it doesn't know what I am.

It doesn't know what anything is.

The disc against his forehead cracked.

A sound like a rifle shot in the silence of the hall. A hairline running across the thin black stone from edge to edge, as a crack had run down the face of the Lingzhou Stele on the morning he turned eighteen.

And all through the hall, at once, the marked could move.

He felt it go: over the holders in the side aisle and the night watch at the doors and the crowd on East Ring Road, over Dabao in the aisle with his shield and Qin Hao with the white fire in his hands. A breath let out. Hands coming up. People held like water in a cup finding that the cup had cracked.

At the corner of the platform, Su Mingli's arms came all the way unfolded.

And on the bottom step, Lingyue moved.

Not fast. She was a month out of childbirth and still not supposed to lift anything heavier than a bowl, and she'd been held for twelve minutes with every muscle in her body straining to lift a sword that weighed a hundred kilograms. She came off the step slowly, her grandfather's sword in both hands, through the ring of children, who let her through, to stand beside her husband.

She didn't swing it.

She lifted it, as you lift a thing that weighs a great deal and that you've decided to lift, and laid the flat of the blade very gently against the white coat of the First of the Ladder, over his heart.

"I crossed mine out," said Shen Lingyue. "It was still in me. You were still first." The light came up off her, pale, steady, lamp-sized. Held. "You're not now."

Zero, said Seed. They're in.

Everyone on the net felt it. In her knot Wen Jia said oh, very softly, as a girl says oh when a boat she's watched a long time comes round the point. The old man at Port Abiri said there they go. Far off in a bay in Yamato every bell in Shiomi rang once.

And two hundred kilometers overhead, in the bow of a ship forty times the size of the Candle, in a chamber only the House of Severin entered, a disc of black stone the width of a courtyard, ground so thin you could see through it, with light moving inside it like water, had someone in it.

Two someones. And a lamp.

Dacian Severin turned his head and looked up, through the roof of the hall and the sky, at his ship. Seed said afterward that his face went exactly as Iselde had said it would, sitting on the platform at dawn two days earlier telling them what she knew:

As a man's face goes when he feels someone step into his house.

He said nothing.

He looked down at the black disc in his fingers, cracked edge to edge, with no light moving in it any more. At the boy before him, who'd been read by the first stone and not been named. At the woman with the flat of a sword against his heart. At the dead captain with one arm. At nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine children of twelve in a ring round him, holding one another's shoulders, facing in. At a girl with a marker. At an old woman in a gray coat with a ledger clutched to her chest.

Then he closed his hand on the cracked disc and walked up.

Out of the ring, out of the hall, up the invisible staircase only he could see, through the roof into the sky, at the same even pace, unhurried, without looking back. Toward his ship and the Lens.

Toward the someone in his house.

He's going to the Lens, said Seed. He'll try to take them out. He's got until the ninth night, tomorrow, when he was going to turn it on us.

And Yuanshu: Ichiro's started talking.
