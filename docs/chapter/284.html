Book 4: Other Orchards

Chapter 284The Sergeant's Arm

czverse9 min read

Fat Tan had kept the arm.

Of course he had. He kept everything. He'd swept the walker-silk off the north wall two years ago and kept it in a box in the vault beside the queens of hearts, and Hush had made two rings from it, and Xiaoyu a mark from a third strand, and a fog-ringer had held that mark up before the face of the oldest thing anyone had ever made. On the eighth night, when the First of the Ladder walked up out of the hall into the sky and the dead were picking themselves up off the tiers and nine thousand children were letting go of one another's shoulders, Fat Tan had come down the aisle with a cloth, knelt at the foot of the platform steps, picked a sergeant's arm with one finger and a thumb up off the polished floor, and wrapped it.

He hadn't asked anyone. He said afterward he didn't think it was a thing you asked. It was a thing you picked up.

It was in a box now, in the vault, on the shelf beside the queens of hearts: a long flat box with a lid that Fat Tan had made himself in the courtyard from the wood of an old Ossuary bench, lined with the same blanket that had lined Xiaodeng's drawer her first month.

On the forty-third morning he brought it out and set it on the long table in the banking hall, and stood back.

Old Seven came away from the bronze door.

He came as he always came, slowly, with his spear, across the marble between the people to the long table. One-armed. He'd stood at the bronze door one-armed for forty-two days and said nothing about it, and nobody had asked, because, Seed said, it was the kind of thing you didn't ask a captain until he was ready, and he hadn't been.

He stopped at the table and looked down at the box. He didn't open it.

He says: thank you. Fat Tan. Thank you.

By the back-room door Fat Tan made a sound and went to make tea, and was gone a long time.

Yuanshu sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees.

"I can do it again," he said. "The graft. If you want an arm." He didn't look away from the tall dead captain at the table. "There are dead who'd give one. I asked on the net. Thirty-one of the First Legion said yes before I'd finished asking. A man in Grid Nine said he'd always wanted to be part of a captain. I don't know what that means."

He says he knows what it means. The man in Grid Nine was a cook.

"Do you want one?"

Old Seven stood at the long table and looked at the box a long time.

He says: not yet. A soldier with one arm is still a soldier. He was on the eastern wall in the Black Tide with men who had one arm, and one of them was the best spear on the line.

He'd like to be a soldier with one arm for a while, so he remembers whose the other one was.

He says: ask me again in a year.

They took the box to stone No. 7 that afternoon.

Old Seven asked them to. Not all of them: Yuanshu; Lingyue, with Xiaodeng in the sling; Fat Tan, who carried it, because he'd picked it up and he'd put it down; and Dabao's mother, who'd heard, as everyone heard everything in the old quarter, and came with a paper boat of dumplings off the cart, steaming, nineteen, because that was what you brought to stone No. 7 and she wasn't going to be the one to break the custom.

They climbed the long green slope of the War Memorial Cemetery from the river in the spring afternoon, between the rows of small gray stones, past the cypresses, to the eastern field.

The Spear Corps stood round stone No. 7, facing out. And on the grass between them and the stone, in a ring, at ease, facing in: sixty shapes of gray ash.

The short one at the front stood up.

Square in the shoulder, with the shape of a pick across its knees, which it laid down on the grass as it rose. It looked at Old Seven coming up the slope one-armed, and then at Fat Tan behind him carrying a long flat box made from the wood of an old bench.

Huo says: sir. I heard, said Seed. The whole net heard. Eighteen thousand of the First Legion watched it happen, and the cook in Grid Nine's been telling everyone. The First of the Ladder took my arm.

Old Seven says: yes.

Huo Changsheng, sergeant of the Third Battalion, ash, looked at his captain a long moment.

Huo says: did you hit him with it first?

Old Seven didn't answer.

Huo says: sir.

Old Seven says: no. He caught the spear.

Huo says: then it was a good arm. It held on to the spear while he caught it, didn't it? Old Seven says yes. Huo says: then that's all right.

Fat Tan set the box on the grass before stone No. 7, beside the place where the turf had been cut a year and a half ago for an old adjutant who'd asked, twice, to lie next to it. Beside the paper boat of dumplings Dabao's mother had set down, steaming, in the spring sun.

Huo knelt beside it.

He didn't open it either; ash doesn't open things. He knelt on the grass beside the long flat box with the sergeant's arm inside it: his arm, which he'd given his captain in a courtyard among potted plants because his captain had lost his own on a stalk in the sky, and which had held a spear for three years, lost two fingers in a round chamber, and then held on to one more spear while the First of the Ladder caught it.

Huo says: I had those fingers forty years. Old Seven says: I know.

Huo says: you lost two in the round chamber, and now you've lost the rest. Old Seven says: yes.

The ash shape laid the shape of a hand flat on the lid of the box.

Huo says: good. That means you were using them.

They buried it there, beside stone No. 7 next to the adjutant, under turf Fat Tan cut with a spade he'd brought, because of course he'd brought a spade. The dumplings went on top, nineteen, in their paper boat, as they'd gone on top of the adjutant's.

Nobody spoke. Old Seven stood at the head of the little grave with his spear grounded in his one hand, facing in. The Spear Corps stood round the stone facing out. The sixty shapes of ash were all standing now, facing in, toward their captain and their sergeant and a small new mound of turf in the spring grass.

At the end Huo stepped back.

Huo says the Third Battalion's been at ease ten months, sir, and he's been thinking.

Old Seven says: you don't think, sergeant. You dig.

Huo says he's been thinking while he digs. The boy's going somewhere, isn't he? Up. With the fisherman in the stone. He can hear it on the net. Everyone can.

At the edge of the eastern field, with Lingyue beside him and Xiaodeng asleep in the sling, Yuanshu went very still.

Huo says: if the boy goes, you go. That's your rule. Older than any of his articles. And you've got one arm. So if you go, the Third Battalion goes.

Huo says: that's not a question, sir.

He didn't tell you, said Seed. I'm sorry. I should have. It came this morning through the Lens, through Ichiro, through the keeper's eye. I wanted to tell you at stone No. 7 with Old Seven there. I didn't know Huo would hear it on the net first. Nobody on the net can keep anything.

Tell me now.

The First of the Ladder asked. Not ordered: asked, the way he asked Halvarde. Badly. Like a word in a book.

He says the flagship will go home to Anthos, to the Assembly. Rowed. Two hundred and forty thousand chose to carry it, in turns, low, asked every fold.

The Lens is going with it. Ichiro and Kiyo are in it. Ichiro says he'd like to go. He'd like to talk to everyone in the Federation, and the Federation is very big, and he's only talked to the fleet so far.

He says the Assembly at Anthos has four thousand seats, one for every House and every world on the Ladder, and every one of them wears a face cut from the Lens.

He'd like the boy who asks to come.

He says he's telling you first: he doesn't know what the Assembly will do. He doesn't know if they'll listen, or if they'll let you leave. He says he's never been in a room where he didn't know what would happen.

He's asking.

He didn't answer.

He stood at the edge of the eastern field in the spring sun with the staff in his hand, where he'd stood on the first night three years ago with a training spear and no permit and asked a dead captain whether he wanted to get up, and looked at his wife.

Lingyue was looking at him. She'd been looking at him since Seed started talking: Xiaodeng asleep against her chest, her grandfather's sword on her back, a pale lamp held in her free hand, her jaw set the way it set before a fight.

"You're going to sit on a step for six hours," she said.

"Yes."

"And then you're going to ask me."

"Yes."

"And I'm going to say obviously."

He said nothing.

"She's asked every morning by a sack," said Shen Lingyue, and put her free hand on the baby's back. "She can be asked on a ship." She looked at the small new mound of turf beside stone No. 7 with nineteen dumplings on top, at a one-armed dead captain, at sixty shapes of ash standing in the grass facing in. "And I'm not sitting in a hall with my hand on your chest this time. I'm coming. Up. With the sword."

"Bai Lu—"

"Bai Lu can come too. She'll want to. She'll want to tell a Paragon he's an idiot." She thumped his arm, not hard. "Go and sit on your step. I'll wait."

He sat on the step of the groundskeeper's hut at the edge of the War Memorial Cemetery until dusk.

Not six hours. Four. Old Seven stood one-armed at the corner of the hut, facing out. The Third Battalion stood in the grass round stone No. 7, sixty shapes of ash facing in, at ease, and didn't sit down again. They were waiting, Seed said. Huo had said: when the boy decides, we'll be standing.

And in the north, low over the end of the world, as the light went, beside the keeper's small white ring and the Elder's vast gray one and the small gold point at the end of a branch, among sixty-one burning cathedrals that had opened their walls and one that would go home rowed: a lamp. Pale. Steady. The color of whale oil in fog.

It's getting dark, said Seed. I'd like to ask you something. I'm telling you first; you don't have to answer.

What do you want?

He sat on the step.

On the ninth night on the north wall, when a fog-ringer asked eight billion people at once, he hadn't been able to answer. He'd stood in his old place and not known, for the first time since he was eighteen.

He knew now.

I want to go and ask them, he thought. All of them. Four thousand seats. Every face cut from the Lens. Every world on the Ladder. I want to stand in the Assembly at Anthos and tell them first, and then ask.

And I want to come home.

That's two wants. That's allowed.

He stood up and went down the slope to the eastern field, where his wife stood by stone No. 7 with a baby and a sword and a lamp, among sixty shapes of ash and a one-armed captain and a paper boat of dumplings on a new grave.

He asked her.

She said obviously.
