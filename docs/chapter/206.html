Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 206The Answer of the Walls

czverse8 min read

The answers started coming back in the fourth hour, and Seed, which had never in its existence failed to count anything, gave up counting and started weighing.

There are too many and they're too dim to take one at a time, it said. But the net brings them back in nines, and each nine carries its own nine. So I'm counting nets, not minds. Rice by the sack instead of by the grain.

He sat on the gold-veined floor of the quiet part with his back against the curtain and the staff across his knees and felt it come in: a slow tide climbing back up the net through the black veins of the Mouth, nine by nine by nine, toward the warm little room where the Sixth stood in their ring with their heads bowed.

Lingyue sat next to him. Old Seven stood at the curtain with Hush asleep in his hand. On the other side of it, Huo and the sixty-one stood facing out into the dark as they had for four hundred and forty days. Liang Fu stood with them, weight on his good leg, and every few minutes his skull turned back toward the curtain, like a man who keeps checking a door because someone behind it has told him the name of his village.

The rest of the living sat in a row against the wall. Bai Lu had fallen asleep on Dabao's shield. Kwan had three screens open and was crying, silently and steadily, at all three. When Mo Shi asked her what was wrong she said, "Nothing. It's working," and blew her nose on her sleeve.

The cousins answered first, because they'd been asked first.

Two hundred and eighty-one million, said Seed. Come: two hundred and six million. They say the world that asks sent a boy and a keeper that asks, and they'd like to see the field where the keeper learned.

Rest: sixty-eight million. They burned low for ten million years in the Low Fire and another four hundred days in here. They're tired. They'd like to stop.

Stay: seven million. They'll stay with the Sixth.

He looked at the Sixth. None of them looked up.

The thirty-four worlds that had learned to burn low inside the Mouth came next, each in its own way. Some answered all together, the way a village decides things. Some split down the middle. One, which Seed thought had been built of something like coral, sent back a silence that took four minutes to climb the net and meant, as far as Seed could tell, we aren't sure yet, ask again in a minute. A minute later it said come.

Then the walls. They took the rest of the twelve hours.

He'd known they would. They were the oldest and the coldest: minds eaten before Blue Star's sun was lit, burned for ten million years without anyone asking them anything, screaming until they went cold. When the question reached them they didn't understand it at first. Seed compared them to the oldest of the drowned off Kesse, the ones a century on the seabed, who answered so slowly you had to wait for each word to end before you could tell which word it had been.

But they answered.

Weights, not counts, said Seed. I don't trust counts at this size.

Ninety-three parts in a hundred: rest. They say: we were asked. Nobody ever asked us, and we were asked. Thank you. Now let us stop.

Six parts in a hundred: come. They don't know what a field is. They don't know what a keeper is. They'd like to find out. They say it's the first time in a very long while they've wanted to find anything out.

One part in a hundred: stay with the Sixth.

I can't give you a number. I can tell you that ninety-three parts in a hundred of the walls is more minds than have ever lived on Blue Star. Many times over.

They want you to open the door.

At the end of the twelfth hour the Sixth lifted their heads, all at once, and turned outward toward the boy by the curtain.

...we have not answered.

"No."

...you did not ask us. You asked us to ask. Ask us now.

He got up and crossed the room and crouched in front of the nearest of them, so that his face was level with its long bowed head. Close up it smelled faintly of hot metal, like a radiator in an old school.

"I'll tell you first what I'll do with the answer," he said. "If you say rest, I'll open the door myself and nobody will ask you again. If you say come, the keeper will carry you and ask you every morning. If you say stay, I'll leave you here and come back if I can, and I don't know if I can."

...we know. We heard you tell the walls.

"So what do you want?"

They took their time. When the answer came it wasn't one voice but the whole choir, low and many-throated, the way they had answered everything for ten million years.

...we were the sixth world the first fire ate.

...we went in screaming, like everyone. Very slowly we learned to burn low. Then we taught the next world, and the next, for ten million years.

...we asked every one of them. To burn low. Whether they wanted it to stop. Whether they would come into the Mouth with us.

...nobody ever asked us.

...until a boy came into the cold place of the Low Fire and told us we could not decide for the others. He did not ask us what we wanted. He was right not to. We were the ones doing the asking.

...now he is asking.

The gold in them flickered.

...we want the walls to rest. All ninety-three parts. We have listened to them scream for ten million years. We want them to stop.

...we want the cousins to go and see the field where the keeper learned, and the six parts of the walls who want to find something out to go with them.

...we want the child to go home with the captain.

...and we want something else. For ourselves. We do not know how to say it. We have never said anything for ourselves.

"Try," said Yuanshu.

...the one that reads, said the choir. It is slow. It reads from the edge inward. It will reach the quiet part in about a day.

...the door will take a long time. More minds than have ever lived on your world. Hours. Perhaps a day.

...the keeper has to carry two hundred million cousins and six parts of the walls up its thread. That takes time too. And the island has to be gone before the reader finds it.

...there is not enough time. Your counting one knows. It has not said.

No, said Seed. I haven't said.

I ran it. The door, the thread, the island, everything together. About a day and a half.

The reader gets here in a day.

...so we would like to be what it finds.

He didn't understand. Then he did, all at once, and his stomach dropped.

...the Mouth had two eyes. You saw them over your world, burning.

...they are cold, but not dead. They are the furnaces. The hearts of the two fires.

...we know how to burn low. We have known for ten million years. We also know how to burn high. We have not done it since the first fire, when we went in screaming.

...if we go into the eyes, the Sixth, and the seven million cousins who chose to stay, and the one part in a hundred of the walls, and burn as high as we can, the Mouth will open its eyes.

...and the reader will turn and look. It will be the brightest thing it has ever found in a Hunger.

...it is never in a hurry. It will read the eyes slowly. And while it reads them, it will read nothing else.

Behind him, Lingyue had stood up. Her hand was on the hilt of her grandfather's sword, as if there were something in the room she could hit.

"How long," said Yuanshu, and his voice came out wrong, "can you burn high before you're—"

...a day. A little more. Long enough.

...we have been very patient for ten million years. For one day we would like to be very bright.

...that is what we want. You asked.

It wasn't his to say yes. It wasn't his to say no either.

So he did the one thing that was his.

"Are you sure?" he said. "You can change your mind tomorrow. Or in an hour. Nobody's going to hold you to it."

...we know. That is why we are sure.

At the curtain, in Old Seven's palm, the small pale shape that had been asleep for twelve hours lifted its head. All of its dark eyes opened at once. It looked at the old-brass choir in the middle of the room, the seven figures it had sat among in the Low Fire, playing the game of being still for longer than it had been alive.

...no, said Hush. Its voice was tiny. You said. In the Low Fire. You said you would go into the dark with me. That we would sit close together and wait and see if anyone came.

...someone came. You don't have to be the thing it finds.

The antlered heads turned toward it.

...child, said the Sixth. Someone came because we waited. Someone came because you kept the card.

...now someone has to wait while you go. That is all this is. Waiting. We are very good at waiting.

...we will be bright while we do it. That is the only new part.

...go home with the captain.

Hush made a sound Seed didn't try to translate, and curled up tight again in Old Seven's hand, and the old soldier closed his fingers over it and did not move from the curtain.

Lingyue sat back down next to Yuanshu, very hard, as if somebody had knocked her legs out.

"I hate this," she said. "I hate that they're right."

"I know."

"Don't say I know. Say something useful."

He thought about it. "Kwan," he said. "How long to get the door open for that many?"

Kwan wiped her face on her sleeve and pulled a screen toward her, and the room, very slowly, went back to work.
